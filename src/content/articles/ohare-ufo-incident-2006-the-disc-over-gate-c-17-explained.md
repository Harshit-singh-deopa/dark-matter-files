# **Quick Facts**

# **The O'Hare UFO Incident**

On the afternoon of **November 7, 2006**, something strange was reportedly seen above one of America's busiest airports.

Several employees working for United Airlines at **Chicago O'Hare International Airport** said they noticed a dark, metallic-looking object hovering above the area around **Gate C-17** in Concourse C.

The reports came from people working in different parts of the airport.

Among those described in the later investigation were:

* Ramp mechanics
* Aviation mechanics
* Supervisors
* A Boeing 737 captain
* A Boeing 737 first officer
* Other United Airlines personnel
* At least one civilian observer outside the airport

The object was generally described as **disc-shaped, gray or metallic, silent and apparently stationary**.

According to several witnesses, it remained in the area for several minutes.

Then came the strangest part.

The object reportedly accelerated upward and disappeared into the overcast.

Several witnesses subsequently reported seeing a **roughly circular hole in the cloud layer** where the object had been.

That detail eventually became one of the central arguments in the debate over what really happened.

The Federal Aviation Administration ultimately suggested that the witnesses had seen a **weather phenomenon**, while independent investigators argued that this explanation did not adequately account for the reported object itself.

# **What Makes the O'Hare Case Important?**

Most UFO reports involve one person seeing something unusual for a few seconds.

The O'Hare case is different.

The reported witnesses were largely **aviation professionals working at an international airport**.

They were surrounded by airplanes every day and were accustomed to identifying aircraft.

The National Aviation Reporting Center on Anomalous Phenomena, or NARCAP, eventually investigated the incident in considerable detail.

Its 2007 technical report contains witness accounts, airport diagrams, weather information, radar analysis, FAA communications, a review of possible hoaxes and a discussion of alternative explanations.

However, there is an equally important limitation:

**There is no authenticated photograph or video of the object itself.**

The strongest evidence consists of testimony and contemporary records rather than an image of the craft.

# **Why Was the Cloud Hole So Important?**

If the witnesses were correct, the cloud opening was potentially a physical effect produced by the departing object.

But there is another explanation.

A phenomenon called a **fallstreak hole**, also known as a **hole-punch cloud** or **cavum**, is real.

NOAA explains that these circular or elliptical gaps can form in clouds containing supercooled water droplets.

When an aircraft passes through such a cloud, changes around the aircraft can cause the droplets to freeze.

Ice crystals then grow and fall out of the cloud, creating a hole.

NASA describes the same phenomenon and notes that aircraft can trigger these openings in suitable cloud conditions.

So the existence of a circular hole in the sky is **not evidence by itself of a UFO**.

# **Why NARCAP Rejected the Simple Weather Explanation**

NARCAP's investigators believed the reported sequence was important.

**Cloud disturbance → freezing droplets → falling ice → opening in cloud.**

If the witnesses correctly described the sequence, then the ordinary weather phenomenon would explain the cloud hole but not necessarily the preceding disc.

This is the strongest argument against treating the FAA explanation as a complete solution.

And aircraft can trigger them.

NOAA and NASA have documented this phenomenon independently of UFO reports.

Therefore, the cloud opening cannot automatically be treated as physical evidence of an unknown craft.

The real question is whether the witnesses accurately interpreted the object they saw before the hole appeared.

# **The Radar Evidence Is More Complicated Than It Sounds**

Another popular claim is:

**“The UFO wasn't on radar.”**

That is broadly true in the sense that no radar return was confidently correlated with the reported object.

But NARCAP's detailed analysis found numerous ordinary primary radar returns in the surrounding area.

The researchers also identified technical reasons why a nearby target might not appear clearly.

In addition, the NEXRAD weather data contained some echoes that were potentially interesting but were too ambiguous to establish a UFO.

So the accurate statement is:

**No publicly available radar data conclusively identify the reported object.**

That is different from saying:

**“Radar proved the object was invisible.”**

# **The O'Hare Tower Blind Spot**

One particularly interesting detail from the NARCAP investigation concerns the airport control tower.

Investigators examined the geometry of the tower and calculated that the reported object could have been located in an area that was difficult for tower personnel to see.

This potentially explains why controllers did not visually observe it even though it was reportedly near the airport terminal.

In other words:

**The lack of a visual sighting from the tower does not automatically contradict the witnesses.**

# **What Happened to the Witnesses Afterwards?**

The incident appears to have had different effects on different witnesses.

Some were willing to speak.

Others refused to participate.

Some reportedly feared being identified.

One witness reportedly became emotionally affected by the event.

NARCAP documented concerns about workplace ridicule and the general stigma associated with reporting UFO sightings.

At least one witness later expressed concern that information identifying him could create problems at work.

However, claims of widespread official intimidation remain unproven.

NUFORC's report is useful as an early record, but it is still an independent UFO reporting organization rather than a government agency.

# **What Is the Strongest Conventional Explanation?**

The strongest conventional explanation is probably some combination of:

**fallstreak/hole-punch cloud + perceptual misinterpretation + an unrelated or ordinary aerial object.**

The fallstreak mechanism is scientifically established.

The difficult part is explaining why multiple witnesses independently described a solid object hovering for several minutes.

That is why the case remains controversial.

# **What Is the Strongest Anomalous Explanation?**

The strongest anomalous interpretation is:

**a genuine physical object of unknown origin was hovering over the airport and then rapidly departed through the cloud layer.**

This interpretation fits the witness testimony.

It does not, however, tell us what the object actually was.

Jumping from:

**“unknown object”**

to

**“alien spacecraft”**

requires an additional assumption that the evidence does not establish.

# **Could the Object Have Been Human-Made?**

Absolutely.

A classified aircraft, surveillance platform or experimental technology cannot be completely ruled out from the public evidence.

In fact, this possibility should be considered before extraterrestrial explanations.

The problem is that there is no publicly released evidence identifying the object as such.

# **Could the Witnesses Have Been Wrong?**

Yes.

This does not mean they were lying.

A person can honestly report an observation and still misidentify its cause.

Even professional pilots can misjudge:

* Distance
* Altitude
* Size
* Speed
* Relative motion

especially when looking at an object against a featureless cloud background.

That is one of the biggest reasons scientists distinguish between **witness credibility** and **interpretation accuracy**.

# **Could All the Witnesses Have Seen the Same Wrong Thing?**

Possibly.

Multiple witnesses do not automatically guarantee correct identification.

However, the witnesses were positioned in different areas and described the object independently.

That makes a simple coordinated hoax less convincing.

The real question is whether they independently misinterpreted the same atmospheric or ordinary phenomenon.

There is no definitive answer.

# **The Biggest Problems With the UFO Explanation**

If the object was an extraordinary craft, several questions remain unanswered.

### **Where is the radar track?**

No confirmed track has been released.

### **Where is the photograph?**

No authenticated photograph exists.

### **Where is the video?**

No authenticated video exists.

### **Where is the physical evidence?**

None is publicly known.

### **Why did the object appear only to some observers?**

The tower did not see it.

### **Why are the witness estimates so different?**

Size and altitude estimates vary dramatically.

These are serious weaknesses.

# **The Biggest Problems With the Weather Explanation**

The conventional explanation also has weaknesses.

### **Why did multiple people describe a solid object?**

A cloud hole alone does not necessarily explain this.

### **Why did witnesses report hovering before the hole?**

That sequence is difficult for a simple fallstreak explanation.

### **Why was the object described as metallic?**

Several observers independently used similar descriptions.

### **Why did witnesses report rapid movement?**

A stationary cloud phenomenon should not behave like a departing craft.

### **Why did the FAA's records initially appear inconsistent with public statements?**

The later FOIA material demonstrated that United personnel had contacted the tower about the event.

# **The Final Verdict**

The **2006 O'Hare UFO incident is a genuine historical UFO/UAP report**, not an internet legend created years later.

The strongest evidence is not a photograph.

It is the combination of:

**multiple witnesses + contemporary communications + FAA records + independent investigation.**

The witnesses included aviation professionals who described a strange disc hovering near an active airport terminal.

The FAA eventually acknowledged that its tower had received reports, although it did not identify a corresponding radar target.

The agency attributed the event to a weather phenomenon.

The existence of **hole-punch clouds is scientifically established**, making the meteorological explanation credible enough that it cannot simply be dismissed.

At the same time, the weather explanation does not automatically explain every part of the witness testimony — particularly the reported solid object hovering before the cloud opening appeared.

The most scientifically responsible conclusion is therefore:

> **Something unusual was genuinely reported at O'Hare on November 7, 2006, and the surviving records confirm that the report reached airport authorities. What the witnesses actually saw remains unresolved.**

The case does **not prove aliens**.

It also cannot honestly be described as completely debunked merely because the FAA suggested a weather phenomenon.

That middle ground is what makes the O'Hare case interesting.
