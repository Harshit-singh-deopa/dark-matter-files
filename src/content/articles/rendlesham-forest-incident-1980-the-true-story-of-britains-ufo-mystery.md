# **Where the Incident Happened**

Rendlesham Forest is located in **Suffolk, eastern England**, near the former RAF bases **RAF Woodbridge** and **RAF Bentwaters**.

During the Cold War, these installations were operated by the United States Air Force.

The location was significant because the personnel involved were not ordinary civilian UFO enthusiasts. They were military personnel working around an active air base.

The National Archives describes the event as one of Britain's best-known UFO incidents and confirms that USAF personnel investigated the forest on two separate nights. ([The National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The forest was immediately outside the eastern perimeter of RAF Woodbridge.

This geography becomes important later because **Orford Ness lighthouse** was visible from parts of the forest and became one of the principal conventional explanations for some of the reported lights.

The chronology of Rendlesham is slightly complicated because some later documents contain incorrect dates.

The famous Halt memorandum gives the first event as occurring on **December 27**, but contemporary police records and the wider case chronology place the initial incident in the early hours of **December 26, 1980**.

The guards initially considered the possibility that an aircraft might have crashed.

That was a reasonable concern given their location and responsibilities.

Permission was obtained for personnel to leave the base and investigate.

The incident did not end after the first night.

On the night of **December 27–28**, Lieutenant Colonel **Charles I. Halt** became personally involved.

Halt was the deputy base commander.

A group of military personnel returned to the forest to investigate the earlier reports and the supposed landing area.

That decision would eventually make Rendlesham very different from most historical UFO cases.
