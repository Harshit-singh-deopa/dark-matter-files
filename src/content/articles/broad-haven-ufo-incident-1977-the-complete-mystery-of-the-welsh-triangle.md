**Date:** 4 February 1977
**Location:** Broad Haven, Pembrokeshire, Wales, United Kingdom
**Main witnesses:** Approximately 14–15 schoolchildren
**Reported object:** Silver, cigar-shaped or dome-topped object
**Reported occupant:** Silver-suited humanoid figure
**Related phenomenon:** The wider 1977 "Dyfed Triangle" or "Welsh Triangle" UFO wave
**Status:** Unexplained and disputed
**Physical evidence:** None conclusively established
**Photographic evidence:** No authenticated photograph of the alleged craft is known
**Official records:** Yes — surviving Ministry of Defence UFO files exist

## **The Short Version**

On 4 February 1977, a group of children at Broad Haven Primary School in Pembrokeshire, Wales, reported seeing a strange metallic object in a field near their school.

The object was described as silver or silvery-green, elongated or cigar-shaped, with a raised dome and a coloured light. Several children also claimed they saw a tall figure dressed in a silver or metallic-looking suit.

The story quickly attracted local and national attention. The school's headmaster, Ralph Llewellyn, questioned the children and later collected drawings and written descriptions. The incident subsequently became associated with a much larger series of UFO reports across west Wales, eventually known as the **Dyfed Triangle** or **Broad Haven Triangle**.

What makes the case particularly interesting is that it eventually reached the British Ministry of Defence. Surviving government records show that officials were sufficiently interested in the wider Welsh reports to involve the RAF Police in a discreet inquiry.

However, the evidence does **not** prove that an extraterrestrial spacecraft landed near the school.

There is no confirmed photograph of the craft, no recovered material, no radar recording conclusively linked to the sighting and no independently verified physical trace.

At the same time, the case has never received a universally accepted explanation. Some witnesses continued to maintain that they saw something unusual decades later.

That combination — multiple witnesses, government records, disputed drawings and the absence of physical evidence — is what has kept Broad Haven alive as one of Britain's most famous UFO mysteries.

# **1. Where Did the Broad Haven UFO Incident Happen?**

Broad Haven is a coastal village in **Pembrokeshire, southwest Wales**.

In 1977, the area was part of the larger county of **Dyfed**, which existed between 1974 and 1996. The surrounding region was rural, relatively isolated and close to several military facilities.

The village itself lies on the Pembrokeshire coast, overlooking St Brides Bay.

The location became important because reports did not stop at the school. During the months that followed, residents in the surrounding area reported strange lights, unusual objects and alleged humanoid figures.

This eventually produced the name **"Dyfed Triangle"**, although there was never a clearly defined official triangle or single geographic boundary.

Historian David Clarke describes the 1977 events as a short-lived but intense UFO "flap" concentrated around the Pembrokeshire coast.

# **2. What Happened on 4 February 1977?**

The central incident occurred at **Broad Haven Primary School** during the children's break.

Accounts vary over the exact time and duration, which is an important point when evaluating the case.

The surviving accounts generally place the event around the children's lunchtime or early afternoon.

A group of pupils noticed something unusual in a field beyond or near the school grounds.

The object was described in broadly similar terms:

* Metallic or silver in appearance
* Elongated, cigar-like or saucer-like
* A raised dome on the upper portion
* A coloured or flashing light associated with the dome
* Partly obscured by trees, bushes or vegetation
* Apparently resting on or very close to the ground
* Some children reported a humming sound
* Some witnesses described a humanoid figure nearby

The exact description was not identical between every witness.

Some children compared the object to a cigar.

Others described something more like a saucer or two saucers joined together.

Several accounts mentioned a dome.

The reported colour of the light also varies between accounts, with descriptions including red, orange, yellow and green.

These variations matter because later retellings sometimes make the children's descriptions appear more uniform than the surviving material actually shows.

# **3. How Many Children Saw It?**

This is one of the first details that becomes confusing when researching Broad Haven.

Popular accounts frequently state that **14 children** saw the object.

Some archival reconstructions instead refer to **15 children**, while some databases give different totals.

The safest description is therefore:

> **Approximately 14–15 pupils were involved in the main school sighting.**

The children were mostly boys between roughly nine and eleven years old.

One of the boys who later became particularly well known was **David Davies**, who was approximately ten years old at the time.

Unlike many of the other children, Davies continued discussing the incident publicly as an adult.

Importantly, Davies has generally been careful not to claim that what he saw was definitely an alien spacecraft. In later interviews, he described it more cautiously as an unexplained or strange object.

# **4. What Did the Children Actually Say They Saw?**

The descriptions vary, but several recurring features appear throughout the historical accounts.

The object was generally described as having a **silver or metallic body**.

Some children described a shape similar to a cigar.

Others described a disc or saucer-like base with a dome above it.

A light was reportedly visible on or around the upper section.

One of the contemporary accounts reproduced by UFO researchers describes the object as silvery-green, with a yellow-orange to red light and a dome.

Another witness description compared the object to two saucers joined together.

The object was apparently located in a field rather than simply being a distant light in the sky.

This is significant.

Broad Haven was therefore not originally a simple "strange light in the sky" case.

The witnesses claimed to have seen a **structured object close to the ground**.

# **5. The Mysterious Silver-Suited Figure**

Perhaps the strangest part of the story is the alleged occupant.

Several children claimed to have seen a person-like figure close to the object.

The figure was described as:

* Tall
* Human-shaped
* Wearing silver or metallic clothing
* Sometimes described as having unusual or pointed ears
* Associated with the strange object

Some accounts say the figure appeared to emerge from or stand beside the craft.

The exact details differ between witnesses, however.

This is important because the humanoid claim became increasingly prominent as the wider Pembrokeshire UFO wave developed.

It was not necessarily an equally strong part of every child's original account.

# **6. The Headmaster's Investigation**

The school's headmaster in 1977 was **Ralph Llewellyn**.

Initially, the children's claims were treated with skepticism.

The idea that a group of primary-school children had seen a flying saucer naturally sounded extraordinary.

However, the children's insistence eventually led Llewellyn to investigate the matter more seriously.

He interviewed the pupils separately and collected written accounts and drawings.

This is where one of the most famous pieces of evidence in the Broad Haven case originated.

The resulting drawings appeared sufficiently similar that the case gained considerable publicity.

Llewellyn reportedly did not believe the children were capable of organizing a sophisticated hoax.

His position was more cautious than simply declaring that they had seen aliens.

He believed that the children genuinely believed they had witnessed something unusual.

# **7. The Famous Drawings — And an Important Correction**

For decades, the Broad Haven story has frequently been presented like this:

**The children were separated immediately after seeing the UFO and independently drew almost identical pictures.**

That version is incomplete.

Later research by historian **David Clarke**, based on the school's contemporary records and Ministry of Defence material, found an important chronological problem.

The children were reportedly questioned and asked to produce their drawings on **Monday, 7 February 1977**.

The original sighting had occurred on **Friday, 4 February**.

That means several days had passed, including a weekend.

During that period, the children could potentially have discussed the event with one another, their families or other people.

This does not prove that the drawings were fabricated.

It simply means they cannot be treated as a perfect experiment in independent eyewitness recall.

The drawings remain genuine historical evidence that the children produced similar representations.

But their evidential value is weaker than many popular versions of the story suggest.

# **8. Were the Drawings Really Similar?**

Yes — there is genuine historical documentation showing that drawings were produced and preserved.

Several depict a broadly similar metallic, elongated or saucer-like object.

However, they were **not identical**.

There are variations in:

* Shape
* Dome size
* Lights
* Surface details
* Proportions
* Orientation

Some drawings also included figures.

The similarities are interesting because they show that several children remembered broadly related visual features.

But the differences are equally important.

A careful historical assessment should therefore say:

**The drawings demonstrate a degree of commonality between the children's accounts, but they do not independently prove that an extraterrestrial craft was present.**

# **9. The First UFO Investigation**

The story soon came to the attention of **Randall Jones Pugh**, a local veterinary surgeon and UFO investigator associated with the British UFO Research Association (BUFORA).

Pugh became one of the major figures documenting the wider Welsh UFO wave.

He interviewed witnesses and investigated several reports.

He later worked with naturalist and author **F.W. Holiday** on *The Dyfed Enigma*, published in 1979.

Pugh's involvement helped transform what might otherwise have remained a local school story into a national UFO story.

Contemporary UFO publications also recorded the Broad Haven reports while the wider Welsh flap was still occurring.

# **10. The Story Reaches the Police**

The incident did not remain purely within UFO research circles.

The children's accounts and drawings were reportedly passed through local authorities and eventually reached the Ministry of Defence.

This is important because Britain maintained an official system for receiving and assessing UFO reports.

The UK National Archives confirms that surviving Ministry of Defence UFO records include reports, correspondence, parliamentary material and related investigations.

For 1977, the key surviving collection includes **DEFE 24/1206**, covering UFO reports and correspondence from that year.

# **11. What Did the British Ministry of Defence Think?**

This is one of the most fascinating parts of the case.

The Ministry of Defence did not publicly announce that aliens had visited Broad Haven.

In fact, the official position was considerably more mundane.

However, later-released records show that officials were paying attention to the broader Welsh UFO wave.

In June 1977, the head of the relevant Ministry of Defence branch, **S4(Air)**, contacted the RAF Police's Provost and Security Service and requested a **"discreet enquiry"** into the Welsh reports.

This was unusual.

The surviving correspondence indicates that the UFO desk had not previously used the RAF Police in this way for UFO investigations.

The fact that officials investigated does **not** mean that the government believed the sightings were extraterrestrial.

The Ministry of Defence's primary concern was defence and aviation security.

The National Archives explains that a British "UFO" record generally referred to something observed but not identified — not necessarily an alien spacecraft.

# **12. The RAF Police and the "Practical Joker" Theory**

One of the strongest skeptical explanations emerged from the official investigation.

RAF personnel reportedly considered the possibility that a **practical joker** was responsible for at least some of the reports.

A later Ministry of Defence briefing referred to the RAF Police's view that the Welsh UFO wave could have involved a practical joker.

Another important observation concerned the alleged silver-suited figures.

An RAF officer reportedly suggested that the descriptions resembled **protective suits used by workers at local oil facilities and refineries**.

This was a particularly interesting theory because the Milford Haven area had major oil-related industrial facilities.

Therefore, someone walking through the countryside wearing industrial protective clothing could potentially have been mistaken for a bizarre humanoid figure.

This explanation has considerably more historical weight than some later internet theories because it appears in the contemporary official discussion.

# **13. The Glyn Edwards Confession**

Nearly two decades later, another important development occurred.

In 1996, local businessman **Glyn Edwards** reportedly admitted that he had participated in a prank during the 1977 UFO wave.

According to his account, he had worn a silver-lined protective suit and walked around the area.

He described doing it for amusement.

His confession is important because it provides a named individual who acknowledged creating at least part of the mysterious "silver man" phenomenon associated with the wider Pembrokeshire UFO reports.

However, there is a major limitation.

**Edwards did not prove that he created the UFO seen by the schoolchildren.**

His admission concerns the silver-suited figure and the wider UFO atmosphere around Pembrokeshire.

It does not establish that he manufactured the metallic object described by the children at Broad Haven Primary School.

Therefore:

**Glyn Edwards' confession may explain part of the wider UFO wave, but it does not conclusively solve the main school sighting.**

# **14. The February 17 School Incident**

The Broad Haven story did not end on 4 February.

On **17 February 1977**, another unusual report reportedly came from the school.

A teacher and two canteen workers were said to have observed a metallic or silvery-yellow object in a field.

The object was reportedly accompanied by a humming sound.

One account states that a human-like figure was briefly seen entering the object.

The weather and visibility were reportedly poor at the time.

This later sighting is important historically because it meant the Broad Haven story was no longer based solely on the original group of children.

However, it is still eyewitness testimony rather than photographic or instrument-based evidence.

# **15. The Broad Haven Triangle Becomes the Dyfed Triangle**

By this point, UFO reports were being received from locations throughout the surrounding area.

Reports included:

* Strange lights
* Metallic objects
* Disc-shaped objects
* Humanoid figures
* Objects apparently landing or hovering
* Unusual activity around farms
* Alleged electrical disturbances

The area eventually became known as the **Broad Haven Triangle**, **Welsh Triangle**, or **Dyfed Triangle**.

The exact boundaries were never scientifically defined.

It was essentially a media and UFO-research label for an area where a relatively large number of unusual reports were being collected.

David Clarke notes that the wider flap extended beyond the school itself and included reports around places such as Ripperston Farm and Little Haven.

# **16. The Ripperston Farm Reports**

One of the most important locations in the wider mystery was **Ripperston Farm**.

The Coombs family — including Billy and Pauline Coombs — reported a number of unusual experiences.

Among the claims were:

* Strange lights
* UFO sightings
* A large silver-suited figure
* Unusual electrical problems
* Strange activity around vehicles
* Unexplained movement of livestock
* Other alleged encounters

The most famous incident reportedly occurred during the early hours of **23 April 1977**.

The family claimed that a very tall figure wearing what appeared to be a metallic or spacesuit-like outfit appeared outside a window.

The figure was described as approximately seven feet tall.

These reports helped turn the local UFO story into something much larger.

However, they should not be treated as independent proof of the Broad Haven school incident.

They involved different witnesses, different locations and different circumstances.

# **17. The Haven Fort Hotel Incident**

Another major report came from **Rosa/Rose Granville**, associated with the Haven Fort Hotel at Little Haven.

On **19 April 1977**, she reportedly saw a strange illuminated object and two human-like figures.

The object was described in later accounts as resembling an upside-down saucer.

The figures were reportedly wearing clothing resembling suits.

Granville's report became important because she contacted her Member of Parliament, **Nicholas Edwards**, seeking answers from the Ministry of Defence.

The MOD subsequently became involved in the wider Welsh reports.

David Clarke's later archival research places this event firmly on 19 April 1977 and notes that some later retellings have incorrectly placed it earlier in the sequence.

# **18. What Did the Government Tell Parliament?**

Despite the secretive inquiry, the public government position was restrained.

The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Defence, **James Wellbeloved**, reportedly stated that apart from Granville's report, the Ministry of Defence had **no record of unusual activity in the area**.

This created an interesting contrast.

Publicly, the government appeared to downplay the reports.

Internally, surviving records show that the UFO desk had asked the RAF Police to make a discreet inquiry.

This does not necessarily demonstrate a government cover-up.

A more conservative interpretation is that officials wanted to assess whether there was a genuine defence or aviation issue without publicly giving the UFO reports additional publicity.

# **19. Was There a Government Cover-Up?**

This is one of the most controversial questions surrounding Broad Haven.

UFO writers have sometimes argued that the British government secretly knew much more than it admitted.

There is certainly evidence that government officials were interested.

There is also evidence that the RAF Police conducted or were asked to conduct a discreet inquiry.

But there is **not enough evidence to establish that the British government recovered an alien spacecraft, alien bodies or extraterrestrial technology from Broad Haven.**

There is also no confirmed evidence that officials knew the object was extraterrestrial.

The surviving documents demonstrate government interest.

They do not demonstrate extraterrestrial knowledge.

That distinction is essential.

# **20. The Military-Technology Theory**

Another popular explanation is that the witnesses saw some kind of experimental military aircraft.

This theory gained popularity because Broad Haven was located within a region containing several military installations.

RAF Brawdy was nearby, while other military facilities existed elsewhere in Wales.

The theory suggests that unusual aircraft could have been operating in the area, possibly explaining reports of strange shapes or movements.

There is, however, no publicly established evidence identifying a particular secret aircraft as the object seen by the children.

Historian David Clarke notes that journalist Hugh Turnbull, who followed the story locally, believed something military might have been involved.

# **21. The Harrier Jet Theory**

A later explanation suggested that some sightings could have involved **Hawker Harrier aircraft**.

Harriers were capable of unusual flight behaviour, including hovering and very slow movement.

To an observer unfamiliar with the aircraft, certain manoeuvres could potentially appear strange.

However, this does not explain every detail of the Broad Haven reports.

In particular, it does not automatically explain the children's description of a metallic object apparently resting in a nearby field.

Therefore, the Harrier explanation remains a possible explanation for **some reports**, rather than a demonstrated solution to the entire Welsh Triangle.

# **22. The Oil-Refinery Protective-Suit Theory**

This theory specifically addresses the mysterious humanoid figures.

The Milford Haven area had major petroleum and industrial facilities.

Workers could use protective clothing that might look unusual from a distance.

The RAF reportedly noticed that descriptions of the silver-suited figures resembled protective clothing associated with refinery work.

This becomes even more interesting because of Glyn Edwards's later admission that he actually wore a silver-lined protective suit while carrying out a prank in the area.

This combination makes it reasonably plausible that **at least some of the "silver people" reports were caused by humans wearing unusual protective clothing.**

But again, it does not prove that the schoolchildren's object was a prank.

# **23. The Sewage-Tanker / Farm-Machinery Theory**

One of the more mundane explanations proposed for the school incident is that the children misidentified an ordinary vehicle or piece of equipment.

Because the object was reportedly in a field and partially obscured by vegetation, perspective could have dramatically altered its apparent appearance.

A metallic vehicle, tanker or piece of agricultural equipment viewed from a distance could potentially appear unusual, especially to children who were already excited by UFO stories.

There is no definitive evidence showing that a specific tanker or machine was actually present at exactly the location and time of the sighting.

Therefore this remains a **possible identification**, not a proven solution.

# **24. The Science-Fiction Influence Theory**

Another possibility is that popular culture influenced what the children remembered.

The 1970s were saturated with science-fiction imagery.

Television programmes, magazines, books and UFO stories were common.

David Clarke's research has examined how UFO imagery circulating in popular culture could influence witness drawings and descriptions.

This does not mean that the children deliberately invented their story.

Human memory is reconstructive.

If someone sees something unfamiliar, the brain often interprets it using familiar visual concepts.

A strange object could therefore become a "flying saucer" in a person's memory because that was the cultural language available to describe it.

# **25. The Social-Contagion Theory**

This is probably one of the strongest psychological explanations.

The children were part of the same school community.

They could talk to each other.

They could hear other children's descriptions.

The event also quickly attracted newspapers, television crews and UFO investigators.

That creates an environment where individual memories can gradually become more similar.

This is particularly relevant to the famous drawings because the drawings were made **three days after the sighting**, rather than immediately afterward.

That does not prove contamination occurred.

But it means the possibility cannot be excluded.

# **26. The "Children Wouldn't All Lie" Argument**

Supporters of the case often make a simple argument:

**"One child could lie, but why would an entire group of children tell the same story?"**

It is a reasonable question.

The headmaster himself reportedly did not believe the children were capable of organizing a sophisticated hoax.

Several witnesses continued to insist decades later that they saw something unusual.

That gives the case genuine historical interest.

But there is an important distinction:

**Multiple sincere witnesses do not automatically mean the interpretation of the event is correct.**

A group of people can sincerely misidentify the same object.

They can also influence one another's memories.

Therefore, sincerity and extraterrestrial origin are two completely different questions.

# **27. The "Mass Hallucination" Theory**

Some people have suggested that the children experienced a collective hallucination.

This explanation is generally too simplistic.

There is no good reason to assume that a large group of healthy children suddenly experienced exactly the same hallucination.

A more realistic psychological explanation would involve:

* An ambiguous stimulus
* Excitement
* Social influence
* Memory reconstruction
* Existing UFO expectations
* Repeated storytelling

In other words, **misidentification plus social reinforcement** is a more plausible skeptical model than a literal shared hallucination.

# **28. The Extraterrestrial Hypothesis**

Of course, the most famous theory is that the children saw an extraterrestrial spacecraft.

Supporters point to:

* Multiple witnesses
* The apparent structure of the object
* The reported dome and light
* The alleged humanoid
* Later sightings in the surrounding region
* Government interest
* The absence of a definitive conventional identification

Under this hypothesis, the object could have been an unknown spacecraft and the humanoid figure could have been an occupant.

The problem is evidence.

There is no authenticated extraterrestrial material.

No spacecraft was recovered.

No scientifically verified alien biological evidence was obtained.

No conclusive radar record has been linked to the school sighting.

No photograph clearly showing the craft exists.

Therefore, extraterrestrial origin remains a **hypothesis rather than an established fact**.

# **29. The Alien Underground-Base Theory**

One of the more extreme theories suggested that extraterrestrial beings had established an underground or underwater base in the region.

Some UFO researchers associated the reports with the coastline and locations such as the Stack Rocks.

The theory was helped by the presence of military installations and restricted areas.

However, there is no verified evidence of an extraterrestrial base beneath Pembrokeshire.

This belongs firmly in the category of speculative UFO folklore rather than documented history.

# **30. The Welsh Folklore Theory**

An unusual interpretation developed around the wider Dyfed Triangle.

Some researchers noticed similarities between the reported entities and older Welsh or Celtic stories involving strange beings, fairies and supernatural visitors.

The idea is not necessarily that the children literally encountered fairies.

Rather, the theory suggests that cultural expectations can influence how people interpret unusual experiences.

This became particularly prominent in later documentaries examining the Broad Haven story.

Netflix's *Encounters*, for example, explicitly explored similarities between the UFO reports and Welsh/Celtic folklore.

# **31. Did Anyone Photograph the UFO?**

This is one of the biggest weaknesses in the Broad Haven case.

Despite the amount of media attention the incident received, there is **no authenticated photograph clearly showing the alleged craft from the school incident.**

There are photographs of:

* Broad Haven
* The school
* Witnesses
* Newspaper coverage
* Children's drawings
* Investigators

But these should not be confused with photographic evidence of the UFO itself.

This distinction is extremely important for anyone writing about the case.

**A photograph of a witness or a newspaper containing a UFO story is not a photograph of the UFO.**

# **32. Was There Any Physical Evidence?**

No physical evidence conclusively connected to the school sighting has been established.

There is no verified:

* Alien material
* Metal fragment
* Engine component
* Biological sample
* Radiation signature
* Landing gear
* Mechanical component

that has been scientifically authenticated as coming from the alleged craft.

Some later reports in the wider Dyfed Triangle included claims of disturbed ground, electrical problems and other physical effects.

But these reports should not be confused with independently verified scientific evidence.

# **33. Was There Radar Evidence?**

There is no publicly established radar recording conclusively demonstrating that an unidentified spacecraft was present during the Broad Haven school sighting.

This is another major difference between Broad Haven and certain later UFO cases involving military radar data.

Broad Haven's primary evidence remains:

**Eyewitness testimony + drawings + government correspondence.**

It is not a sensor-confirmed UFO case.

# **34. What Happened to the Children?**

The children grew up and most eventually disappeared from public attention.

The case nevertheless followed them for decades.

Some former pupils continued to insist that they had seen something unusual.

David Davies became one of the most prominent adult witnesses.

In later interviews, he remained cautious about claiming that the object was extraterrestrial.

His position is particularly interesting because he does not need to believe in aliens to maintain that he saw something he could not explain.

That is essentially the distinction between:

**"I saw something strange."**

and

**"I saw an alien spacecraft."**

The first claim requires only eyewitness testimony.

The second requires considerably stronger evidence.

# **35. Did Any Witness Ever Admit It Was Fake?**

No schoolchild has produced a credible confession proving that the entire school incident was fabricated.

However, **Glyn Edwards did confess to participating in a silver-suit prank connected to the wider 1977 UFO wave.**

This creates an important middle ground.

It is possible that:

* Some reports were genuine misidentifications.
* Some were influenced by rumours.
* Some involved deliberate pranks.
* Some involved ordinary aircraft.
* Some witnesses sincerely misunderstood what they saw.

The existence of one prank does not automatically make every report fraudulent.

But it does demonstrate that at least part of the broader mystery had a human source.

# **36. Why Did the Case Become So Famous?**

Several things happened almost perfectly for a UFO legend to develop.

First, the witnesses were children.

Second, there were multiple witnesses.

Third, the object was reportedly close to the school rather than merely a distant light.

Fourth, drawings existed.

Fifth, humanoid figures were allegedly involved.

Sixth, the story reached national newspapers and television.

Seventh, government records later demonstrated that officials had taken an interest.

Finally, the wider area produced additional reports.

Each new element strengthened the mythology surrounding the original incident.

The result was a story that became much larger than the original school sighting.

# **37. The Role of the Media**

The media played a major role.

Once newspapers began reporting the story, Broad Haven attracted journalists, television crews and UFO investigators.

The village became a national UFO hotspot.

Historian David Clarke has noted that the media attention itself may have influenced the development of the wider flap.

This is an important point.

Media coverage does not mean the original sighting was fake.

But once an unusual story becomes public, people naturally begin comparing their experiences with it.

Someone who sees an ordinary aircraft after hearing about a UFO may be more likely to describe it as a UFO.

That phenomenon can cause a cluster of reports to develop around a single original story.

# **38. The 1977 Cultural Background**

Broad Haven happened during an era when UFOs were already deeply embedded in popular culture.

UFO books were widely available.

Television regularly featured science fiction.

The famous movie **Close Encounters of the Third Kind** was released in the United Kingdom later in 1977.

The timing is interesting, although the film cannot explain the February reports because it had not yet been released in Britain.

More importantly, UFO stories were already circulating internationally before Broad Haven.

David Clarke has suggested that the immediate influence on some Welsh children's reports may have been other contemporary UFO stories rather than later films.

# **39. What Was the "Dyfed Triangle"?**

The Dyfed Triangle was not an official government designation.

It was a name used to describe the concentration of UFO reports in parts of west Wales during 1977.

The wider reports extended across areas including:

* Broad Haven
* Little Haven
* Ripperston
* Milford Haven
* Haverfordwest
* Areas around RAF Brawdy
* Other parts of Pembrokeshire

The reports were not all identical.

Some involved lights.

Others involved alleged landed objects.

Some involved humanoids.

Some involved unusual activity around farms.

This is why researchers debate whether these reports should really be considered one phenomenon.

# **40. The Case of Ripperston Farm and the Silver Figure**

The Ripperston Farm reports deserve special attention because they helped cement the image of mysterious "silver men" in the public imagination.

The Coombs family reportedly experienced repeated unusual incidents.

One of the most famous claims was the appearance of a very tall figure wearing a silver suit outside their home.

The family reportedly contacted the police.

Years later, one responding officer described the family as extremely frightened.

This does not prove the figure was extraterrestrial.

It does, however, show that the wider Welsh UFO story involved genuine reports made by people who apparently believed something unusual had happened to them.

# **41. What Did Skeptics Think?**

Skeptical explanations generally fall into several categories.

### **1. Misidentified ordinary object**

The children may have seen farm machinery, a vehicle or another ordinary object.

### **2. Aircraft**

Military aircraft, including Harriers, could have appeared unusual.

### **3. Protective clothing**

The silver humanoid reports could have been caused by people wearing industrial protective equipment.

### **4. Pranks**

The later Glyn Edwards confession demonstrates that deliberate hoaxing really did occur within the wider wave.

### **5. Social contagion**

Stories spread rapidly between children and adults, potentially making later accounts more similar.

### **6. Memory reconstruction**

The children's memories may have changed during the days, weeks and decades following the incident.

### **7. Media influence**

National coverage may have encouraged additional reports.

These explanations do not necessarily have to be mutually exclusive.

The entire Dyfed Triangle could have been a mixture of unrelated events.

# **42. What Do UFO Researchers Think?**

UFO researchers have generally emphasized the number of witnesses and the apparent consistency of the central object description.

The most interesting arguments include:

* Several children described a structured object.
* Some descriptions were produced before extensive national publicity.
* The headmaster did not believe the children were capable of a sophisticated hoax.
* Later witnesses claimed to have seen unusual objects in the same region.
* The government maintained records concerning the reports.
* Some witnesses continued to stand by their accounts decades later.

From this perspective, the case demonstrates that something genuinely unusual may have been observed, even if its identity remains unknown.

# **43. The Biggest Problem for the UFO Explanation**

The central problem is the lack of hard evidence.

There is no confirmed:

**Photograph → Radar → Physical material → Independent scientific test**

chain.

Instead, the evidence looks more like:

**Witnesses → Drawings → Newspaper reports → UFO investigation → Government correspondence**

That is enough to establish that the event was historically reported.

It is not enough to establish extraterrestrial visitation.

# **44. The Biggest Problem for the Skeptical Explanation**

The skeptical side also has weaknesses.

No specific ordinary object has been conclusively identified as the craft seen by the children.

The Glyn Edwards confession explains a silver-suited prank, but not necessarily the metallic object.

The tanker/farm-equipment theory remains speculative.

The military-aircraft theory has not produced a documented aircraft identification matching all the reported details.

Therefore, saying **"the case was completely debunked"** would go beyond the evidence.

A more accurate conclusion is:

**Some elements have plausible conventional explanations, while the central object remains unidentified.**

# **45. What Is the Most Likely Explanation?**

There is no scientifically proven answer.

However, if the evidence is ranked by what can actually be supported, a reasonable assessment is:

### **Most plausible: Misidentification + social influence**
