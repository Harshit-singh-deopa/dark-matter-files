# **What Was the Miracle of the Sun?**

The **Miracle of the Sun** refers to an extraordinary event reported on **October 13, 1917**, at Cova da Iria, near Fátima in central Portugal.

The event was connected to a series of reported apparitions experienced by three young shepherd children:

**Lúcia dos Santos**, aged 10

**Francisco Marto**, aged 9

**Jacinta Marto**, aged 7

According to Lúcia's later accounts, the children had reported seeing a mysterious woman whom they identified as the Virgin Mary.

The reported apparitions began on **May 13, 1917**, and were said to occur on the 13th of each month through October, with the August event taking place later because the children were prevented from reaching Cova da Iria on August 13.

During the July apparition, the children reportedly said that a public sign would occur in October.

That promise eventually attracted an enormous crowd.

# **Who Were the Three Children?**

The three children were cousins and came from the rural community around Fátima.

### **Lúcia dos Santos**

Lúcia was the oldest of the three, aged 10 in 1917.

According to the Fátima tradition, she was the principal spokesperson among the children and later became a religious sister.

She lived until **February 13, 2005**, making her the only one of the three children to survive into the 21st century.

### **Francisco Marto**

Francisco was Lúcia's cousin and Jacinta's older brother.

He was 9 years old during the apparitions.

According to the Fátima accounts, Francisco could see the apparition but did not hear the words spoken by the figure. Lúcia and Jacinta therefore told him what had been said.

Francisco died on **April 4, 1919**, during the influenza pandemic, after becoming seriously ill with pneumonic illness.

### **Jacinta Marto**

Jacinta was Francisco's younger sister and was 7 years old in 1917.

According to the accounts preserved by the Shrine of Fátima, she could see and hear the apparition.

She died in **1920**, at a very young age, following illness. She and Francisco were later beatified and eventually canonized by Pope Francis in 2017.

# **The Events Before October**

The October event did not happen in isolation.

It followed several months of reported apparitions and increasing public attention.

According to the Shrine of Fátima's chronology, the children reported their first Marian apparition on **May 13, 1917**.

The children continued to report experiences in June and July.

By August, however, local authorities had become concerned about the growing crowds and religious excitement surrounding the children.

The three children were taken to Vila Nova de Ourém and prevented from reaching Cova da Iria on August 13.

According to the Shrine's historical documentation, the reported apparition instead occurred at **Valinhos on August 19**.

By September, the crowds had become much larger.

The Shrine records that the roads were packed with people on September 13.

All of this created enormous anticipation for October.

# **Did the Sun Actually Move?**

There is no scientific evidence demonstrating that the astronomical Sun physically moved.

If the Sun had literally moved dramatically toward Earth and then returned, the consequences would have been catastrophic.

The Earth and the Sun are separated by approximately 150 million kilometers.

The Sun is vastly larger than Earth.

Therefore, the literal interpretation that the Sun physically descended toward the crowd cannot be reconciled with ordinary astronomy.

What witnesses experienced was therefore almost certainly a **visual phenomenon involving the appearance of the Sun**, rather than the physical Sun changing its position relative to Earth.

The important question is what caused that visual experience.

# **The Optical Explanation**

One of the most frequently proposed explanations involves **optical effects produced by looking toward a bright Sun**.

The human eye is not designed to stare directly at the Sun.

Very bright light can produce afterimages, glare and temporary visual distortions.

If many people were looking toward the Sun after waiting for hours in unusual weather conditions, some could have experienced visual effects.

A person might perceive the Sun as:

* vibrating
* changing shape
* changing color
* appearing to rotate
* appearing to move

This explanation can account for some descriptions without requiring the Sun itself to behave abnormally.

However, it does not automatically explain every report.

# **Could Atmospheric Conditions Have Played a Role?**

Yes.

Atmospheric conditions are another major possibility.

The crowd had been standing in rainy and humid conditions.

Clouds were breaking apart.

Moisture and changing cloud cover can alter the appearance of sunlight.

The Sun can appear as a bright disk through thin clouds, haze or moisture.

Atmospheric scattering can also create unusual color effects.

This could potentially contribute to reports of the Sun appearing yellow, orange, red or surrounded by changing colors.

Again, this is an explanation for the **appearance** of the Sun, not evidence that the Sun physically changed.

# **The "Dancing Sun" and Retinal Effects**

Another proposed mechanism is a phenomenon sometimes called **retinal image movement** or solar glare effects.

When someone stares toward an extremely bright source, involuntary eye movements can cause the image to appear to shift.

The brain can interpret the changing retinal image as movement of the object itself.

This could potentially explain why some witnesses perceived the Sun as moving or spinning.

The important limitation is that there was no controlled experiment at Fátima in 1917.

Consequently, modern researchers cannot determine exactly how much of the reported phenomenon resulted from retinal effects.

# **The Mass-Suggestion Theory**

Another explanation focuses on psychology.

Thousands of people had traveled to Fátima expecting something extraordinary.

They had been told for weeks that a sign would occur.

This creates an unusual psychological environment.

If one person shouted that the Sun was moving, others might look upward and interpret ambiguous visual effects in the same way.

Social expectation can influence perception.

This does not necessarily mean that everyone was lying.

People can sincerely experience something unusual while interpreting it differently because of their expectations.

The mass-suggestion hypothesis is therefore different from saying that the entire event was a deliberate hoax.
