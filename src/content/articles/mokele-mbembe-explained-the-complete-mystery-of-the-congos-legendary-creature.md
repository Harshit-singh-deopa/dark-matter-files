# **What Is Mokele-mbembe?**

Mokele-mbembe is generally described in modern cryptozoological literature as a large creature associated with the rivers, swamps and lakes of the Congo Basin.

The most familiar modern description is that of a massive, four-legged animal with a long neck, long tail, relatively small head and smooth skin. Some reports describe it as approximately the size of an elephant or hippopotamus. Other descriptions differ considerably, sometimes giving the creature horns, unusual feet or other features.

This inconsistency is important.

There is no single scientifically established description of Mokele-mbembe because there is no confirmed specimen from which zoologists could determine its anatomy.

The familiar image of a long-necked dinosaur is largely the result of the way Western writers and cryptozoologists interpreted African reports during the twentieth century.

The name is commonly translated as something similar to **“one who stops the flow of rivers,”** although translations and interpretations vary.

# **Where Is Mokele-mbembe Supposed to Live?**

The location most strongly associated with the creature is **Lake Télé and the surrounding Likouala wetlands in the Republic of the Congo**.

Lake Télé is a real freshwater lake surrounded by extensive swamp forest within the Congo Basin. Scientific research describes the lake as approximately 5.6 kilometres in diameter and situated in an exceptionally biodiverse region.

The surrounding Lac Télé Community Reserve is an important conservation area containing swamp forests, rainforest, waterways and large populations of wildlife.

The remoteness of the region is one reason the Mokele-mbembe story has remained compelling. Travel through the wetlands can be difficult, and many areas are accessible primarily by waterways and small boats.

Modern scientific work in the area has documented extensive biodiversity, including gorillas, chimpanzees, crocodiles, birds, fish, reptiles and amphibians. Recent environmental-DNA and camera-trap work has continued to reveal how biologically rich the region is.

But the existence of a difficult-to-explore environment does **not** by itself demonstrate the existence of an unknown giant animal.

# **The Important Problem With the Early History**

Mokele-mbembe is often presented online as though Europeans had been recording encounters with the same dinosaur-like animal for hundreds of years.

The actual documentary history is more complicated.

One frequently cited early source is a 1776 account by the French missionary Abbé Liévin-Bonaventure Proyart describing large claw-like tracks left by an animal that was not seen.

However, the account does **not** describe a long-necked dinosaur, and it does not use the name Mokele-mbembe.

For that reason, historians of cryptozoology caution against automatically treating the 1776 report as the first Mokele-mbembe sighting.

The modern dinosaur interpretation developed much later.

# **The 1909 Dinosaur Connection**

One of the most important events in the history of the legend occurred in **1909**, when German animal dealer and author **Carl Hagenbeck** published *Beasts and Men*.

Hagenbeck described reports of a strange creature supposedly living in African swamps and compared it to something between an elephant and a dragon. He then speculated that it might be a surviving dinosaur resembling a brontosaur.

This is extremely important because the reports Hagenbeck discussed were not presented as a scientific discovery of a living dinosaur.

They were stories and second-hand information.

Hagenbeck essentially interpreted those stories through the scientific and popular fascination with newly discovered dinosaur fossils.

The idea then spread through newspapers and popular literature.

This is one reason historians Daniel Loxton and Donald Prothero have argued that the modern Mokele-mbembe legend was strongly shaped by the cultural excitement surrounding dinosaurs in the early twentieth century.

# **The 1910 Newspaper Sensation**

Reports of supposedly living dinosaurs in Africa soon began appearing in newspapers.

The stories became sensational enough that some publications treated the possibility seriously.

However, not all contemporary observers accepted them.

A particularly revealing example appeared in **Nature in 1920**.

Nature reported that a sensational claim about a gigantic dinosaur-like reptile in the Congo had been investigated and was traced to a practical joke. The Smithsonian African Expedition explicitly denied that its members had gone to Africa to hunt a brontosaurus.

This is an important reminder that the early history of African “living dinosaur” reports already contained exaggeration, journalism and hoaxes.

# **The 1913 von Stein Account**

Another famous source frequently cited by Mokele-mbembe enthusiasts is a report attributed to German Captain Ludwig Freiherr von Stein zu Lausnitz during an expedition in Central Africa in 1913–1914.

Later writers claimed that von Stein had recorded reports from local guides describing a large, dangerous animal associated with rivers and swamps.

The account is often quoted as describing an animal approximately the size of an elephant or hippopotamus, with a long neck and other unusual characteristics.

However, there is an important documentary problem.

The original report has been difficult to independently verify, and much of the wording commonly reproduced today comes through later publications rather than a readily accessible original document.

Therefore, the von Stein account should be described as a **reported historical source**, not as independently verified zoological evidence.

# **How the Modern Mokele-mbembe Image Developed**

During the twentieth century, the creature gradually became standardized in Western popular culture.

The usual description became:

* enormous body
* long neck
* long tail
* four legs
* relatively small head
* reddish-brown, gray or dark skin
* plant-eating behavior
* association with water
* ability to remain partially submerged

The resemblance to a sauropod dinosaur became the defining feature.

Cryptozoologists began proposing that a population of sauropods had somehow survived the mass extinction at the end of the Cretaceous period.

That hypothesis, however, faces major scientific problems.

Non-avian dinosaurs disappeared from the fossil record approximately 66 million years ago, and there is no accepted evidence that a large sauropod survived into modern times.

# **Roy Mackal and the Search for Mokele-mbembe**

The person most strongly associated with the modern search was **Roy P. Mackal**, a University of Chicago biochemist who became deeply interested in cryptozoology.

Mackal had already investigated reports of the Loch Ness Monster before turning his attention to Central Africa.

In **1980 and 1981**, he organized expeditions to the Congo Basin to investigate reports of Mokele-mbembe. His expeditions interviewed local inhabitants, examined possible tracks and searched waterways and wetlands.

The expeditions did not obtain a confirmed specimen.

Mackal nevertheless considered some eyewitness reports and tracks significant enough to continue investigating the possibility of an unknown animal.

His expedition is important historically because it transformed Mokele-mbembe from a relatively obscure story into one of the best-known cryptozoological investigations of the late twentieth century.

# **What Mackal's Expedition Found**

Mackal's team reported finding unusual tracks.

In a December 1981 report, UPI quoted Mackal as saying that some tracks were roughly 9–10 inches in diameter and that vegetation around the trail appeared disturbed.

Mackal initially considered an elephant explanation but argued that certain characteristics did not seem to fit.

At the same time, Mackal acknowledged that a giant lizard or another known animal could potentially explain some evidence.

The tracks were never scientifically demonstrated to belong to a previously unknown dinosaur.

That distinction is critical.

A footprint-like impression in a difficult tropical environment can have many possible explanations, especially when the animal that supposedly made it is never observed directly.

# **The Herman Regusters Expedition**

Another famous investigation took place in **1981**, led by American engineer **Herman Regusters**.

Regusters traveled into the Lake Télé region with an expedition equipped with cameras, recording equipment and navigation technology.

Contemporary reporting described the expedition as an attempt to find the creature.

Regusters later claimed that he and members of his expedition had encountered an unusual long-necked animal.

The expedition became controversial because the strongest claimed observations did not produce a clear, independently identifiable photograph.

Regusters' own expedition report is preserved digitally through the California Institute of Technology's repository, making it one of the most useful primary documents for understanding what he himself claimed.

# **The Famous Regusters Photograph**

Regusters claimed that his expedition obtained a photograph of the creature.

The image has often been reproduced in books and television programs as possible evidence of Mokele-mbembe.

However, the photograph is extremely indistinct.

It does not provide enough detail to establish the identity, size or anatomy of the object.

It could not be used by zoologists to demonstrate the existence of an unknown species.

The problem illustrates one of the central issues surrounding Mokele-mbembe:

**many pieces of alleged evidence are ambiguous rather than independently verifiable.**

Skeptical investigations have also highlighted repeated instances in which expedition teams reported seeing something but were unable to preserve usable photographic or physical evidence.

# **The Regusters Sound Recording**

Regusters also reported hearing unusual sounds during the expedition.

He attempted to record them.

The resulting recordings became part of the evidence cited by supporters of the expedition.

However, the recordings were not sufficiently distinctive to establish that they came from a previously unknown animal.

There is no scientifically accepted acoustic signature belonging to Mokele-mbembe.

Consequently, the recordings remain part of the historical expedition record rather than proof of an undiscovered species.

# **The 1983 Marcellin Agnagna Expedition**

In **1983**, Congolese biologist **Marcellin Agnagna** led another expedition to Lake Télé.

Agnagna later reported that he had seen the creature.

According to accounts of the expedition, he described an animal emerging in the lake and remaining visible for a short period.

The major problem was that no usable photograph was obtained.

The explanation later associated with the expedition was that the camera had accidentally been left in macro mode.

This became one of the most discussed examples of Mokele-mbembe evidence that disappeared at the crucial moment.

Skeptical investigators have highlighted the incident because the expedition reportedly had photographic equipment but still failed to obtain an identifiable image.

# **Later Expeditions**

Mokele-mbembe investigations continued after the famous expeditions of the early 1980s.

Researchers, adventurers, journalists, television crews and cryptozoologists returned to the Congo Basin at different times.

The 2010 scientific review of Lake Télé lists numerous expeditions and investigations extending across several decades.

The important result is that none of the expeditions produced a scientifically verified specimen of Mokele-mbembe. Some investigators reported sightings, but no physical evidence established the animal as a new species.

# **The National Geographic Expedition**

In **2005**, the National Geographic Society supported an expedition to Lake Télé led by herpetologist **Brady Barr**.

This expedition was primarily interested in snakes rather than proving the existence of a dinosaur.

It later became associated with the Mokele-mbembe story because of its location and the continuing interest in mysterious animals of the Congo Basin.

# **The Elephant Explanation**

One of the strongest ordinary explanations for some sightings is the **forest elephant**.

An elephant moving through swamp water can expose only part of its body.

From a distance, vegetation can hide the legs and torso while the trunk and head remain visible.

Movement through dense vegetation can also make the animal appear much larger or stranger than it really is.

This explanation cannot automatically account for every Mokele-mbembe report, but it is consistent with modern observations from the Congo Basin.

# **The Hippopotamus Explanation**

Hippopotamuses have also been suggested as possible sources of some reports.

A partially submerged hippopotamus can appear to be an enormous unidentified animal when seen briefly from a distance.

Its rounded back and movement through water can also produce ambiguous shapes.

However, a hippopotamus does not explain the classic long-necked description.

Therefore, it is better regarded as a possible explanation for **some observations**, not the entire legend.

# **The Crocodile Explanation**

Crocodiles are another possibility.

Only the head and upper back may be visible above water, while the remainder of the body remains hidden.

The Congo Basin contains several crocodilian species, including the African dwarf crocodile.

A distant observer seeing only part of an animal in dark water could potentially misjudge its size.

Again, this does not explain every traditional description.

# **The Rhinoceros Theory**

Some researchers have proposed that historical stories about large unknown creatures could have been influenced by rhinoceroses.

# **The Folklore Theory**

Another explanation is that Mokele-mbembe is fundamentally a **folklore tradition rather than an undiscovered species**.

This does not mean that people who tell the stories are necessarily lying.

Folklore can preserve memories of real animals, environmental dangers, unusual experiences and supernatural beliefs.

Stories can also change as they pass between cultures.

Once Western audiences became fascinated with dinosaurs, reports of unusual African creatures were increasingly interpreted as evidence for surviving dinosaurs.

This may explain why the modern creature has such a strong dinosaur-like appearance.

# **The Surviving Dinosaur Theory**

This is the most famous theory.

According to the hypothesis, Mokele-mbembe could be a surviving population of sauropod dinosaurs.

Supporters point to:

A surviving population of giant sauropods would be expected to leave biological evidence.

None has been verified.
