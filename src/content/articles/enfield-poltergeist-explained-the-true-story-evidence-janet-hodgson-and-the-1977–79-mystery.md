# **Where Did the Enfield Poltergeist Happen?**

The events occurred at a council house on **Green Street in Enfield, North London**.

The house was an ordinary working-class suburban home rather than an old mansion, castle or supposedly ancient haunted building.

This was one of the features that made the story so striking.

There was no famous historical reputation attached to the property before 1977. The story emerged from the experiences reported by the family living there.

The household was headed by **Peggy Hodgson**, a single mother. Her children included Margaret, Janet, Johnny and Billy. Janet, who was **11 years old** when the disturbances began, eventually became the main focus of the investigation.

The family's father had left the household before the major events, leaving Peggy to raise the children.

The disturbances therefore occurred in an ordinary residential environment rather than an isolated or traditionally "haunted" location.

# **The Night Everything Began**

The case is generally dated to the night of **31 August 1977**.

Peggy Hodgson was at home with her children when unusual knocking and banging began.

According to the family's account, the disturbances appeared to come from different parts of the house.

At first, they were not immediately interpreted as a paranormal event.

Like anyone hearing unexplained noises inside a house, the family initially considered ordinary possibilities.

The situation changed when furniture was reportedly seen moving.

According to the SPR's historical account, Peggy reported seeing a chest of drawers apparently move across a bedroom floor. Neighbours were subsequently contacted, and the events began attracting outside attention.

# **The Neighbours Become Involved**

The Hodgsons were not the only people who claimed to hear unusual noises.

Neighbours **Vic and Peggy Nottingham** became involved and reported hearing disturbances.

This was important because the case was no longer based entirely on statements made by members of the Hodgson family.

Other people were now visiting the property and reporting strange events.

The Nottingham family became some of the most frequently cited witnesses in accounts of the case.

However, the quality of testimony varied considerably from incident to incident. Some witnesses saw specific events themselves, while others only heard sounds or were told what had supposedly happened.

That distinction becomes important when assessing the case today.

# **The Police Arrive**

One of the most famous aspects of the Enfield case is the involvement of the police.

Police officers attended the house after the family reported disturbances.

The SPR account records that **WPC Caroline Heeps** witnessed a chair apparently move across the living room.

The chair reportedly moved several feet without an obvious person pushing it.

This became one of the most frequently cited pieces of testimony supporting the case because it came from a police officer rather than a member of the family.

However, the officer's observation was still an individual eyewitness account rather than a controlled scientific experiment.

That difference is important.

It can establish that the officer genuinely reported seeing something unusual, but it cannot by itself establish that a supernatural force caused the movement.

# **The Daily Mirror Investigates**

The story soon reached the **Daily Mirror**.

On **4 September 1977**, neighbours contacted the newspaper because they wanted someone to investigate the strange events.

Reporter **Douglas Bence** and photographer **Graham Morris** visited the house.

Their visit became one of the most important early stages of the case.

According to Bence's later account, Morris was struck by a flying Lego brick during the visit. Bence said he personally witnessed the aftermath but did not see the entire trajectory of the object himself.

The Daily Mirror returned to the property several times.

Photographs were taken.

Reporters interviewed witnesses.

The story began receiving national attention.

This also created one of the central complications of the entire case:

**The investigation was happening while the story was simultaneously becoming a media sensation.**

# **Maurice Grosse Enters the Case**

The Daily Mirror eventually contacted the **Society for Psychical Research**.

The SPR had existed since the nineteenth century and had investigated claims involving alleged paranormal phenomena.

One of the members who became interested was **Maurice Grosse**, a retired inventor who had recently joined the organization.

Grosse first visited the house on **5 September 1977**.

He quickly became convinced that the family was experiencing something unusual.

He began recording incidents and interviewing witnesses.

Grosse's investigation eventually became one of the longest-running investigations into the case.

The SPR's archive records that the investigation produced a substantial body of written and recorded material.

# **Guy Lyon Playfair Joins the Investigation**

Another important investigator was **Guy Lyon Playfair**.

Playfair arrived at the house on **12 September 1977**.

He and Grosse eventually became the two investigators most closely associated with the case.

Together they spent an extraordinary amount of time observing the household.

The SPR records describe approximately **180 visits and 25 overnight vigils** during their investigation. More than **180 hours of tape recordings** were also produced.

Other sources and later archival descriptions refer to a much larger body of surviving audio material associated with the investigation.

The SPR has confirmed that cassette recordings donated by Grosse and Playfair are preserved in its audio-visual collection.

This surviving archive is one of the strongest reasons the Enfield case remains historically interesting.

It is not simply a story that was told decades later.

There are surviving recordings, photographs, written accounts and contemporary journalism.

# **The Mysterious Voice**

The most famous part of the entire case began later in 1977.

A deep male voice was reportedly heard coming from **Janet Hodgson**.

The voice sounded very different from Janet's normal speaking voice.

Investigators described it as rough, masculine and much deeper than the voice expected from an 11-year-old girl.

The voice reportedly claimed to belong to a man named **Bill Wilkins**.

It also reportedly provided information about his life and death.

The voice became the central mystery of the Enfield investigation.

# **Who Was Bill Wilkins?**

According to the voice, "Bill Wilkins" had once lived in the house.

It allegedly claimed that he had died downstairs.

This became particularly interesting because investigators later researched the property's previous occupants.

A former resident named **William Wilkins** had reportedly lived at the property and died there.

Supporters of the case considered this one of its most striking details.

Skeptics, however, pointed out that information about previous occupants could potentially have been discovered through ordinary means.

The important historical point is that **the existence of a former William Wilkins and the voice's statements about him became part of the case record, but this does not establish that the voice was actually produced by a deceased person.**

# **Why Janet Became the Center of the Case**

This question eventually became one of the most important disagreements between investigators.

# **Was Janet Faking the Voice?**

Yes, this possibility was seriously investigated.

Years later, Janet acknowledged that she and her sister had deliberately faked some incidents.

This is a very important distinction.
