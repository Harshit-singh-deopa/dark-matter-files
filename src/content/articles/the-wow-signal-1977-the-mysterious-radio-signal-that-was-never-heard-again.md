# **The Big Ear Radio Telescope**

The story of the Wow! Signal begins with the enormous **Big Ear radio telescope** operated by Ohio State University.

Big Ear was located near Delaware, Ohio, and looked very different from the large steerable radio dishes commonly seen today.

The telescope used a large fixed reflector system to observe the sky as Earth's rotation carried celestial sources through its observing beam.

Big Ear had originally been used for the Ohio Sky Survey, which catalogued radio sources across the sky.

Beginning in **1973**, the observatory became involved in a systematic search for signals that might indicate extraterrestrial technology.

The SETI equipment examined radio frequencies using a system containing **50 channels**, each approximately 10 kHz wide.

The computer system used an **IBM 1130**, while the program responsible for recording the observations was known as **N50CH**.

Much of the operation was automated, meaning that unusual signals were not necessarily noticed immediately by someone sitting beside the telescope.

This detail became very important in 1977.

# **Why Scientists Were Searching for Radio Signals**

Radio waves are extremely useful for studying the universe.

Stars, planets, galaxies, nebulae and other astronomical objects can produce radio emissions.

SETI researchers also realized that an advanced technological civilization could theoretically use radio waves to communicate over enormous distances.

One particularly interesting region of the radio spectrum is around **1420 MHz**, the frequency associated with neutral hydrogen.

Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe.

Because hydrogen is so common, the hydrogen line has long been considered an important universal astronomical reference point.

SETI researchers have therefore paid particular attention to this region of the spectrum.

However, it is important to understand that a signal near the hydrogen line is **not automatically an alien signal**.

Natural hydrogen itself produces radio emission around this frequency.

# **The Night of August 15, 1977**

On **August 15, 1977**, Big Ear was carrying out its normal automated observations.

At approximately **11:16 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time**, the telescope detected something unusual.

A powerful narrowband radio signal passed through the telescope's observing system.

The telescope recorded the event automatically.

Nobody immediately realized that anything extraordinary had happened.

The signal disappeared.

The observation continued.

Several days passed before anyone noticed the unusual data.

# **Jerry Ehman Finds the Signal**

Astronomer **Jerry R. Ehman** was reviewing the computer printouts from Big Ear when he noticed an unusual sequence.

Among the ordinary data was:

**6EQUJ5**

The pattern immediately stood out.

The numbers and letters represented the changing strength of the detected signal.

The signal had risen dramatically above the background noise and then fallen again.

Ehman recognized that this looked remarkably similar to the expected response of a distant point-like radio source passing through Big Ear's beam.

He took a red pen.

He circled the sequence.

Then he wrote one word beside it:

**“Wow!”**

That handwritten word became the name of one of the most famous events in SETI history.

# **What Does 6EQUJ5 Mean?**

One of the biggest misconceptions about the Wow! Signal is that **6EQUJ5 was an alien code**.

It was not.

The sequence does not translate into an alien sentence.

It does not spell a message.

It is simply a compact representation of the signal's strength as recorded by the telescope.

The Big Ear computer represented different signal-to-noise levels using numbers and letters.

The sequence:

**6 → E → Q → U → J → 5**

shows the signal becoming stronger and then weaker.

The strongest value was **U**, representing a signal approximately 30 times stronger than the relevant background noise in the measurement system.

Therefore, the famous “6EQUJ5” is essentially a graph of the signal's rise and fall expressed as characters.

# **Why Did It Last 72 Seconds?**

The signal is generally described as lasting approximately **72 seconds**.

This was connected to the way Big Ear observed the sky.

The six characters in the famous sequence represented successive measurements separated by approximately 12 seconds.

Six measurements of roughly 12 seconds each produce approximately 72 seconds.

The signal's gradual rise and fall was also important.

It looked similar to what researchers expected when a distant source passed through the telescope's beam because of Earth's rotation.

# **The Strange Rise and Fall**

The signal did not simply appear as random radio noise.

Its recorded strength increased and decreased in a very specific pattern.

It rose:

**6 → E → Q → U**

and then declined:

**U → J → 5**

The shape was important because Big Ear was designed in such a way that a distant radio source should gradually become stronger as it entered the telescope's beam, reach a maximum, and then weaken as it passed out of the beam.

The Wow! Signal closely followed that general pattern.

This was one of the reasons the original researchers considered an astronomical source plausible.

# **Where Did the Signal Come From?**

The signal appeared to come from the general direction of the constellation **Sagittarius**.

However, Big Ear could not identify a single star as the source.

The telescope's configuration resulted in two possible nearby positions in the sky.

The original analysis placed the possible source around the region of:

**Right ascension: approximately 19h 25m or 19h 28m**

**Declination: approximately −26°57′**

Later researchers have refined these coordinates using previously unpublished archival data.

The modern Arecibo Wow! research gives slightly different coordinates because researchers have reanalyzed the original observations using newer methods.

This is one reason different websites sometimes give slightly different coordinates for the Wow! Signal.

# **The Signal's Frequency**

The original Big Ear analysis estimated the signal at approximately:

**1420.4556 MHz**

This placed it extremely close to the neutral hydrogen line.

More recent research has refined the estimated frequency to approximately:

**1420.726 MHz**

The difference reflects later analysis of the historical data rather than evidence that two completely different signals were detected.

The important point is that the Wow! Signal occurred in the neighborhood of the hydrogen line, which is a scientifically interesting part of the radio spectrum.

# **Why the Hydrogen Line Matters**

The hydrogen line is important because hydrogen exists throughout the universe.

It provides astronomers with a natural reference point for radio observations.

Because of this, SETI researchers have historically considered it an attractive frequency around which a civilization might theoretically choose to transmit a deliberate signal.

The reasoning is straightforward.

A civilization searching for another intelligent civilization might choose a frequency that another technologically advanced civilization would recognize as scientifically significant.

But this idea is only a reason to pay attention to the frequency.

It is **not evidence that the signal was deliberately transmitted**.

Natural astronomical processes also produce radiation around the hydrogen line.

# **Was the Wow! Signal an Alien Message?**

This is the question most people associate with the case.

Researchers would ideally need a repeat signal, an independently confirmed detection, identifiable modulation, a clear source or some other evidence demonstrating that the signal was produced by technology.

None of that happened.

# **Why Wasn't It Detected Again?**

This is perhaps the most frustrating part of the entire case.

After discovering the signal, astronomers attempted to observe the same region again.

The source was not detected again by Big Ear.

Without another detection, none of these possibilities can be confirmed.

# **Why Big Ear Could Not Simply Point Back at the Source**

Another commonly misunderstood detail is the telescope itself.

Big Ear was not a modern radio telescope that could simply turn toward the source and track it continuously.

Its design depended heavily on Earth's rotation.

As the Earth turned, celestial objects moved through the telescope's observing beam.

The telescope's dual-feed system also meant that a genuine celestial source could potentially produce a characteristic pattern as it passed through one beam and, depending on circumstances, potentially another.

By the time Jerry Ehman discovered the unusual printout, the event had already happened.

Researchers could not simply rewind the sky and watch the signal again.

# **The Terrestrial Interference Theory**

One of the first possibilities researchers had to consider was radio interference from Earth.

Astronomer **Antonio Paris** proposed that the signal might have been associated with a comet, particularly **266P/Christensen**.

The theory received considerable media attention.

However, the comet explanation was strongly debated.

Critics questioned whether the proposed comet was actually in the correct location at the time of the original event and whether cometary emission could realistically produce a signal with the observed strength and narrow bandwidth.
