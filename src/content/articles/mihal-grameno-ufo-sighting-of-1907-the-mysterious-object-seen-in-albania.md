## **The Strange Account from the Albanian Mountains**

More than a century before UFOs became a familiar part of popular culture, an Albanian writer and freedom fighter recorded an unusual sight in the mountains.

The witness was **Mihal Grameno**, a journalist, writer and Albanian nationalist who was fighting alongside the guerrilla group of **Çerçiz Topulli** during the Albanian national movement against Ottoman rule.

According to Grameno's later memoir, one night while the fighters were stationed on a high mountain, a **shining object appeared in front of them**.

He wrote that the object remained suspended in the air for several minutes before disappearing.

The account has subsequently been described by UFO researchers and Albanian media as one of the earliest documented UFO reports in Albania. However, there is an important distinction: **Grameno did not use the modern term "UFO."** The UFO interpretation was applied much later to his description of an unidentified object.

That makes the story particularly interesting. It is not a report written in the modern UFO era. It is a historical description of something the witness apparently could not identify.

# **Who Was Mihal Grameno?**

Mihal Grameno was born in **Korçë on January 13, 1871** and died there on **February 5, 1931**.

He was much more than an alleged UFO witness. Grameno was a journalist, writer, political activist and participant in Albania's national liberation movement.

As a young man he spent time in Romania, where he became involved with the Albanian national movement and the patriotic society **Drita**.

In 1907 he joined the armed group led by Çerçiz Topulli. Grameno subsequently accompanied the fighters through southern Albania and documented many of their experiences.

His importance to this story comes from the fact that he eventually wrote down his memories of those years in his book ***Kryengritja Shqiptare***, generally translated as ***The Albanian Uprising***.

The book is not a UFO book. It is primarily a historical memoir about the Albanian resistance movement and the experiences of Topulli's guerrilla group.

The National Library of Albania's bibliography records a 1925 edition of *Kryengritja Shqiptare*, published in Tirana by the General Directorate of Education. The bibliographic entry lists the book at 204 pages with 10 portrait plates.

# **The 1907 Mountain Encounter**

The central account is remarkably short.

Grameno describes a night when the fighters of Çerçiz Topulli had established a temporary camp or position on a high mountain.

He then describes a **shining object** flying in front of them.

According to the commonly reproduced Albanian text:

> “Një natë, teksa luftëtarët e Çerçizit kishin ngritur një limer majë një bjeshke të lartë, na fluturoi përpara një objekt shkëlqyes që qëndroi disa minuta pezull në ajër dhe pastaj u zhduk.”

In English, the passage is commonly rendered approximately as:

> “One night, while Çerçiz's fighters were stationed on a high mountain, a shining object flew in front of us, remained suspended in the air for several minutes, and then disappeared.”

The passage is reproduced by several Albanian publications discussing the case, which attribute it to Grameno's *Kryengritja Shqiptare*.

The key descriptions are therefore quite limited:

* It happened **at night**.
* Grameno and the fighters were on a **high mountain**.
* The object was described as **shining**.
* It apparently **flew in front of them**.
* It then remained **suspended in the air for several minutes**.
* It eventually **disappeared**.

There is no detailed description of its exact shape in the surviving quotation commonly reproduced online.

There is also no reported measurement of its size, altitude, distance, speed or direction.

Those missing details are important.

# **Was the Incident Really in 1907?**

The year **1907** is widely attached to the account because Grameno was with Topulli's fighters during that period, and modern Albanian UFO articles generally date the sighting to 1907.

However, there is a subtle historical issue.

Grameno's book was published later. The National Library of Albania records the 1925 edition, and the book itself explains that Grameno wrote it from notes and memories he had kept from his time in the mountains.

Therefore, the safest historical wording is:

**The sighting is generally dated to 1907 and comes from Grameno's later memoir describing his experiences with Çerçiz Topulli's fighters.**

It would be too strong to claim that we possess a contemporaneous 1907 UFO report written on the night of the event.

# **Where Did It Happen?**

This is one of the biggest unanswered questions.

The commonly reproduced account says only that the fighters were on **a high mountain**.

It does **not provide a precise modern geographic coordinate** for the UFO sighting in the passage normally cited by later UFO publications.

The fighters operated across southern Albania during their campaign. Historical accounts place Topulli's group in various areas of southern Albania, including regions around Gjirokastër, Delvinë, Përmet, Tepelenë, Kolonjë and elsewhere.

Because the original UFO passage does not provide a precise location, assigning the sighting to a particular modern village or mountain would go beyond the available evidence.

For that reason, the most accurate description is simply:

**A mountainous area of southern Albania, during Grameno's time with Çerçiz Topulli's guerrilla group.**

# **The Historical Setting**

The sighting occurred against a very unusual background.

Albania was still part of the **Ottoman Empire**, and Albanian nationalist groups were working toward greater political and cultural independence.

Çerçiz Topulli's armed group was active during the years 1907–1908. Grameno joined the group and traveled with its fighters through the mountains.

The group faced Ottoman military and police forces and often moved through difficult terrain while attempting to avoid detection.

Historical accounts describe the fighters spending long periods in mountainous areas and moving from place to place.

This context matters because the UFO account was not recorded by a casual observer looking at the sky from a city.

It came from a man who was living in the mountains with a small armed group during a period of political conflict.

At the same time, that does **not** automatically make the identification of the object more reliable. Being experienced, educated or observant does not eliminate the possibility of a misidentification.

# **What Did Grameno Think He Had Seen?**

This is where the story becomes especially interesting.

There is no strong evidence that Grameno himself concluded that he had seen an extraterrestrial spacecraft.

The modern UFO interpretation comes largely from later writers.

Grameno's wording simply describes an **unidentified shining object**.

He does not provide a technical explanation.

He does not claim that the object contained occupants.

He does not mention aliens.

He does not describe communication with the object.

He does not describe a landing.

He does not report physical evidence.

He does not claim that the object came from another planet.

Therefore, calling it an **unidentified aerial observation** is much more historically defensible than saying Grameno reported an alien spacecraft.

# **Why Has It Become Known as a UFO Case?**

Modern UFO terminology is applied retrospectively.

A UFO, in its basic historical meaning, simply refers to an aerial object that the observer cannot identify.

Since Grameno described an object that appeared unusual and apparently remained suspended in the sky, later writers classified the event as a UFO sighting.

Albanian media have repeatedly described it as the earliest known documented UFO account in Albania.

That wording should still be treated carefully.

**"Earliest documented UFO account in Albania" does not mean "first UFO ever seen in Albania."**

It means that among the historical reports that modern writers have located, this is one of the earliest written accounts that has subsequently been interpreted as a UFO sighting.

# **Could It Have Been an Early Aircraft?**

This is one of the first possibilities people naturally consider.

Powered aviation was still extremely young in 1907.

The Wright brothers had demonstrated powered flight in 1903, but airplanes were primitive compared with later aircraft and had not yet become a normal sight in the Albanian countryside.

Some modern articles therefore argue that an airplane is unlikely.

However, saying that an airplane is **impossible** would go too far.

The account gives us no exact location, distance, altitude, direction, shape or duration precise enough to completely eliminate every conventional aircraft-related explanation.

At the same time, Grameno specifically described the object as something that remained suspended in the air for several minutes.

A conventional 1907 airplane would have been a poor match for that description, particularly if the witnesses accurately perceived the object as stationary.

So an early aircraft is **possible in principle but not an especially convincing explanation from the description alone**.

# **Could It Have Been a Meteor?**

A meteor is another frequently mentioned explanation.

But a meteor normally appears to move rapidly across the sky rather than remain apparently stationary for several minutes.

If Grameno's description of a stationary object is accurate, a typical meteor would therefore be a poor match.

Some Albanian UFO articles make this argument directly.

There is nevertheless an important caveat.

We do not know exactly what Grameno meant by the object's apparent suspension, nor do we know its angular motion relative to the surrounding stars or terrain.

Without an independent observation, astronomical data or a detailed contemporary record, a modern investigator cannot completely reconstruct what the witnesses saw.

# **Could It Have Been a Comet?**

A comet is even less compatible with the reported behavior.

Comets are distant astronomical bodies and do not suddenly appear in front of observers and hover over a mountain for several minutes before disappearing.

The later UFO literature therefore generally rejects a comet interpretation.

However, there is no evidence that Grameno himself conducted an astronomical identification attempt.

The comet theory is primarily a modern attempt to explain or eliminate possible astronomical objects.

# **Could It Have Been a Balloon?**

A balloon is one of the more interesting conventional possibilities.

Balloons can appear stationary or nearly stationary when viewed from the ground, particularly when their movement is difficult to perceive.

A reflective or illuminated balloon could also appear bright or shiny.

The problem is that the original account gives us no information about the object's shape, size, altitude or wind conditions.

We therefore cannot calculate whether a balloon would have behaved appropriately on that particular night.

There is also no surviving weather record linked specifically to the sighting that would allow a reliable reconstruction.

So a balloon is **possible, but unproven**.

# **Could It Have Been an Optical Illusion?**

This is another reasonable possibility.

At night, a bright distant object can be difficult to judge in terms of distance and movement.

A stationary light can appear to move because of the observer's own movement, while a moving object can sometimes appear stationary against a dark background.

The mountainous environment could have made distance and scale especially difficult to judge.

The fighters were also observing the object under nighttime conditions, without modern optical instruments.

This does not prove that the event was an illusion.

It simply means that human perception should be considered when evaluating a centuries-old sighting based on a short written description.

# **Could It Have Been Something Unusual but Completely Natural?**

Yes.

There are many natural or conventional objects that can look strange under particular lighting conditions.

Possibilities could include:

* A bright astronomical object viewed under unusual atmospheric conditions
* A distant aircraft
* A balloon
* A lantern or other illuminated object
* Atmospheric optical effects
* An object whose distance and motion were misjudged
* A combination of several visual effects

Because Grameno did not record enough information to distinguish among these possibilities, none can currently be established as the answer.

The theory is therefore speculative.
