## **Quick Facts**

# **What Happened in Bonsall?**

On the evening of **5 October 2000**, Sharon Rowlands was at her home in the Bonsall area of Derbyshire when she reportedly heard an unusual or eerie noise outside.

Rowlands had received a consumer camcorder only a few days earlier. After going outside and looking toward the surrounding countryside, she said she noticed a strange luminous object in the sky.

According to contemporary reports, the object appeared to be over woodland and fields roughly **two miles from her home**.

She began recording it.

The resulting recording became one of the better-known British UFO videos of the early 2000s. Contemporary reporting described the footage as lasting approximately **six and a half minutes**.

Rowlands later described the object as resembling a **large disc with a section missing from its lower part**. In another description, she called it a segmented disc with a dark centre and coloured lights around its edge.

She was particularly struck by the apparent size of the object.

From her perspective, it appeared enormous and at one point seemed close enough that she feared it might come down into the field.

# **The Woman Behind the Video**

Sharon Rowlands was not presented in contemporary coverage as a professional UFO investigator.

In fact, one of the interesting aspects of the story was that she reportedly considered herself a **sceptic** before the experience.

She later explained that she had not previously been convinced by UFO stories, but that seeing the object herself made her reconsider the subject. Contemporary reports also described Rowlands and her husband Hayden as ordinary residents with no previous involvement in producing UFO footage.

That background became an important part of the story.

Unlike many famous UFO cases that begin with an anonymous witness or a decades-old story, Bonsall had a named witness, a physical recording and contemporary media coverage.

# **What Did Sharon Rowlands See?**

Rowlands' description was unusual.

She said the object appeared:

* Large and luminous
* Disc-shaped or segmented
* To have a **dark centre**
* To have coloured lights around its edge
* To change apparent size
* To pulse
* To hover over the countryside
* At one point to appear to move closer
* Eventually to change orientation and disappear

Contemporary accounts describe the object as having **red, yellow, orange and blue lights**.

One description compared its shape to a disc with a "bite" taken out of the bottom.

It is important to remember that these descriptions are **Rowlands' observations**, not independently established physical characteristics of an extraterrestrial craft.

# **The Six-and-a-Half-Minute Recording**

The most important part of the case is the camcorder recording.

The footage was reportedly around **six and a half minutes long**.

The surviving excerpts that have circulated show a bright object against a dark sky. Because the camera was attempting to focus and zoom toward the distant light, the object appears heavily blurred and optically distorted.

According to descriptions of the circulated footage, the object appears to:

1. Hover in the sky.
2. Move toward the right.
3. Produce pulses of light.
4. Change its apparent appearance.
5. Rotate or flip.
6. Show two intense reddish lights.
7. Eventually disappear in a flash.

One particularly interesting feature is the appearance of **concentric rings** around the bright centre in some portions of the footage.

This feature later became extremely important because it can occur naturally when a camera records a bright light while it is out of focus.

# **Why Does the Video Look So Strange?**

The camera itself may be one of the most important pieces of the mystery.

Rowlands was using a consumer camcorder rather than professional astronomical equipment.

When a camera is pointed toward a bright distant light at night and the focus is incorrect, the light can spread across multiple pixels and produce a large glowing shape.

Instead of seeing a small point of light, the viewer can see something resembling:

**a glowing disc → rings → coloured edges → dark centre.**

Cheap or consumer camera optics can also produce colour fringes around extremely bright sources.

This means that an ordinary light source can potentially appear much more complicated on video than it does to the naked eye.

That became one of the strongest arguments against the object being a huge physical spacecraft.

# **The Strange Lights Around Bonsall**

The Rowlands video became even more interesting because it appeared during a period in which other people in the Bonsall area were also reporting unusual lights.

Contemporary reporting stated that **19 UFO sightings had been reported around Bonsall during the four months leading up to the Rowlands incident**.

Other reports included descriptions such as:

* A **ball of fire**
* Two large bright lights
* A pink glow
* A vertically shaped object resembling a shoebox

One man walking his dog reportedly described seeing a pinkish glow in the sky that remained visible for several minutes before suddenly disappearing.

These reports helped establish Bonsall's reputation as a local UFO hotspot.

However, there is an important distinction:

**Those other sightings do not independently prove that Rowlands' object was the same phenomenon.**

They demonstrate that several unusual-light reports existed in the region, but they do not establish that the witnesses saw one physical object.

# **The UFO Researchers' Reaction**

The footage attracted considerable attention from UFO researchers.

One of the most prominent figures associated with the case was **Omar Fowler**, founder of the Derby-based Phenomenon Research Association.

Fowler reportedly viewed Rowlands' recording and considered it highly significant. He regarded Bonsall as an important location for UFO reports and compared the footage favourably with other contemporary UFO videos.

From the UFO-believer perspective, the case had several attractive features:

* A named witness
* A physical video
* Several minutes of footage
* Multiple unusual reports from the surrounding area
* A witness who claimed to have been sceptical beforehand
* An object that appeared to behave strangely

These points helped the video become a major subject of British UFO discussion in the early 2000s.

# **The Hollywood Connection**

The story became much bigger in 2001.

American television producer **Robert Kiviat**, associated with programs dealing with UFOs and alleged paranormal footage, became interested in the recording.

Contemporary reports said Kiviat Productions had obtained or intended to obtain the footage and that it was being considered for a television production called **"Could It Be True?"**.

The press also reported an extraordinary figure of approximately **£20,000** for the footage.

Kiviat reportedly described the recording as one of the most impressive UFO videos ever made.

However, this is where the story becomes complicated.

# **Did Sharon Rowlands Really Receive £20,000?**

This claim needs to be treated carefully.

Some contemporary reports stated that Rowlands had sold the footage to an American television company for **more than £20,000**.

But later reporting and correspondence disputed this.

Researcher Patrick Gross published a clarification in 2001 stating that, according to information from Santiago Yturria and Robert Kiviat, **no money had actually changed hands and no completed deal had been made**.

The explanation given was that an agent had apparently been acting on behalf of Kiviat Productions in Britain without the company itself initially knowing about the situation. The proposed arrangement reportedly collapsed after newspapers became involved.

Therefore, it is safer for a serious article to say:

> **Contemporary newspapers reported a £20,000 sale, but later clarification disputed that claim and stated that no money had changed hands.**

This is much more accurate than simply saying that Rowlands definitely received £20,000.

# **The NASA Connection**

Another major part of the story involved NASA.

The press reported that the Bonsall footage appeared similar to unusual objects recorded during the **STS-75 Space Shuttle mission** in 1996.

Those images had already become famous among UFO enthusiasts because some viewers interpreted the objects near the Shuttle as spacecraft.

The comparison helped generate claims that the Bonsall object might be related to the objects seen during the Shuttle mission.

However, this needs to be separated from fact.

There is evidence that the Bonsall footage was **promoted as something NASA might examine**.

There is not good evidence that NASA subsequently authenticated the Bonsall object as an extraterrestrial spacecraft.

Likewise, Dr. Bruce Maccabee was reportedly mentioned as a potential person who might examine the footage, but there is no established published NASA analysis by him proving what the object was.

So:

**NASA interest was reported. NASA confirmation was not.**

# **What Was the STS-75 Connection?**

The comparison came from the appearance of the objects in the famous 1996 Shuttle footage.

The STS-75 mission carried out experiments involving a tethered satellite. During the mission, cameras recorded numerous bright objects that UFO enthusiasts later interpreted as spacecraft.

However, the objects have also been explained as **nearby particles, ice and debris illuminated by sunlight**, with camera focus and optical effects making them appear much larger or more structured.

This is relevant to Bonsall because it demonstrates how easily an optical effect can transform a small bright object into something that looks enormous on video.

The existence of a visual similarity therefore does **not** establish that the two recordings show the same type of craft.

# **Was There an Official Government Investigation?**

Unlike some famous UFO cases investigated by military agencies, Bonsall does not appear to have received a formal government investigation comparable to Project Blue Book.

The **Meteorological Office** was contacted in connection with the case.

Its response was significant but limited.

The office reportedly said there were no unusual or freak weather conditions on 5 October that could account for the reported circular appearance.

That does **not** mean the Meteorological Office concluded that the object was extraterrestrial.

It simply meant that unusual weather was not identified as an obvious explanation.

The distinction is important.

# **The Streetlight Theory**

The most famous mundane explanation came years later.

In the 2008 Channel 5 documentary **Britain's Closest Encounters**, video expert **Peter Marriot** examined the footage.

His conclusion was that the object Rowlands filmed was **a street lamp**.

Rowlands rejected that explanation.

This became one of the central disagreements surrounding the case.

The streetlight theory is not completely unreasonable.

A bright lamp filmed while severely out of focus can produce a circular glowing shape with a dark centre and coloured edges.

The camera's focusing system can also make the apparent size and structure of the light change dramatically.

In other words, some of the strangest characteristics of the Bonsall footage can potentially be produced by ordinary optics.

# **Why Some People Rejected the Streetlight Explanation**

Supporters of the UFO interpretation have argued that the streetlight explanation does not fit the witness's description of the location.

Rowlands maintained that she was looking toward an area where there were no street lamps.

The UFO research group BEAMS has also strongly rejected the streetlight explanation, arguing that there were no streetlights in the direction in which the object was filmed.

However, this argument has an important limitation.

The full original recording and complete technical analysis have not been made publicly available in a form that allows everyone to independently determine exactly which light source was in the camera's field of view.

Therefore, neither side has a completely decisive demonstration.

# **Could It Have Been an Aircraft?**

Another possibility is a conventional aircraft.

Aircraft lights viewed from a distance can appear very strange, particularly when recorded with a zoomed consumer camcorder at night.

Different navigation and landing lights can produce:

* White lights
* Red lights
* Green lights
* Flashing patterns
* Apparent changes in size
* Apparent movement

The BBC's contemporary reporting also noted that some people believed military aircraft might explain some of the sightings around Bonsall.

However, there is no established evidence identifying Rowlands' particular object as a military aircraft.

So aircraft remain a **possible explanation**, rather than a proven one.

# **Theory 1 — A Defocused Streetlight**

This is probably the strongest conventional explanation.

A bright stationary light can become a large circular blob when a camera loses focus.

The dark centre could result from the optical structure of the lens, while the coloured edge can be produced by optical aberration and the camera's response to intense light.

As Rowlands zoomed toward the object, the camera's focus could have changed, causing the apparent object to expand, contract and transform.

This explanation can account for a surprisingly large amount of the footage.

**Problem:** The exact light source has not been conclusively demonstrated publicly.

# **Theory 2 — An Aircraft**

A conventional aircraft is another possibility.

A distant aircraft can appear almost stationary when flying toward or away from the observer.

Its lights can also create unusual shapes when filmed out of focus.

**Problem:** No specific aircraft has been conclusively identified as the object filmed by Rowlands.

# **Theory 3 — Optical and Camera Effects**

This theory does not require the object to have been a streetlight.

It could have been **any bright distant light source**.

The camera's focus, zoom, lens characteristics, digital imaging system and atmospheric conditions could have transformed a relatively ordinary light into the strange disc seen in the footage.

This is particularly important because the surviving video is noticeably out of focus.

# **Theory 4 — An Unknown Aircraft or Unidentified Object**

The cautious UFO position is simply:

**Something was filmed, but its identity cannot currently be established.**

This does not automatically mean extraterrestrial.

The term UFO/UAP literally concerns something that has not been identified.

Under this interpretation, the footage is genuine evidence of an unexplained visual observation, but its origin remains unknown.

This is probably the most scientifically cautious description of the case.

# **Theory 5 — Extraterrestrial Spacecraft**

The more extraordinary interpretation is that Rowlands recorded an extraterrestrial craft.

Supporters point to:

* The unusual disc shape
* The dark centre
* Coloured lights
* Apparent hovering
* Apparent shape changes

However, none of these observations establishes an extraterrestrial origin.

There is no publicly verified alien material, biological evidence, radar record or independently authenticated spacecraft associated with the case.

Therefore, **the extraterrestrial explanation remains a hypothesis, not an established fact**.

# **Theory 6 — A Hoax**

A hoax has also been suggested in discussions surrounding UFO footage.

The possibility cannot simply be dismissed because video can be manipulated or staged.

However, there is no strong publicly established evidence demonstrating that Rowlands deliberately fabricated the recording.

At the same time, the absence of evidence of a hoax does not prove that the footage depicts an alien craft.

The appropriate position is therefore:

**No convincing public evidence establishes either a deliberate hoax or an extraterrestrial spacecraft.**

# **The Biggest Problem With the Case**

Ironically, the most important piece of evidence — the original recording — is also the biggest limitation.

The original tape was reportedly kept secure, including being stored in a bank vault in Nottingham before copies were circulated.

Only excerpts have become widely accessible.

That creates a serious problem for modern analysis.

Without the complete original-quality recording, researchers cannot easily examine:

* Every frame
* Original resolution
* Original exposure data
* Camera focus behaviour
* Zoom behaviour
* Exact movement of the camera
* Complete duration of the event
* What happened immediately before and after the famous portions
* Whether the apparent shape changes were caused by the object or the camera

This is one reason why the case remains controversial.

# **Was the Object Really Huge?**

Probably not — at least, there is no evidence that allows us to establish its physical size.

Some newspaper reports repeated claims that the object could have been **several miles wide**.

But that conclusion came from estimating its apparent size and distance based on the witness's perception.

A distant object can appear enormous if its actual distance is unknown.

Likewise, an out-of-focus light can appear much larger than the physical source.

One UFO researcher who reportedly viewed the complete footage argued that the object was much smaller than the sensational newspaper estimates, suggesting something closer to **50 feet** rather than miles across.

That estimate itself should also be treated as an opinion, because the necessary distance measurements were not independently established.

# **So, Was the Bonsall UFO Real?**

There are actually two different questions here.

**Was there really a video?**

Yes. A genuine recording associated with Sharon Rowlands' 2000 sighting exists and excerpts have circulated publicly. Contemporary reporting also confirms that the video was a real object of media and UFO-research interest.

**Was the object an extraterrestrial spacecraft?**

There is no reliable evidence establishing that.

The footage is interesting, but its poor focus and optical characteristics make conventional explanations possible.

The streetlight theory is particularly interesting because defocused bright lights can genuinely produce disc-shaped and ring-like images.

At the same time, the exact identification of the object has never been demonstrated conclusively enough to close the case.

# **Final Verdict**

The **Bonsall UFO case remains an intriguing but unresolved piece of British UFO history**.

Sharon Rowlands appears to have genuinely believed that she had recorded something extraordinary, and her recording became famous because it appeared to show a large luminous disc behaving strangely over the Derbyshire countryside.

The UFO interpretation is certainly fascinating.

But the evidence does not justify saying that an alien spacecraft was proven.

The most reasonable conclusion is that **Rowlands recorded a real light or object, but its exact identity remains uncertain**.

A defocused artificial light, aircraft or another conventional source could explain much of what appears in the footage. The strongest weakness of the case is also its greatest mystery: the original-quality recording has never been made sufficiently available for comprehensive independent analysis.

Until the complete original footage, its technical metadata and the exact viewing geometry can be independently examined, the Bonsall object should remain classified as:

**UNIDENTIFIED — BUT NOT PROVEN EXTRATERRESTRIAL.**
