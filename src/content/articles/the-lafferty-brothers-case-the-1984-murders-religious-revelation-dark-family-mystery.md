# **The Lafferty Brothers Murders: The Complete Story of the 1984 Utah Case**

## **Case Overview**

The **Lafferty brothers case** was a double murder committed in **American Fork, Utah, on July 24, 1984**.

The victims were **Brenda Wright Lafferty, 24**, and her **15-month-old daughter, Erica Lafferty**.

The killers were Brenda's brothers-in-law, **Ronald Watson Lafferty and Daniel Charles Lafferty**.

The case became nationally known because Ron Lafferty claimed that he had received a revelation from God commanding that Brenda, Erica and two other people be "removed." The killings were also connected to the brothers' involvement with a small fundamentalist group known as the **School of the Prophets**. Court records, however, also document a strong personal motive involving Ron's divorce, his anger toward Brenda, and his belief that Brenda had contributed to the breakdown of his marriage.

Ron and Dan were arrested weeks after the murders and eventually convicted in separate trials.

**Dan Lafferty:** two concurrent life sentences without parole.

**Ron Lafferty:** death sentence, later overturned and then imposed again after a retrial.

Ron spent approximately 34 years on death row before dying of natural causes in Utah State Prison on **November 11, 2019**, at age 78. Dan remains imprisoned under his life sentences.

# **The Victims**

## **Brenda Wright Lafferty**

Brenda Wright was born on **July 19, 1960**, in Logan, Utah, and was raised in a Latter-day Saint family in Idaho.

Her family remembered her as intelligent, outgoing, athletic and ambitious.

She competed in the **Miss Twin Falls Pageant in 1980**, finishing first runner-up, and received scholarship money that helped her pursue higher education. She attended the University of Idaho before transferring to **Brigham Young University**, where she studied broadcast journalism.

Brenda worked as a student news anchor at KBYU and hoped to pursue a career in television journalism.

She met **Allen Lafferty**, Ron and Dan's younger brother, while attending BYU.

They married on **April 22, 1982**.

Brenda's family later emphasized that she genuinely valued her faith and that her personality was very different from the fictionalized version portrayed in some dramatizations of the case. Her sister Sharon Wright Weeks has repeatedly argued that Brenda should be remembered for her life rather than only for the circumstances of her death.

# **Erica Lafferty**

Erica was Brenda and Allen's daughter.

She was only **15 months old** when she was murdered.

The inclusion of a toddler in Ron's alleged "removal revelation" became one of the most disturbing elements of the case.

According to trial testimony, Ron believed Erica would eventually become like her mother and therefore included the child in his claimed revelation.

Brenda and Erica were ultimately buried together in a cemetery in **Kimberly, Idaho**.

# **The Lafferty Family Before the Murders**

The Lafferty family was large and deeply involved in the Latter-day Saint community.

Ron had previously been regarded as a successful member of his community. He served in church and civic roles and had a relatively conventional life.

That began changing around **1982**.

Ron became increasingly influenced by his brother **Dan**, who held increasingly radical political and religious beliefs.

Dan opposed government authority, taxation and modern interpretations of the LDS faith. He also supported **plural marriage**, which had long been rejected by the mainstream LDS Church.

Ron gradually adopted many of Dan's beliefs.

His appearance changed dramatically, and he became increasingly focused on religious and political ideas.

In **1983**, Ron and Dan were excommunicated from the mainstream LDS Church.

# **Ron Lafferty's Marriage**

Ron was married to **Diana Lafferty**.

The marriage deteriorated as Ron became increasingly committed to his new beliefs.

One major issue was Ron's interest in **plural marriage**.

Diana eventually left him and moved to Florida with their six children.

Ron became convinced that several people were responsible for the collapse of his marriage.

Among them were:

* His sister-in-law **Brenda Lafferty**
* Brenda's infant daughter **Erica**
* **Chloe Low**, who had counseled Diana
* **Richard Stowe**, an LDS leader who had been involved in Ron's excommunication

Court records state that Ron believed Brenda had encouraged and assisted Diana in leaving him. He also blamed Low and Stowe for their roles in the divorce and his excommunication.

This personal anger is extremely important when examining the case.

# **The School of the Prophets**

Ron and Dan became involved with a small fundamentalist group known as the **School of the Prophets**.

The group was associated with **Robert Crossfield**, a man who claimed prophetic authority and taught members about receiving divine revelations.

Ron and Dan believed that they could receive communications from God.

Members met to discuss these claimed revelations and religious ideas.

The group was not the mainstream LDS Church.

In fact, the Lafferty brothers had already been excommunicated from the mainstream church.

This distinction is important because the Lafferty murders should **not** be described as a crime committed by the mainstream LDS Church or as a typical representation of mainstream Latter-day Saint beliefs.

# **Ron's Claimed Revelations**

Ron claimed that he began receiving revelations directly from God.

One particularly important revelation became known as the **"removal revelation."**

According to court records, Ron claimed that four people had become obstacles to what he believed was God's work.

They were:

1. **Brenda Lafferty**
2. **Erica Lafferty**
3. **Chloe Low**
4. **Richard Stowe**

The revelation instructed that the people be "removed."

The other members of the School of the Prophets did not accept Ron's revelation as legitimate.

The disagreement became so serious that the group broke apart.

Ron and Dan, however, continued to believe that the revelation needed to be fulfilled.

# **The Second Revelation**

Ron also claimed to have received another revelation on **March 13, 1984**.

This revelation concerned preparing or "consecrating" an instrument that could be used to carry out the removal.

The other members of the group rejected the idea.

The School of the Prophets subsequently disintegrated over the disagreement.

Ron and Dan nevertheless continued discussing the killings.

# **Why Was Brenda Targeted?**

This is where the case becomes more complicated than simply saying:

**"Ron killed Brenda because God told him to."**

The prosecution argued that Ron's religious claims were intertwined with personal anger and revenge.

Ron blamed Brenda for helping Diana leave him.

Brenda also opposed the increasingly extreme beliefs of Ron and Dan.

She reportedly discouraged her husband Allen from becoming deeply involved with their brothers' group.

Ron therefore saw Brenda as an obstacle to both his personal life and his religious ambitions.

At the 1996 retrial, prosecutors argued that the murders were essentially revenge killings in which religion was used to justify the violence. Brenda's sister Sharon Weeks later strongly emphasized the same interpretation, saying the real story was more about **jealousy and revenge** than religion.

However, the religious-revelation explanation was also genuine in the sense that Ron repeatedly claimed to believe it.

The exact relationship between his religious beliefs, mental state and personal anger was heavily debated during his trials and appeals.

# **July 24, 1984 — The Day of the Murders**

On **July 24, 1984**, Ron and Dan traveled with two other men:

* **Charles "Chip" Carnes**
* **Ricky Knapp**

The four men traveled together in a green station wagon.

According to testimony, Ron had initially talked about obtaining firearms.

He eventually acquired weapons, but the murders were committed with knives.

The group went to the American Fork home where Brenda and Erica lived.

Allen Lafferty was away at work.

Ron and Dan entered the home.

Carnes and Knapp remained outside in the vehicle.

# **The Murder of Brenda**

Inside the home, Brenda was attacked.

Court records state that she was severely beaten, strangled with a vacuum cord and had her throat cut.

She died in the kitchen.

Her husband Allen later discovered her body.

The Utah Supreme Court described the physical evidence and circumstances in its later opinions, and the prosecution presented extensive forensic evidence at the trials.

# **The Murder of Erica**

Erica was killed inside the home.

Dan was identified in court records as the person who killed the child.

The killings were extremely violent.

Because the case involves a child victim, detailed crime-scene descriptions are intentionally omitted here. Your website can accurately describe the murders without reproducing graphic material.

The Utah Supreme Court later confirmed that Erica was 15 months old and that her throat had been cut.

# **What Happened Immediately Afterward?**

Ron and Dan left the apartment.

The four men then drove to the home of **Chloe Low**, one of the people named in Ron's revelation.

They intended to kill her as well.

However, Chloe was not home.

The men broke into her house and took various items.

After leaving Low's home, Ron discussed going after **Richard Stowe**.

Stowe was the LDS leader involved in Ron's excommunication.

The group began driving toward Stowe's home.

They missed the turnoff.

The brothers ultimately abandoned the plan.

They stopped at a service station and then headed toward **Wendover, Nevada**.

This is important because **Brenda and Erica were not necessarily the only intended victims**.

The other two people named in the alleged revelation were also targeted.

# **The Escape to Nevada**

The group reached Wendover, Nevada.

They rented a small kitchenette apartment, cleaned themselves up and spent the night.

The two other men eventually separated from the brothers.

Carnes and Knapp later became important witnesses in the prosecution.

The Lafferty brothers continued traveling.

# **The Arrests**

Charles Carnes and Ricky Knapp were arrested on **July 30, 1984**, after leaving the Lafferty brothers.

Ron and Dan remained fugitives.

They were eventually arrested in **Reno, Nevada, on August 17, 1984**, by FBI agents.

The murders had occurred less than a month earlier.

# **The Evidence Against Ron and Dan**

The prosecution had several important categories of evidence.

### **Witness testimony**

Charles Carnes and Ricky Knapp traveled with the brothers on the day of the murders.

Carnes later testified that Ron admitted killing Brenda.

He also testified that Ron thanked Dan for killing Erica.

### **The written revelation**

Police discovered the document containing Ron's alleged "removal revelation."

It specifically named Brenda, Erica, Chloe Low and Richard Stowe.

The document became one of the most important pieces of evidence demonstrating Ron's motive and state of mind.

### **Physical evidence**

Investigators recovered and examined weapons, clothing and other evidence.

During the 1996 retrial, crime-lab testimony connected physical evidence to the defendants and their movements.

### **Statements**

Statements attributed to Ron and Dan were also used during the proceedings.

The courts spent years examining whether particular statements and evidence had been properly admitted.

# **The Competency and Mental-Health Question**

Ron Lafferty's mental state became one of the most complicated parts of the case.

Before trial, multiple psychiatric evaluations were conducted.

In 1984, court-appointed experts initially found Ron competent to proceed.

Then, on **December 29, 1984**, Ron attempted suicide by hanging while in jail.

He was found unconscious and without a heartbeat but was resuscitated.

The incident raised concerns about possible brain damage and his ability to participate in his defense.

In January 1985, a judge found him incompetent at that time and ordered continued treatment.

Later evaluations found improvement.

Ron was ultimately found competent to stand trial.

The mental-health issue did not end there.

It eventually became the reason Ron's original conviction was overturned years later.

# **Dan Lafferty's Trial**

Dan was tried separately from Ron.

His trial took place in **January 1985**.

Dan largely represented himself, with appointed attorneys available in an advisory/standby role.

He was convicted on all six charges.

Those charges included:

* Two counts of capital murder
* Two counts of aggravated burglary
* Two counts of conspiracy to commit murder

The jury could not unanimously agree on the death penalty.

Under Utah law at the time, that meant Dan received life imprisonment rather than death.

He received **two life sentences without parole**, to be served concurrently.

# **Why Dan Was Not Sentenced to Death**

The jury's decision is one of the unusual aspects of the case.

Dan was convicted of the murders but did not receive the death penalty.

A juror later indicated that some jurors considered his religious beliefs and other mitigating circumstances when deciding against death.

This does **not** mean the jury accepted that God had ordered the murders.

It means the jury considered the circumstances relevant to sentencing and could not reach the unanimous result required for execution.

# **Ron Lafferty's First Trial**

Ron was tried later.

His trial ran from **April 25 through May 7, 1985**.

He was convicted of:

* Two counts of first-degree murder
* Two counts of aggravated burglary
* Two counts of conspiracy to commit first-degree murder

He was sentenced to death for the two murder convictions.

The Utah Supreme Court later affirmed the convictions and sentences in **1988**.

# **Why Ron's First Conviction Was Overturned**

Ron appealed his conviction through the courts.

The critical issue eventually became **competency to stand trial**.

In **1991**, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit ruled that the trial court had used the wrong legal standard when evaluating Ron's competency.

The court concluded that it was not enough for Ron to have a basic factual understanding of the proceedings.

The question also involved whether he could rationally understand and participate in his defense.

His religious delusions and mental state therefore became central to the appeal.

The convictions and death sentence were vacated, opening the way for a new trial.

# **Ron Was Again Found Incompetent**

After the federal ruling, Ron underwent additional competency proceedings.

In **November 1992**, a judge found him incompetent to assist his attorneys.

Doctors testified concerning religious delusions and his distrust of the judicial system.

Ron was sent for treatment.

Later, his competency was restored and he was eventually cleared to face another trial.

# **The 1996 Retrial**

Ron was retried in **1996**.

This time, his defense included arguments concerning his mental illness and responsibility.

The prosecution again argued that the murders were driven by Ron's anger toward Brenda and others, with the claimed revelation functioning as a justification.

The jury rejected the defense.

On **April 10, 1996**, Ron was again convicted.

He was found guilty of the same basic group of offenses, including the two murders, aggravated burglary and conspiracy.

He was again sentenced to death.

# **The Knife Evidence**

During the retrial, prosecutors presented evidence concerning the knife used in the murders.

A cutlery-store owner testified that Ron had obtained knives from his store earlier in 1984.

A knife recovered along Interstate 80 was examined as part of the physical evidence.

Crime-lab experts also testified about evidence connecting the brothers to the crime.

# **The Disputed Question: Who Actually Killed Brenda?**

There is an important detail that should not be oversimplified.

At trial, **Dan testified that he alone slit Brenda's throat**.

However, Charles Carnes testified that Ron admitted killing Brenda after they left the home.

The courts acknowledged this conflict in the evidence.

Regardless of exactly which brother held the knife at the final moment, both were legally convicted of the murders.

The prosecution's case against Ron included evidence that he had planned the killings, participated in the attack and had directly admitted responsibility according to Carnes' testimony.

# **Ron Lafferty's Death Sentence and Appeals**

After his 1996 conviction, Ron spent the next decades challenging his conviction and sentence.

The case went through numerous state and federal appeals.

The Utah Supreme Court upheld his retrial conviction in **2001**.

The U.S. Supreme Court later denied review.

Ron continued pursuing federal habeas and other legal challenges.

In **August 2019**, the Tenth Circuit rejected his latest attempt to obtain a certificate of appealability, leaving him with very limited remaining legal options.

# **Ron Chose a Firing Squad**

Utah law permitted execution by firing squad under certain circumstances.

Ron selected the firing squad as his preferred method of execution.

At one point, his execution appeared increasingly close.

But it never happened.

# **Ron Lafferty's Death**

On **November 11, 2019**, Ron Lafferty died at **Utah State Prison**.

He was **78 years old**.

He had spent approximately **34 years on death row**.

He died of natural causes before the state could execute him.

His death ended the possibility of carrying out the sentence against him.

# **Dan Lafferty After the Murders**

Dan remained imprisoned under his two concurrent life sentences.

Over the years, Dan gave interviews from prison.

His statements were particularly controversial because he continued to describe the murders through the religious framework that had surrounded the original crime.

In a 2004 interview, he expressed no conventional remorse for the killings and continued to defend his actions according to his beliefs.

As of the most recent reporting I found, Dan remains incarcerated at **Utah State Prison**. A 2025 report about his daughter Rebecca's memoir also described a telephone call from Dan while he was an inmate there.

# **The 2015 Springville Connection**

Dan Lafferty became involved in another controversial Utah case while imprisoned.

He had developed a relationship with **Benjamin and Kristi Strack**, a couple whose family later died in a 2014 mass killing-suicide in Springville, Utah.

In 2015, Dan told the Associated Press that he believed his ideas had influenced the couple.

This did **not** mean Dan was prosecuted for the deaths.

It is relevant because it demonstrated that his radical beliefs continued to influence how he interacted with people from prison decades after the Lafferty murders.

# **What Was the Real Motive?**

There is no single explanation accepted by everyone.

The evidence supports several overlapping factors.

## **Theory 1 — Religious Extremism**

Ron genuinely claimed that God had instructed him to remove Brenda, Erica, Chloe Low and Richard Stowe.

The written revelation exists as evidence that he had articulated this belief before the murders.

Ron and Dan continued to act on the revelation even after other members of the School of the Prophets rejected it.

This makes religious extremism an undeniable part of the case.

**Evidence: Strong.**

# **Theory 2 — Jealousy and Revenge**

This is one of the most important alternative interpretations.

Ron blamed Brenda for encouraging his wife Diana to leave him.

Brenda also opposed the direction Ron and Dan were taking.

The prosecution argued that the religious revelation was partly a justification for a deeply personal act of revenge.

Brenda's sister Sharon Weeks has strongly supported this interpretation, arguing that the real story was more about jealousy, anger and revenge than religion.

**Evidence: Strong circumstantial support.**

# **Theory 3 — Mental Illness and Religious Delusions**

Ron underwent multiple psychiatric evaluations.

His competency became a major legal issue.

Doctors disagreed at different stages about his mental state and ability to assist his attorneys.

The Tenth Circuit ultimately found that the original court had applied an incorrect competency standard.

However, the legal system did not ultimately find Ron legally insane in a way that prevented his second conviction.

His 1996 jury rejected the relevant defense arguments.

**Evidence: Strong evidence that mental-health issues existed; their exact role in causing the murders remains debated.**

# **Theory 4 — The "Revelation" Was a Justification**

Another interpretation is that Ron's religious language was not the sole cause of the murders.

Instead, he may have used his claimed revelation to give religious legitimacy to personal anger.

This interpretation fits the prosecution's argument that Brenda was targeted because Ron blamed her for his divorce.

It also fits the fact that the revelation included people who Ron personally associated with his divorce and excommunication.

**Evidence: Strong circumstantial theory, but the exact psychological motive cannot be proven with certainty.**

# **Theory 5 — The Brothers Were Acting From Genuine Belief**

The opposite interpretation is that Ron and Dan genuinely believed their actions were required by divine revelation.

Their statements, the written revelation and their later comments provide evidence that they did not view the murders in the same moral framework as ordinary criminals.

Dan continued to express versions of this belief years later.

This does not make the belief objectively true; it explains the ideological framework through which the brothers understood their actions.

# **What Did the School of the Prophets Think?**

This is another important detail often lost in popular retellings.

The entire group did **not** simply approve of Ron's revelation.

Other members rejected the alleged revelation as not being from God.

The dispute became so serious that the group broke apart.

Only Ron and Dan, along with limited support from others, continued to believe that the "removal" had to occur.

Therefore, it is inaccurate to say:

**"The School of the Prophets ordered the murders."**

The more accurate statement is:

**Ron claimed to receive a revelation and Ron and Dan acted on it after other members of the group rejected it.**

# **What Did Brenda's Family Think?**

Brenda's family has spent decades trying to preserve her memory.

Her sister Sharon Wright Weeks has repeatedly criticized portrayals that focus almost entirely on the murderers and their religious beliefs.

She has argued that Brenda was a vibrant young woman with ambitions, friends, a career goal and a strong faith.

Weeks also believes that the popular narrative sometimes turns Brenda into merely a character in a story about Ron and Dan.

That is why, for a responsible website, it is better to dedicate a substantial section to **who Brenda actually was** rather than treating her only as a murder victim.

# **The Death Penalty Debate**

The case also became important in Utah's debate over capital punishment.

Sharon Weeks originally wanted Ron executed.

But after decades of appeals and repeated court proceedings, her position changed.

She eventually became an advocate for eliminating Utah's death penalty.

Her argument was that decades of death-row litigation created another form of prolonged suffering for victims' families.

Ron ultimately died naturally before his sentence was carried out.

Weeks has said that she accepted the jury's decisions but no longer supported the death penalty itself.

The Lafferty murders later became famous outside Utah through **Jon Krakauer's 2003 true-crime book *Under the Banner of Heaven***.

The book used the Lafferty case as a central narrative while also examining the history of Mormon fundamentalism and violence.

In **2022**, the book was adapted into an FX/Hulu miniseries starring Andrew Garfield.

# **Why This Case Remains So Disturbing**

The Lafferty case is not simply a story about two criminals.

It is a story about how **personal resentment, family conflict, extremist ideology, claimed divine authority and mental-health issues became intertwined**.

Ron transformed his anger toward his former wife, Brenda and other people into what he described as a religious mission.

Dan joined him.

The result was the murder of a 24-year-old woman and her 15-month-old daughter.

The other intended victims survived because circumstances intervened.

Ron eventually spent more than three decades waiting for an execution that never happened.

Dan remained imprisoned.

And Brenda and Erica's family spent decades dealing with the consequences.

The case became famous through books and television, but the most important fact is much simpler:

**Brenda Wright Lafferty and her daughter Erica were real people whose lives ended on July 24, 1984.**

The murders were solved, the perpetrators were convicted, and the court record provides a substantial factual account. What remains debated is how much weight should be given to religion, personal revenge, psychological illness and Ron's claimed revelations when explaining why the crime happened.
