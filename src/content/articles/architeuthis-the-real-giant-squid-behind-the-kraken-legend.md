# **An Animal Once Mistaken for a Sea Monster**

For centuries, stories of enormous creatures living beneath the ocean were part of maritime folklore.

Sailors described strange shapes, enormous tentacles and creatures that seemed almost impossible to explain. Some of these stories became associated with the legendary **Kraken**, a gigantic sea monster said to inhabit the waters of northern Europe.

The truth turned out to be considerably more interesting.

There really is a giant cephalopod living in the deep ocean.

It is the **giant squid**, belonging to the genus *Architeuthis*.

Modern taxonomy recognizes *Architeuthis dux* as the accepted species. Genetic research involving specimens from different parts of the world found remarkably little mitochondrial variation, supporting the interpretation that giant squid belong to a single globally distributed species.

The animal is not a mythical monster and there is no evidence that it attacks or sinks ships.

Instead, it is an elusive deep-sea predator that spends most of its life in an environment that is extremely difficult for humans to observe.

That fact explains much of the mystery surrounding it.

# **What Is the Giant Squid?**

The giant squid is a member of the cephalopod group, which includes squid, octopuses, cuttlefish and nautiluses.

Its scientific name is generally given as ***Architeuthis dux***.

It possesses:

* Eight arms
* Two exceptionally long feeding tentacles
* A large mantle
* A powerful beak
* Large eyes
* A funnel used for jet propulsion
* Numerous suckers on its arms and tentacles
* A flexible internal structure called a gladius

The two long tentacles are particularly important.

They can extend far beyond the shorter arms and are used to capture prey.

The giant squid also has extraordinarily large eyes. One Smithsonian specimen's eye is roughly the size of a soccer ball, making it one of the most remarkable visual organs known in the animal kingdom.

These huge eyes are believed to be useful in the almost completely dark environment where giant squid live.

# **How Large Is a Giant Squid?**

The exact maximum size of the species is still debated because measurements of stranded and damaged specimens can be difficult.

However, scientists have confirmed individuals measuring around **13 metres (43 feet)** in total length. Smithsonian identifies nearly 43 feet as the largest giant squid recorded by scientists.

Another important point is that giant squid length includes their extremely long feeding tentacles.

This means a 13-metre squid does not have a 13-metre body.

The mantle and shorter arms make up a much smaller portion of the total length.

The female is generally larger than the male.

For example, the female giant squid displayed at the Smithsonian was approximately **11 metres long when alive**, although preservation caused the specimen to shrink considerably.

A giant squid specimen preserved at the Natural History Museum in London measures approximately **8.62 metres** and was collected near the Falkland Islands in 2004.

# **Where Do Giant Squid Live?**

Giant squid are deep-ocean animals.

They have been found in oceans around the world, and their remains have been recovered from many different regions.

They are particularly associated with deep-water environments.

Scientists believe they spend much of their lives hundreds to thousands of metres below the surface.

This deep-water lifestyle is one of the main reasons they remained mysterious for so long.

At depths where giant squid are found, there is little or no sunlight. Temperatures are low, pressure is enormous and conventional underwater cameras can disturb animals with bright artificial lighting.

Australian Museum information places giant squid within pelagic and bathypelagic ocean environments, including depths from hundreds to more than 2,000 metres.

Long before scientists understood giant squid, enormous marine creatures appeared in folklore.

# **The Nineteenth-Century Turning Point**

The nineteenth century changed the giant squid story.

Naturalists began receiving actual remains of enormous squid.

These specimens allowed scientists to move from folklore toward zoology.

Danish zoologist **Japetus Steenstrup** played a major role in recognizing the giant squid as a genuine animal.

The genus *Architeuthis* dates to the nineteenth century, and *Architeuthis dux* remains the accepted species name in modern taxonomic databases.

But the most important breakthrough was still to come.

Scientists needed a specimen that could be properly examined and photographed.

# **The Newfoundland Giant Squid of the 1870s**

In Newfoundland, Canada, fishermen encountered giant squid remains during the 1870s.

One of the most important figures in this story was **Reverend Moses Harvey**, a Newfoundland naturalist and collector.

In October 1873, Harvey obtained a large portion of a giant squid.

The discovery was significant because it provided scientists with physical evidence of an animal that had previously seemed almost legendary.

A more complete specimen was subsequently obtained from **Logy Bay** near St. John's.

Harvey preserved the specimen and allowed it to be studied and photographed.

This became one of the defining moments in the scientific history of the giant squid.

# **The First Photograph of a Giant Squid**

One of the most remarkable images in marine natural history dates from the 1870s.

A giant squid specimen was photographed in Reverend Moses Harvey's home.

The famous image shows the squid draped across a bathtub.

It looks unusual by modern standards, but the historical importance of the photograph is enormous.

Smithsonian identifies the 1874 photograph as the **first photograph ever taken of a giant squid**.

The original photograph has become one of the most famous pieces of giant-squid history.

A surviving version of the photograph is available through Wikimedia Commons and is identified there as a public-domain image.

The photograph helped transform the giant squid from something resembling a sailor's monster into an identifiable zoological animal.

# **The Alecton Encounter**

Another important nineteenth-century episode occurred in 1861.

The French naval vessel **Alecton** encountered a large squid in the Atlantic.

The crew attempted to capture it.

The event became an important early example of people encountering a giant squid alive rather than simply finding a stranded carcass.

The encounter also contributed to the growing scientific interest in enormous squid.

Historical illustrations of the Alecton encounter remain part of the giant squid's scientific history.

# **Why Did Scientists Know So Little About It?**

This is one of the most fascinating parts of the story.

Scientists therefore had to rely heavily on dead specimens.

Many discoveries came from squid that had washed ashore or from remains found inside the stomachs of sperm whales.

Sperm whales are important natural predators of giant squid, and their stomach contents have provided scientists with evidence that the two animals regularly interact in the deep ocean.

# **The Giant Squid and the Sperm Whale**

The relationship between sperm whales and giant squid is one of the most important clues to giant-squid biology.

Sperm whales are capable of making deep dives and are known to feed on giant squid.

Researchers have found large numbers of giant squid beaks in sperm-whale stomachs.

The hard beak is particularly useful to scientists because soft squid tissue is much more likely to disappear during digestion.

This means a sperm whale can effectively become a biological record of what giant squid are living in an area.

It also demonstrates that giant squid are not simply passive deep-sea animals.

They are active predators themselves.

# **The First Live Giant Squid Photograph**

For most of the twentieth century, scientists still had never photographed a living giant squid in its natural environment.

That finally changed in the early 2000s.

Japanese scientists **Tsunemi Kubodera and Kyoichi Mori** developed a method for attracting giant squid with bait.

In 2004, they obtained important photographic evidence.

The work eventually led to publication of photographs and DNA evidence identifying the animal as *Architeuthis dux*. Smithsonian reports that the DNA barcode obtained from a tentacle confirmed the animal's identity.

This was a major scientific milestone.

For the first time, researchers were not simply looking at a dead squid.

They were beginning to understand how the animal behaved.

# **The 2006 Live Giant Squid Encounter**

In 2006, Kubodera and his colleagues made another historic breakthrough near Japan's Ogasawara Islands.

Researchers used bait suspended beneath a research vessel.

A giant squid attacked the bait.

The scientists succeeded in bringing the animal toward the surface and filming it alive.

The squid was approximately **7 metres long**.

Smithsonian describes this as the first time a giant squid was filmed alive.

The footage was extremely important because it demonstrated that the animal was not merely a strange deep-sea carcass.

It was an active predator capable of attacking prey.

# **The 2012 Breakthrough**

The greatest breakthrough came in **2012**.

A Japanese research team, working with broadcaster NHK and the Discovery Channel, succeeded in filming a giant squid **in its natural deep-sea environment**.

The expedition used a specially designed submersible and an optical lure.

Instead of flooding the darkness with powerful white lights, the researchers used a system designed to minimize disturbance.

The lure imitated the type of bioluminescent signal produced by deep-sea organisms.

The method worked.

The giant squid approached the equipment.

For the first time, humans obtained moving images of a giant squid behaving in its natural environment.

Smithsonian describes the 2012 footage as the first video of a living giant squid in its natural habitat.

# **What Did the 2012 Footage Reveal?**

The footage changed some assumptions about the animal.

The squid was not simply drifting motionless through the darkness.

It actively approached the lure and interacted with it.

Researchers observed an animal capable of moving deliberately through the deep water and using its long tentacles to investigate potential prey.

The discovery also showed why traditional deep-sea exploration methods had struggled.

Bright lights and noisy equipment can potentially alter the behavior of animals adapted to almost complete darkness.

The 2012 expedition demonstrated that **stealthy observation** could be much more effective.

# **The 2019 Giant Squid in the Gulf of Mexico**

The story did not end in Japan.

In 2019, scientists working in the Gulf of Mexico used the **Medusa** deep-sea camera system to search for giant squid.

Medusa was designed to operate with very low visible-light disturbance.

It uses dim red illumination and sensitive cameras, making it much less intrusive than conventional systems.

After several deployments, researchers succeeded in recording a giant squid approximately **100 miles southeast of New Orleans**.

The animal was estimated to be around **10 to 12 feet long**.

The footage represented the first confirmed filming of a living giant squid in American waters.

This was particularly significant because thousands of conventional deep-sea dives had failed to produce comparable observations.

The success reinforced the idea that the way researchers search for deep-sea animals can be just as important as the technology they use.

# **Are There Multiple Species of Giant Squid?**

For many years, scientists believed that several different giant-squid species might exist.

The problem was understandable.

Giant squid were found in widely separated oceans, and individual specimens varied in appearance and size.

Scientists gave different specimens different scientific names.

Genetic research later provided an important clue.

A 2013 study examined mitochondrial genomes from **43 Architeuthis samples** collected across the known range.

Researchers found extremely low genetic variation and no detectable mitochondrial population structure.

The findings supported the conclusion that the world's giant squid may represent a single species, ***Architeuthis dux***.

Modern World Register of Marine Species taxonomy accepts *Architeuthis dux* as the species name and treats numerous other historical names as synonyms.

This does not mean every taxonomic question about the animal has disappeared, but it represents a major change from the complicated naming system of earlier decades.

Scientists have confirmed an animal capable of reaching enormous lengths, possessing extraordinary eyes and living in an environment that remains difficult for humans to observe.

The giant squid is therefore not a mythical monster.

It is something more scientifically interesting: **a real animal that spent centuries hiding in one of the least accessible habitats on Earth.**

And despite more than a century of scientific investigation, much of its life story remains unwritten.
