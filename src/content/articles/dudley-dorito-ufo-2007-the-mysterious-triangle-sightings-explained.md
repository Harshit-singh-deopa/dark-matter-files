## **Quick Facts**

# **What Was the Dudley Dorito?**

The **Dudley Dorito** is the nickname given to a collection of UFO reports from the **West Midlands of England**, particularly the Black Country.

The nickname came from descriptions of a large triangular or arrowhead-shaped object that supposedly resembled a triangular corn snack.

The reports became especially prominent in **late November and December 2007**, when local newspaper *Express & Star* began receiving large numbers of calls from people claiming to have seen an unusual object.

Witnesses described combinations of:

* A large triangular or arrowhead-shaped silhouette
* Three prominent lights
* Orange or reddish lights
* White lights in some later reports
* Little or no apparent sound
* Slow movement
* Hovering
* In some accounts, sudden acceleration

The important thing, however, is that **the evidence does not establish that all of these reports were sightings of one physical craft**.

The term "Dudley Dorito" was created by the media to describe similar-looking reports. Once the nickname became known, later witnesses could naturally compare what they saw with earlier stories.

That makes the case fascinating—but also considerably more complicated than the popular version suggests.

# **The First Major Sighting — 28 November 2007**

The central event in the story occurred on **Wednesday, 28 November 2007**.

According to contemporary reporting, UFO Research Midlands (UFORM), a Stourbridge-based UFO group, received reports of a large triangular object moving across the area.

One of the principal descriptions placed the object around **Halesowen**, travelling in the direction of **Stourbridge**, at approximately **7 p.m.**

The object was described as resembling a huge triangular "Dorito."

One particularly interesting claim came from UFORM chairman **Steve Poole**, who said that a witness had filmed more than 30 seconds of the object.

According to the contemporary report, the object was described as having distinctive reddish lights underneath it, with the lights apparently changing position as it travelled.

Another person reportedly watched the object through binoculars from the **Lickey Hills** in the direction of Sutton Coldfield/Birmingham.

UFORM also said two additional people had contacted them within hours of the sighting.

This is where the case began attracting serious local attention.

# **The Newspaper Appeal**

The *Express & Star* published an appeal asking other people who had seen the object to come forward.

The response was substantial.

The newspaper reported that people from different parts of the Black Country contacted both the newspaper and UFORM.

This produced several additional witness accounts.

However, there is an important distinction:

**These reports should not automatically be treated as independent observations of one object.**

Some people reported seeing something at different times and from different locations.

The newspaper publicity also occurred before many of the later reports.

Therefore, while the number of witnesses is interesting, it cannot by itself prove that one enormous triangular craft travelled across the region.

# **Katherine Hemmings and Two Friends**

One of the witnesses identified in the *Express & Star* was **Katherine Hemmings**, who was 13 at the time.

She said she and her friends **Tasmin Jones and Chelsea Smith** were walking home near Wednesfield High School when they suddenly turned around and noticed something unusual in the sky.

Their reported sighting was around **4 p.m.**

They described seeing a large object with lights.

Katherine reportedly said that the object made no noticeable noise.

What makes her account interesting is that she reportedly said she had previously been sceptical about UFO sightings and normally assumed unusual objects were likely to be aircraft or birds.

The three girls initially told their families rather than the newspaper.

Their story became public after the newspaper began covering the wider sightings.

# **Pat and Mick Scotford — Wollaston**

Another reported sighting came from **Pat Scotford and her husband Mick**.

They were in their garden on Devon Road in **Wollaston** at approximately **7:30 p.m.**

Pat reportedly described seeing **three orange lights**.

Her first thought was apparently that they could be three aircraft flying in formation.

However, she concluded that the lights seemed too close together.

She believed the object was moving towards the direction of **Kinver**.

Again, this was a witness account rather than an independently verified identification of a physical craft.

# **Lisa Timmins — Bushbury**

Another person identified by the newspaper was **Lisa Timmins**, a 38-year-old driver.

She reportedly saw the object while at her boyfriend's home on Whitgreave Avenue in **Bushbury**.

Her reported viewing time was between approximately **6:15 and 6:40 p.m.**

Unlike some of the more detailed witnesses, Timmins reportedly did not claim to know what she had seen.

Her description was simply that it appeared to be quite large.

That uncertainty is important.

A UFO report does not necessarily mean the witness believed they had seen an extraterrestrial spacecraft. It simply means the object was not identified by the observer.

# **What Did the Object Actually Look Like?**

Descriptions varied.

The most common description was a **triangle or arrowhead**.

Some witnesses described something resembling an isosceles triangle rather than a perfect equilateral triangle.

Reported lighting arrangements included:

* Three orange lights
* Orange/red lights
* White lights
* Lights positioned around the edges
* A reddish/orange central light in some later accounts

The shape was sometimes described as:

**“Dorito-shaped”**

or

**“arrowhead-shaped.”**

That variation matters.

A perfectly solid triangular aircraft would normally be easier to describe consistently than a distant collection of lights against a dark sky.

It is therefore possible that some witnesses were seeing an actual structured object, while others may have been interpreting separated lights as the outline of a triangle.

# **The Claimed 30-Second Video**

One of the most frequently repeated elements of the original story is the claim that a witness filmed the object for **more than 30 seconds**.

This was reported by UFORM chairman Steve Poole in the contemporary *Express & Star* coverage.

However, there is a major evidence problem.

The **original camera file has not been publicly established with a clear chain of custody and independently verifiable metadata**.

Later videos and clips associated with the Dudley Dorito circulate online, but that does not automatically make them the original 2007 recording.

This distinction is crucial.

A video appearing on YouTube years later is not automatically evidence that:

1. It was recorded in 2007.
2. It was recorded in Dudley.
3. It depicts the original event.
4. It has not been edited.
5. It shows an extraordinary aircraft.

So the safest conclusion is:

**A video was claimed to exist, but the original recording cannot currently be treated as publicly authenticated evidence.**

# **December 2007 — More Witnesses Come Forward**

The story expanded rapidly after the initial reports.

The *Express & Star* reported that people in **Wednesfield and Dudley** had contacted the newspaper following its appeal.

The newspaper described the object as an orange triangle with three lights.

The number of people contacting the paper was described as being in the dozens or even scores.

However, these numbers refer to people who **reported seeing something after the newspaper appeal**, not to a scientifically controlled simultaneous observation.

That distinction is often lost when the case is retold online.

# **The Lower Gornal Sighting**

Another important report came shortly afterward from **Lower Gornal**.

A later report published through Birmingham UFO Group reproduced the *Express & Star* account of a sighting around **Wallows Wood**, off The Straits.

Residents reportedly saw an object around **6:15 p.m.**

It was described as an arrowhead with **three orange lights arranged in an isosceles triangle**.

The witnesses reportedly watched it moving slowly before it disappeared from view in the direction of **Wolverhampton**.

One witness, **Peter Wasdell**, said that his wife saw the object after their dog began barking.

He then came outside and watched it with other family members and neighbours.

The reported observation lasted approximately **15 minutes**.

# **Craig Lowe's Video**

UFO investigator **Craig Lowe**, associated with the Birmingham UFO Group, said he had captured the December sighting on videotape.

Interestingly, Lowe did not necessarily believe the object was extraterrestrial.

He reportedly suggested that it might have been **some form of military aircraft**.

That is an important detail because the military-aircraft theory did not originate only from sceptics.

Some UFO investigators themselves considered secret or experimental aviation technology as a possible explanation.

# **January 2008 — The Mystery Continues**

The reports did not end with 2007.

In January 2008, another sighting was reported in **Lower Gornal**.

Two young girls, **Chelsea and Casey Bridges**, reportedly saw an unusual object from their grandmother's home on the Priory estate in Dudley.

They were reportedly in bed when they noticed something in the sky in the direction of **Dudley Castle**.

The girls reportedly became frightened and went downstairs.

Their grandmother, **Marie Bridges**, asked them to draw what they had seen.

The drawings reportedly showed a diamond/triangular object with lights.

The girls' descriptions were not identical:

* One reportedly described lights at the corners.
* The other described lights around the object.

That difference is another reason the case should be treated as witness testimony rather than precise technical measurement.

# **Why the Drawings Were Interesting**

The drawings attracted attention because the children apparently produced similar general shapes independently.

However, drawings made after a sighting are still influenced by memory.

The witnesses had also been exposed to considerable media coverage of the "Dudley Dorito" by this point.

Therefore, the drawings are interesting historical evidence of what the children remembered—but they cannot establish the physical geometry of an unknown aircraft.

# **The Birmingham Airport Connection**

The reports attracted enough attention that **Birmingham International Airport** was contacted.

Airport spokesman **John Morris** reportedly said the airport took UFO reports seriously and acknowledged that it had received inquiries following the earlier sightings over Halesowen.

This does **not** mean Birmingham Airport confirmed that a UFO had been detected.

It means the airport was aware that members of the public were reporting unusual objects.

That is an important distinction.

# **Was the Dudley Dorito Recorded by the British Government?**

This is one of the most interesting parts of the case.

The British **Ministry of Defence** maintained a public database of UFO reports.

The government's published **2007 UFO report** contains numerous sightings from across Britain, including several triangular objects and unusual lights.

However, the published 2007 list does **not contain an obvious entry corresponding to the central 28 November Dudley/Halesowen/Stourbridge report**.

That does not prove that the government knew nothing about it.

It simply means that the publicly released MOD annual list does not provide a matching entry that establishes an official investigation of the Dudley Dorito.

The UK's National Archives explains that MOD UFO records generally consisted of reports, correspondence and policy material, and that the purpose of military handling was primarily to determine whether sightings had possible defence significance—not to establish extraterrestrial visitation.

# **Did the MOD Investigate the Dudley Dorito?**

There is no publicly available evidence establishing that the MOD carried out a dedicated investigation into the Dudley Dorito as a single unidentified aircraft.

This is important because some internet versions of the story imply that the British government secretly investigated the craft.

The available public record does not support such a confident statement.

The MOD's UFO system recorded many reports during this period.

But **a UFO being reported to authorities was not equivalent to an official investigation proving that the object was an extraordinary craft.**

# **What Does the Official 2007 MOD Database Tell Us?**

The MOD database provides useful context.

During 2007, Britain received many reports involving:

* Triangular objects
* Orange lights
* Red lights
* Spheres
* Discs
* Strange formations
* Objects appearing to hover
* Objects apparently moving unusually

For example, the published database includes a **28 October 2007 report near Rotherham** involving a bright triangular object with one flashing strobe and numerous fixed lights.

There were also numerous reports involving orange and red lights later in the year.

This demonstrates that triangular UFO reports were not unique to Dudley.

# **The 2011 Dudley Dorito Sighting**

One of the most interesting later reports occurred in **January 2011**.

Security officer **Glyn Richards** reportedly saw a triangular object from his garden in **Quarry Bank**.

According to contemporary reporting, the object appeared to be travelling towards Dudley from the direction of Halesowen.

Richards reportedly described:

* A triangular shape
* White lights
* Red flashing lights toward the centre
* Approximately 20 seconds of visibility

The extraordinary part of his story was that he claimed a **second object** appeared.

He reportedly described the second object as having a bright white light and said it appeared to chase the first object away.

The first object then reportedly disappeared rapidly in a straight line.

Richards reportedly said he had not previously been a strong believer in UFOs and considered the possibility that it was some form of military aircraft.

# **The 2011 Hagley Sighting**

Another important later report occurred in **December 2011** near **Hagley**.

Property worker **Gary Nock** and members of his family reportedly saw a dark triangular object from the car park of the Badgers Sett pub.

The reported sighting occurred at approximately **6:40 p.m.**

The object was described as:

* Black or very dark
* Triangular
* Having bright white lights
* Flying relatively low
* Travelling from the Birmingham direction
* Apparently banking or turning
* Silent

The family reportedly lost sight of it near the **Clent Hills**.

Nock reportedly believed it might have been an experimental military aircraft and compared its appearance with the B-2 stealth bomber, while noting that the object did not appear to make the expected noise.

Again, this was a witness interpretation—not evidence that a secret aircraft was responsible.

# **The Dudley Dorito Was Not Limited to Dudley**

As reports accumulated, similar triangular objects were reported around several parts of the Black Country and surrounding region.

Locations associated with reports included:

* Dudley
* Halesowen
* Stourbridge
* Wednesfield
* Wollaston
* Lower Gornal
* Wolverhampton
* Tipton
* Brierley Hill
* Oldbury
* Quarry Bank
* Hagley
* The Clent Hills

The expanding geographical range is one reason the story became a local UFO legend.

But it also creates an analytical problem.

The farther apart the sightings were in time and location, the harder it becomes to demonstrate that they were caused by the **same physical object**.

# **The Most Important Question: Was It One Object?**

There is currently no solid evidence proving this.

The name "Dudley Dorito" makes the reports sound like one continuing event.

The actual evidence is much messier.

The original reports occurred at different:

* Times
* Locations
* Viewing angles
* Distances
* Weather conditions
* Observation durations

Some witnesses reported three lights.

Others described lights around the entire object.

Some described an orange object.

Others described a black triangle with white lights.

Some reports lasted seconds.

Others reportedly lasted many minutes.

Therefore, the most scientifically cautious description is:

> **The Dudley Dorito was a series of broadly similar UFO reports, not a demonstrably single aircraft repeatedly observed across the region.**

# **Theory 1: A Secret Military Aircraft**

This is probably the most famous alternative explanation.

Several witnesses themselves suggested a military aircraft.

The triangular appearance naturally brings to mind aircraft such as:

* B-2 Spirit
* Experimental aircraft
* Unpublicised military designs
* Advanced reconnaissance aircraft
* Hypothetical black-project aircraft

The theory gained popularity because the United States and other countries have historically developed aircraft whose existence was initially secret.

However, there is a major problem.

The **B-2 Spirit is not a silent aircraft**.

It produces substantial engine noise, particularly at low altitude.

Several Dudley witnesses specifically emphasised the absence of sound.

That doesn't completely rule out an aircraft, because distance, wind, background noise and atmospheric conditions can affect what people hear.

But it makes a conventional low-flying B-2 explanation less convincing.

# **Theory 2: Eurofighter Typhoon**

Another theory that appeared in contemporary discussions was the **Eurofighter Typhoon**.

Its angular design can look triangular from certain angles.

The aircraft was also operational in Britain during the period.

However, the same problem remains:

**A Eurofighter is a powerful jet aircraft and is not silent.**

The witnesses who described an apparently slow-moving or hovering silent triangle would therefore require additional assumptions.

The Eurofighter explanation is possible for some sightings but does not automatically explain all reports.

# **Theory 3: Multiple Aircraft Flying in Formation**

This explanation deserves more attention than it usually receives.

Suppose several aircraft are flying relatively close together.

From the ground at night, their lights can merge into a single apparent shape.

A witness may perceive:

**three lights → triangle → triangular craft**

This is particularly relevant because several witnesses specifically described **three lights** rather than a clearly visible solid fuselage.

Pat Scotford, for example, initially thought she might be seeing three aircraft flying in formation before deciding the lights appeared unusually close together.

This explanation could potentially account for some—but not necessarily all—of the reports.

# **Theory 4: Conventional Aircraft Seen From an Unusual Angle**

Aircraft at night can be surprisingly difficult to identify.

Their landing lights, navigation lights and anti-collision lights can produce unusual visual patterns.

At long distances, the actual aircraft body may disappear against the dark sky while the lights remain visible.

A moving aircraft can therefore appear:

* Larger than expected
* Slower than expected
* Stationary
* Triangular
* Silent

depending on distance and atmospheric conditions.

This is one of the simplest explanations for at least some UFO sightings.

# **Theory 5: Helicopter**

Helicopters are another possible explanation.

A helicopter can hover, move slowly and appear to remain almost stationary.

At sufficient distance, rotor noise can become difficult to hear.

However, several Dudley witnesses specifically described a triangular structure rather than simply a group of lights.

Therefore, a helicopter may explain individual reports but does not necessarily explain the entire collection.

# **Theory 6: Optical Illusion**

Human vision is not a measuring instrument.

At night, the brain has difficulty estimating:

* Distance
* Size
* Speed
* Altitude
* Shape

Bright lights can also create halos and blur.

If three lights are separated by a certain distance, the observer's brain may automatically interpret them as the corners of a single object.

This is particularly important in the Dudley case because the **lights themselves were often more clearly described than the physical body of the supposed craft**.

# **Theory 7: A Large Balloon**

Weather balloons and other lighter-than-air objects have historically caused UFO reports.

A balloon could explain:

* Silent movement
* Slow motion
* Apparent hovering
* Strange illumination

However, a normal balloon does not naturally explain a clearly defined triangular shape.

Wind direction and movement would also need to match the reported trajectories.

So this is possible for individual observations but not a particularly strong universal explanation.

# **Theory 8: The TR-3B**

This is one of the most popular explanations on UFO websites.

The supposed **TR-3B** is often described online as a secret triangular American aircraft using revolutionary propulsion or anti-gravity technology.

The Dudley Dorito is frequently connected to this idea because of its triangular shape and reported lights.

There is, however, a serious evidentiary problem:

**There is no publicly verified evidence demonstrating that the legendary TR-3B exists as the craft described in UFO literature.**

The Dudley sightings therefore cannot be used as proof of the TR-3B.

Claims that the Dudley Dorito was definitely a TR-3B go far beyond the available evidence.

# **Theory 9: Advanced Secret Technology**

This theory is more cautious than the TR-3B claim.

It simply proposes that witnesses may have seen an experimental aircraft unknown to the public at the time.

This is historically possible.

Governments have developed classified aircraft.

But possibility is not evidence.

No publicly available document has established that an experimental aircraft was responsible for the Dudley sightings.

Therefore:

**Secret military aircraft remain a hypothesis, not a demonstrated explanation.**

# **Theory 10: Extraterrestrial Spacecraft**

The most exciting theory is that the Dudley Dorito was an extraterrestrial craft.

Supporters point to:

* The triangular shape
* Apparent silence
* Slow movement
* Reported hovering
* Multiple witnesses
* Reported unusual acceleration
* Similar descriptions across different years

However, none of these characteristics is uniquely extraterrestrial.

Most importantly, there is no verified evidence of:

* Alien occupants
* Extraterrestrial material
* A recovered object
* Biological evidence
* Unambiguous radar data
* Authenticated high-resolution photographs
* An authenticated original video proving extraordinary flight characteristics

Therefore, extraterrestrial origin remains **speculation rather than an established fact**.

# **Theory 11: A Social or Media Effect**

This is one of the most interesting explanations for the *pattern* of sightings rather than for any particular object.

Once the newspaper began calling the object the **"Dudley Dorito"**, the public had a ready-made mental image.

Someone seeing three unusual lights might think:

**"That looks like the Dudley Dorito."**

They might then report it as the same object.

This does not mean witnesses were lying.

It means that human perception and memory are influenced by information.

The media attention could therefore have created a feedback loop:

**Initial sighting → newspaper coverage → public awareness → additional reports → more coverage → more people comparing sightings to the original.**

This is a plausible explanation for why the reports became associated with one named object.

# **Theory 12: Hoax**

A deliberate hoax is always possible in UFO cases.

Some photographs and videos circulating online have questionable provenance.

However, there is no strong evidence demonstrating that the entire Dudley Dorito wave was deliberately fabricated.

There were many individual witnesses who gave accounts to local media.

A hoax would therefore need to explain whether:

* One person fabricated an object
* Multiple people independently fabricated sightings
* Some reports were genuine misidentifications
* Later witnesses were influenced by previous publicity

The available evidence does not establish a coordinated hoax.

# **What About the Photographs?**

This is where the internet version of the Dudley Dorito becomes especially confusing.

Photographs claimed to show the object have circulated for years.

Some have been described as potentially significant UFO evidence.

But there is a major problem:

**The provenance of the famous Dudley Dorito images is not sufficiently documented to treat them as authenticated photographs of an extraordinary aircraft.**

A photograph needs more than an interesting appearance.

Ideally, investigators would want:

* Original file or negative
* Original camera information
* Date and time
* Exact location
* Original photographer
* Unedited copy
* Metadata where available
* Chain of custody
* Independent examination
* Known reference objects for scale
* Atmospheric conditions
* Independent witnesses

The public Dudley Dorito material does not provide this complete evidentiary chain.

# **What About the Video?**

The same problem applies.

A clip circulating online is not automatically the original 2007 recording.

Some later videos associated with the Dudley Dorito are genuine recordings in the sense that somebody really recorded something—but that does not prove what the object was.

One particularly discussed recording is associated with **Jamie Gardiner** and a sighting near **Merry Hill, Dudley**, commonly dated to around 2009.

Descriptions of that footage include a dark triangular object with lights.

But online discussions have gone much further, with some people claiming the footage shows advanced secret aircraft.

Those claims have not been independently established.

# **A Critical Problem With Online Dudley Dorito Images**

A large number of UFO websites reuse the same photographs.

This can create the illusion of independent confirmation.

For example:

Website A publishes a photograph.

Website B copies it.

Website C copies Website B.

Website D cites Website C.

After several years, four websites appear to confirm the same photograph.

But all four may actually trace back to **one original image of uncertain provenance**.

This is why original newspaper archives and primary records are much more useful than random UFO blogs.

# **Did Police Investigate It?**

There is no evidence of a major police investigation into the Dudley Dorito itself.

Years later, West Midlands police UFO-related reports were discussed in the press.

A 2017 *Express & Star* report stated that West Midlands Police had received UFO-related reports in subsequent years, but these were not generally investigated as conventional police cases.

A UFO sighting is normally not a police matter unless there is an associated safety, criminal or public-order issue.

# **Why Didn't the Government Explain It?**

There are several possible reasons.

First, the MOD did not exist to solve every unusual object seen by civilians.

Its primary interest was whether an observation had potential defence significance.

The National Archives explains that British UFO records include public reports and official correspondence, but many sightings were eventually attributed to ordinary things such as aircraft, balloons, satellites or astronomical phenomena.

Second, a report that cannot be identified from a short eyewitness description does not necessarily justify a major investigation.

Third, the central Dudley reports were primarily **civilian eyewitness accounts**, rather than a case involving a clearly documented radar track, aircraft interception or physical evidence.

# **Was There Radar Evidence?**

No publicly available evidence has established a radar track corresponding to the Dudley Dorito.

This is a major difference between the Dudley case and some better-documented aviation UFO cases.

There is no publicly available dataset demonstrating:

* Radar detection
* Object altitude
* Exact velocity
* Radar cross-section
* Acceleration
* Flight path

Therefore, claims about the object travelling at extraordinary speeds cannot be scientifically established from the public evidence.

# **How Many People Actually Saw It?**

This is difficult to determine precisely.

Contemporary reports used phrases such as **"dozens"**, **"scores"** and described large numbers of people contacting newspapers or UFO groups.

However, there is no publicly available scientific witness database containing every caller, exact observation time, location and independent statement.

The safest wording is therefore:

**Numerous people reported sightings, but the exact number of independent witnesses cannot be established from the surviving public evidence.**

This is more accurate than claiming that a specific number of people definitely saw one enormous craft.

# **Why Did So Many People Report Similar Objects?**

There are several possibilities.

### **1. There really was an unusual object**

A large aircraft or other object could have been seen by people across a wide region.

### **2. Several ordinary objects looked similar**

Aircraft lights can create surprisingly similar visual patterns.

### **3. People were influenced by earlier reports**

Once the Dudley Dorito became famous, people had a specific shape in mind.

### **4. A combination occurred**

This may actually be the most reasonable possibility.

Some sightings could have involved conventional aircraft, while others may have remained unexplained.

The entire collection does not have to share one explanation.

# **Why the Silence Matters**

One feature appears repeatedly in witness descriptions:

**silence.**

This is one reason the case became so interesting.

A large aircraft moving relatively slowly at low altitude should normally produce an audible engine or aerodynamic signature.

But sound perception depends heavily on:

* Distance
* Wind direction
* Atmospheric conditions
* Background noise
* Aircraft altitude
* Engine type
* Observer location

Therefore, "silent" should be understood as:

**the witness did not hear an obvious sound.**

It does not prove that the object physically produced no sound.

# **Could It Have Been the B-2 Stealth Bomber?**

The B-2 is frequently brought up because its flying-wing design can appear triangular.

But there is no evidence publicly establishing that a B-2 was responsible for the Dudley reports.

There is also a practical problem.

The B-2 is a large, powerful aircraft.

At close range and low altitude it would not normally be expected to appear completely silent.

So while the B-2 makes an interesting visual comparison, it should not be presented as the confirmed answer.

# **Could It Have Been a Black Project?**

Possibly—but there is no direct evidence.

The phrase "black project" simply means a highly classified programme.

Historically, classified aviation projects have existed.

That makes the idea plausible in principle.

But saying:

**"The object could have been classified technology"**

is very different from saying:

**"The object was a classified aircraft."**

The first is a reasonable hypothesis.

The second requires evidence.

No publicly available evidence currently establishes the second.

# **What UFO Researchers Thought**

UFO researchers generally viewed the reports as interesting because several witnesses described similar triangular geometry and lights.

Some investigators considered the possibility of:

* Secret aircraft
* Advanced military technology
* A genuine unidentified craft
* Multiple related sightings

Craig Lowe, who was involved in the Birmingham UFO Group, reportedly leaned toward a possible military-aircraft explanation for at least one of the sightings.

This is important because not every UFO researcher interpreted the case as extraterrestrial.

# **What Sceptics Thought**

Sceptics generally focused on:

* Aircraft
* Multiple lights
* Perspective
* Night-time perception
* Media influence
* Memory distortion
* Possible hoaxes
* Lack of original evidence

The absence of authenticated radar data and a publicly verifiable original video makes it difficult to establish extraordinary flight characteristics.

From this perspective, the Dudley Dorito may represent several ordinary observations that were grouped together by the media.

# **The Most Interesting Middle Ground**

There is another possibility.

Perhaps some of the reports were ordinary objects.

Perhaps one or two remain genuinely unexplained.

And perhaps the media later combined them into one large UFO story.

That scenario would explain why:

* Some witnesses described three lights.
* Others described a complete triangular craft.
* Some saw orange lights.
* Others saw white lights.
* Some reported seconds.
* Others reported many minutes.
* Different areas produced different descriptions.

The case doesn't necessarily have to be either:

**"everyone saw an alien spaceship"**

or

**"everyone saw an airplane."**

Reality may be considerably more complicated.

# **What Happened After 2007?**

The "Dudley Dorito" name continued to appear in reports across the West Midlands.

Similar reports were associated with areas including Tipton, Brierley Hill and Oldbury.

The 2011 Glyn Richards sighting was particularly notable because he reportedly saw a triangular object followed by another bright object.

The December 2011 Hagley report involving Gary Nock and his family added another account of a dark triangular object with lights.

These later reports helped keep the Dudley Dorito story alive.

However, they should not automatically be treated as proof that the same aircraft had been operating in the area continuously since 2007.

# **What About Later Sightings?**

The Dudley Dorito name has continued to appear in local UFO reporting even after the classic 2007–2011 period.

For example, *Express & Star* reported another alleged triangular object near **Brierley Hill in November 2025**, with the witness comparing it to the historical Dudley Dorito.

But this should be treated as a **separate modern report**, not evidence that the 2007 object was still operating.

# **What We Can Actually Prove**

After separating the documented material from later speculation, the following points are reasonably well supported:

### **1. People really did report unusual objects**

This is documented by contemporary local newspaper coverage.

### **2. Multiple witnesses described triangular or arrowhead-like forms**

Several reports contain broadly similar descriptions.

### **3. Orange/red and white lights were repeatedly mentioned**

This feature appears throughout the reports.

### **4. Some witnesses described the object as silent**

This was a recurring part of the testimony.

### **5. The story was covered by a real local newspaper**

The *Express & Star* published multiple contemporary reports.

### **6. UFORM collected reports**

The Stourbridge-based UFO Research Midlands group was actively involved in gathering witness reports.

### **7. Birmingham Airport was aware of reports**

An airport spokesman acknowledged inquiries following the sightings.

### **8. Similar reports continued afterward**

Reports associated with the nickname continued into later years.

# **What We Cannot Prove**

We cannot currently prove that:

* The object was extraterrestrial.
* It was a TR-3B.
* It was an anti-gravity aircraft.
* It was a secret American aircraft.
* It was a Eurofighter.
* Every witness saw exactly the same object.
* The reported video proves an extraordinary craft.
* The circulating photographs are authenticated original photographs.
* The object travelled at impossible speeds.
* The British government secretly knew exactly what it was.
* The MOD conducted a hidden investigation into the entire case.

These claims go beyond the available evidence.

# **The Evidence Problem**

The biggest weakness of the Dudley Dorito case is not the number of witnesses.

It is the **quality of the surviving physical evidence**.

There is no publicly established:
