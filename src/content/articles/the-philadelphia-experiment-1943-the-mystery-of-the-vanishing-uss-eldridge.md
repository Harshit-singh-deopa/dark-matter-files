# **What Was the Philadelphia Experiment Supposed to Be?**

The alleged Philadelphia Experiment is usually described as a secret U.S. Navy project conducted during **1943**, at the Philadelphia Naval Shipyard.

The supposed objective was extraordinary: to manipulate electromagnetic fields around a ship strongly enough to make it invisible.

Different versions of the story describe the supposed objective in slightly different ways.

Some say the Navy wanted to make the ship invisible to enemy radar.

Others claim the experiment was intended to make the ship completely invisible to the human eye.

The most extreme versions say the experiment eventually caused the ship to disappear physically and reappear approximately 200 miles away in **Norfolk, Virginia**.

The alleged ship was the **USS Eldridge (DE-173)**.

The Navy's historical page describes the claim directly: the alleged experiment supposedly made a destroyer invisible and teleported it between Philadelphia and Norfolk. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

There is no official evidence that this happened.

# **The Real USS Eldridge**

The USS Eldridge was not fictional.

She was a real U.S. Navy **Cannon-class destroyer escort**.

The ship was named after Lieutenant Commander **John Eldridge Jr.**, who had been killed during the Battle of the Atlantic.

The Eldridge was launched on **July 25, 1943**, at the Federal Shipbuilding and Dry Dock Company in Newark, New Jersey.

She was commissioned into the U.S. Navy on **August 27, 1943**.

Lieutenant C. R. Hamilton, USNR, was her first commanding officer. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/us-navy-ships/alphabetical-listing/e/uss-eldridge--de-173-0.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The ship subsequently performed genuine wartime duties.

The Naval History and Heritage Command records that Eldridge conducted training and later escorted convoys carrying Allied personnel and supplies across the Atlantic and Mediterranean routes. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/us-navy-ships/alphabetical-listing/e/uss-eldridge--de-173-0.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This real ship is central to the mystery.

# **The Degaussing Theory**

One of the most interesting explanations for the origin of the Philadelphia Experiment involves a real naval technology called **degaussing**.

Degaussing was used by navies during World War II to reduce a ship's magnetic signature.

Why?

Because magnetic mines could detect the magnetic field produced by a large steel vessel.

A ship could therefore be equipped with electrical cables running around its hull.

An electrical current could then be passed through the cables to counteract the ship's magnetic field.

This was real technology.

The U.S. Navy confirms that degaussing equipment was installed on naval ships and used to protect them from magnetic mines. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# **Did Degaussing Make Ships Invisible?**

In a very limited sense, yes—but not in the way the legend claims.

Degaussing could make a ship **less detectable to magnetic mines**.

It did not make the vessel disappear visually.

It did not make it invisible to ordinary radar.

It did not make it disappear from human observers.

It certainly did not teleport it to another city.

The Navy specifically explains this distinction:

Degaussing could make a ship effectively "invisible" to magnetic mine sensors, but the ship would remain visible to people and other detection systems. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This is one of the most plausible explanations for how a real naval technology could eventually become transformed into an extraordinary story.
