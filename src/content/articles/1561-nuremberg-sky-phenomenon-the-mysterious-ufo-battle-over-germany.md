# **What Happened Over Nuremberg?**

At dawn on **14 April 1561**, people in and around Nuremberg reportedly witnessed an extraordinary display in the sky.

The event was recorded by **Hans Glaser**, a Nuremberg printer and letter-painter, in an illustrated single-sheet publication known as a **broadsheet or Flugblatt**.

According to Glaser's report, the phenomenon appeared between approximately **4 and 5 in the morning**.

The sky around the rising Sun reportedly contained numerous strange shapes.

Glaser described:

* Blood-red semicircular arcs
* Spheres or globes
* Black and reddish objects
* Cross-shaped forms
* Long rods
* Cylindrical or tubular forms
* Crescent-shaped objects
* A very large black spear-like object

The objects were described as moving toward and away from the Sun and apparently fighting one another for **more than an hour**.

# **Who Was Hans Glaser?**

**Hans Glaser** was a Nuremberg printer, publisher and letter-painter.

His name appears at the end of the surviving document.

The archive catalogs the item as:

**"Himmelserscheinung über Nürnberg vom 14. April 1561"**

which can be translated approximately as:

**"Celestial phenomenon over Nuremberg of 14 April 1561."**

The surviving sheet is a **colored woodcut with printed text**, measuring approximately **26.2 × 38 cm**.

It was produced in Nuremberg in 1561 and later became part of the collection of **Johann Jakob Wick**, a Zurich clergyman who collected reports of unusual events. It is now held by the Zentralbibliothek Zürich.

# **What Did the Original Broadsheet Say?**

The surviving account says that at daybreak, between approximately 4 and 5 a.m., a frightening apparition appeared around the Sun.

It says that many people in Nuremberg, outside the city gates and in the surrounding countryside, saw it.

The first phenomenon was described as **two blood-red semicircular arcs**, resembling the Moon in its last-quarter phase.

Glaser then described a dark or blackish spherical object near the Sun.

Around the Sun, he described numerous additional red and other-colored globes.

Between them were red crosses and elongated red forms resembling rods.

Some of the rods contained several globes.

# **The Strange "Battle"**

According to the account, the objects then appeared to move against one another.

Glaser wrote that globes initially near the Sun moved toward objects on either side.

Other globes moved back toward the Sun.

The objects apparently moved back and forth and fought with one another.

The reported conflict supposedly lasted **more than an hour**.

This is the source of the modern description:

### **"The Nuremberg UFO Battle."**

However, the word **"UFO" did not exist in 1561**.

That term is a modern interpretation imposed on the historical account.

The original writer was describing what he considered an extraordinary **celestial sign**, not extraterrestrial spacecraft.

# **The Objects Apparently Fell From the Sky**

The most dramatic part of Glaser's report comes next.

According to the broadsheet, after the apparent conflict became extremely intense, the objects seemed to become exhausted.

They then supposedly fell downward from the region of the Sun.

Glaser compared them to objects that appeared to burn.

He reported that they disappeared on or near the ground amid **large amounts of smoke**.

After this, he described another strange object:

### **A huge black spear.**

The spear was described as:

* Very long
* Very thick
* Pointing east with its shaft
* Pointing west with its tip

Glaser then essentially admitted that the meaning of these signs was unknown, saying that **God alone knew what they meant**.
