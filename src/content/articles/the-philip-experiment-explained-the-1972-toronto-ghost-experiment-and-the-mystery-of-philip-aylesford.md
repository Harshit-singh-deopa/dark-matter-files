# **What Was the Philip Experiment?**

The Philip Experiment was an attempt to investigate one of the oldest questions in psychical research:

**Can people produce apparently paranormal phenomena simply through belief, expectation and concentration?**

Traditional séances usually involve a supposed deceased person.

The Toronto researchers deliberately removed that element.

They decided to invent a person who had **never existed**.

If a group could somehow communicate with this fictional character and produce physical phenomena associated with him, they reasoned, it might provide an interesting challenge to conventional theories about séances and hauntings.

The experiment was associated particularly with **A. R. George Owen** and his wife **Iris Owen**, members of the Toronto Society for Psychical Research.

The group's work was later described in their book *Conjuring Up Philip: An Adventure in Psychokinesis*, published in 1976.

# **The Question Behind the Experiment**

The researchers were interested in reports of:

* table turning
* rapping sounds
* alleged communication with spirits
* poltergeist-like activity
* psychokinesis
* séance phenomena

A traditional explanation was that a genuine spirit or other discarnate entity was responsible.

But another possibility existed.

Perhaps the people attending séances themselves were somehow producing the phenomena.

The Toronto group wanted to explore this possibility.

Their experiment therefore asked a fascinating question:

**If a "ghost" does not actually exist, can people nevertheless create the experience of communicating with one?**

That question is what makes the Philip Experiment different from an ordinary ghost story.

# **The Ideomotor Explanation**

One of the strongest conventional explanations is the **ideomotor effect**.

The ideomotor effect refers to unconscious physical movements produced by expectations, beliefs or mental activity.

A person does not necessarily realize they are moving.

This phenomenon has been studied extensively in connection with:

* Ouija boards
* dowsing rods
* pendulums
* table turning
* automatic writing

A group sitting around a table could potentially produce small movements without consciously deciding to do so.

If several people apply force simultaneously, the combined effect can become much larger.

# **The Group Psychology Explanation**

**Expectation → movement → interpretation → increased expectation → stronger movement.**
