# **Quick Facts**

# **Introduction**

Few modern UFO cases have attracted as much attention as the **USS Nimitz Tic Tac incident**.

The event took place during a U.S. Navy training exercise in November 2004. According to Navy personnel who later described the incident, unusual radar contacts had been appearing in the area for days.

On November 14, fighter crews from the aircraft carrier **USS Nimitz** were sent to investigate one of the contacts.

What happened next became famous.

The pilots reported encountering a strange white object over the Pacific Ocean. It appeared to have no wings, tail, rotor or conventional exhaust. One pilot described it as looking similar to a large **Tic Tac candy**.

The encounter became even more unusual because a later Navy aircraft recorded an infrared video of an unidentified object.

That video eventually became known to the public as **FLIR1**.

In 2020, the U.S. Department of Defense officially released the footage and confirmed that it was genuine U.S. Navy video. The Pentagon nevertheless did **not** identify the object as extraterrestrial. It stated that the phenomena shown in the videos remained unidentified.

This distinction is critical.

**The video is genuine. The encounter was genuinely reported. The object has not been publicly proven to be an alien spacecraft.**

# **The USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group**

The incident occurred while the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group was conducting training operations in the Pacific.

The group included the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier **USS Nimitz** and the guided-missile cruiser **USS Princeton**.

The ships were operating in a large training area off Southern California and near the waters west of Mexico.

The **Princeton** played a particularly important role because its radar operators were reportedly detecting unusual contacts in the area.

The carrier group was also operating F/A-18 fighter aircraft as part of its training activities.

The combination of sophisticated military radar, fighter aircraft and experienced personnel is one of the reasons the incident later became so important to the UAP debate.

# **The Unusual Radar Contacts**

According to later accounts from Navy personnel, unusual radar contacts had been appearing around the training area before the famous visual encounter.

Senior Chief Operations Specialist **Kevin Day**, who was associated with the Princeton's air-defense radar operation, later described seeing multiple unusual contacts.

In later interviews and statements, Day said the radar contacts appeared at very high altitude before descending toward the lower atmosphere and ocean.

Some accounts describe groups of objects appearing around **80,000 feet or higher** and then descending toward much lower altitudes.

Other accounts describe the objects as hovering or remaining in the area before disappearing and reappearing.

These claims have become some of the most spectacular parts of the Nimitz story.

However, there is an important limitation:

**The complete raw radar data have not been publicly released.**

Therefore, modern readers cannot independently reconstruct every claimed altitude, acceleration or trajectory from the original radar records.

A 2026 research review of the public evidence likewise emphasizes that the strongest performance claims rely heavily on witness recollections because the raw radar package is not publicly available.

# **Why the Navy Became Concerned**

The unusual contacts were reportedly considered a potential safety problem for the training exercise.

The objects were not behaving like aircraft that the radar operators recognized.

Eventually, Navy fighter aircraft were directed toward one of the contacts.

This produced the encounter that would later become known as the **Tic Tac incident**.

# **November 14, 2004**

On November 14, 2004, Commander **David Fravor**, commanding officer of Strike Fighter Squadron 41, was flying an F/A-18F Super Hornet.

Lieutenant Commander **Alex Dietrich** was flying another aircraft in the same flight.

Their aircraft were accompanied by weapons systems officers.

The crews were directed toward an area where an unusual contact had been detected.

According to Fravor's later testimony, the pilots initially noticed something unusual happening on the ocean below.

# **The Strange Patch of Water**

When the aircraft approached the reported location, the pilots noticed an area of disturbed white water.

Fravor later described the water as appearing as though something was disturbing the ocean surface.

Above this area was a strange object.

This became one of the most famous details of the entire incident.

The object was described as a smooth, white, oblong shape without visible wings or conventional aircraft features.

Fravor later estimated that it was approximately **40 feet long**, although the actual distance and size were never independently measured.

# **What Did the Tic Tac Look Like?**

The pilots' descriptions became the origin of the object's famous nickname.

The object was reportedly:

* White
* Smooth
* Oblong
* Roughly cylindrical or capsule-shaped
* Without visible wings
* Without visible tail surfaces
* Without visible rotors
* Without an obvious exhaust plume
* Without obvious conventional propulsion

Fravor compared its appearance to a **Tic Tac candy**.

A later official Navy-related document describes the reported object as solid white, smooth and without visible nacelles, pylons or wings, with an estimated length of about 46 feet.

The object did not resemble a conventional fighter aircraft.

# **The Object Over the Water**

The object appeared to be hovering above the disturbed water.

Fravor and the other aircrew watched it from their aircraft.

According to Fravor's later account, he decided to descend toward the object.

The encounter then became much more dramatic.

# **Fravor's Approach**

Fravor began descending toward the object while maneuvering his aircraft.

He later described the object as appearing to react to his movements.

According to his testimony, the object moved in relation to the aircraft and then rapidly departed.

In his 2023 written statement to the U.S. House Oversight Committee, Fravor said that when his aircraft was approximately half a mile from the object, the Tic Tac rapidly accelerated and disappeared from view.

He also stated that the Princeton's radar controller later reported the object at the carrier group's designated combat air patrol point approximately **60 miles away**.

That claim became one of the most extraordinary elements of the case.

# **The Famous 60-Mile Question**

According to Fravor's account, the object disappeared from the pilots' immediate view and then appeared on the Princeton's radar at their CAP point.

The distance between the encounter area and the CAP point was reportedly around **60 miles**.

If the same physical object had traveled that distance in less than a minute, the implied speed would be extraordinary.

This is often presented online as proof that the object traveled at impossible speed.

But there is an important scientific caveat.

The public does not possess the complete radar data needed to establish:

* Exact position
* Exact time
* Exact target identity
* Continuous radar tracking
* Whether the radar return at the CAP point was definitely the same object

Therefore, the **60-mile rapid-transit claim is an important witness account, but it should not be presented as a publicly verified measurement.**

# **Alex Dietrich's Account**

Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich was one of the other pilots present during the encounter.

Her account is particularly important because she independently observed the object.

Dietrich has described seeing a white object that appeared unusual and lacked conventional aircraft features.

However, she has generally been more cautious than some UFO presentations of the event.

She has emphasized the difference between **what she personally saw** and what other people later concluded about the object's speed or capabilities.

That distinction makes her testimony valuable.

It demonstrates that the case does not depend entirely upon one pilot's memory.

# **The Object Disappears**

After the pilots' close encounter, the Tic Tac disappeared.

Fravor later reported that the disturbed water also appeared to disappear.

The pilots then returned toward their assigned area.

The incident could have remained a strange military story known only to the personnel involved.

But another aircraft was about to become involved.

# **The Second F/A-18 Flight**

After Fravor's flight returned, another Navy F/A-18 crew was sent to investigate.

This crew included Lieutenant Commander **Chad Underwood**, who was operating the aircraft's targeting systems.

Unlike Fravor's encounter, Underwood's aircraft carried an **ATFLIR** — an Advanced Targeting Forward-Looking Infrared targeting pod.

This system could record infrared imagery.

During the flight, Underwood acquired another unidentified target.

That recording became famous years later.

# **The FLIR1 Video**

The resulting recording became known as the **FLIR1 video**, or simply the **Tic Tac video**.

The footage is short and heavily technical.

It shows a bright, elongated object against a dark background.

The object appears as an infrared/thermal target rather than a normal photographic image.

The video became particularly famous because the object appears to move rapidly and because the aircraft's sensor system displays tracking information and changing geometry.

But there is another crucial point:

**The FLIR1 video was not recorded by Fravor during his original visual encounter.**

It came from the later F/A-18 flight.

This distinction is frequently lost in online retellings.

# **What Does the FLIR Video Actually Prove?**

The video proves several things.

It demonstrates that:

**A U.S. Navy aircraft recorded a real infrared target.**

The Department of Defense later officially confirmed that the footage was genuine Navy imagery.

It does **not**, by itself, prove:

* The object's physical size
* Its exact distance
* Its acceleration
* Its origin
* Its propulsion system
* That it was the same physical object Fravor saw
* That it was extraterrestrial

The video contains insufficient public information to independently reconstruct all of those properties.

This is one of the most important facts to understand about the case.

# **The Pentagon Officially Releases the Video**

The Nimitz footage had circulated publicly before the U.S. government officially released it.

On **April 27, 2020**, the Department of Defense announced the authorized release of three unclassified Navy videos.

One was from **November 2004** — the Nimitz-era video.

The Pentagon stated that the Navy had previously acknowledged that the circulating videos were genuine Navy videos.

After review, the Department of Defense concluded that releasing them would not expose sensitive capabilities.

Most importantly, the Department stated that the aerial phenomena shown in the videos remained characterized as **“unidentified.”**

This is why the video is often described as **officially authenticated**.

But "authenticated" means the government confirmed the provenance of the footage.

It does not mean the government confirmed an extraterrestrial explanation.
