# **What Is the Face on Mars?**

The "Face on Mars" is the informal name given to a large mesa-like landform in the **Cydonia region of Mars**.

Cydonia lies in the transition zone between Mars's southern highlands and northern plains, within the broader Arabia Terra region. The landscape contains numerous isolated hills, mesas, ridges and other remnants of ancient geological terrain.

One of these formations became famous because an image taken by NASA's **Viking 1 Orbiter on July 25, 1976** appeared to show a human face looking upward from the Martian surface. ([NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia01141-geologic-face-on-mars-formation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The formation itself is not a separate object floating above Mars and is not known to contain any artificial structure. It is part of the Martian terrain.

NASA's geological interpretation is that it is a natural landform shaped by geological processes, including erosion and downslope movement of material. ESA's Mars Express observations later provided additional evidence for this interpretation. ([NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia03905-the-so-called-face-on-mars-in-infrared/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# **The Viking 1 Photograph — July 25, 1976**

The story began during NASA's Viking mission.

Viking 1 was part of America's first successful mission to land spacecraft on Mars and conduct detailed scientific investigations of the planet. The Viking 1 orbiter was photographing different parts of Mars while scientists searched for suitable areas and studied the Martian surface.

On **July 25, 1976**, Viking 1 photographed the Cydonia region while searching for a possible landing site for Viking 2.

One of the images contained the strange face-like formation.

NASA's later documentation identifies the image as showing a feature approximately **1.5 kilometers (about one mile) across**. At the time, Viking 1 was approximately **1,873 kilometers (1,162 miles)** from the surface, and the Sun's illumination angle was approximately **20 degrees**. ([NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia01141-geologic-face-on-mars-formation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The original image was relatively low resolution compared with modern Mars imagery.

That detail is extremely important.

The formation itself did not suddenly become face-shaped in 1976. Rather, a combination of its physical shape, the direction of sunlight and the limitations of the photograph produced an image in which certain ridges and shadows resembled facial features.

# **Why Did It Look Like a Human Face?**

The most important explanation involves **light, shadow, viewing angle and pareidolia**.

Pareidolia is the tendency of the human brain to recognize familiar patterns—such as faces—in ambiguous shapes.

Humans are particularly sensitive to faces. We can see faces in clouds, mountains, rocks, trees and even random patterns.

The Cydonia formation provided a particularly powerful example.

From the angle at which Viking photographed it, portions of the terrain appeared to form:

* two eye-like dark regions
* a central ridge resembling a nose
* a darker region resembling a mouth
* an overall head-like outline

NASA explained that shadows cast by the geological structure created the apparent nose and mouth. The original image also contained missing data caused by transmission errors, affecting portions of what appeared to be facial features. ([NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia01141-geologic-face-on-mars-formation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

In other words, the photograph did not show a perfectly sculpted face.

It showed a natural geological formation photographed under conditions that happened to make it resemble one.

# **The Strange "Eye" and "Nostril" Details**

One of the reasons the original photograph became so compelling was that some small areas appeared to look like additional facial details.

But the image itself contained technical imperfections.

NASA explains that the speckled appearance was partly caused by **missing data, or bit errors, during transmission of the photographic information from Mars to Earth**. Some of these errors happened to affect areas that viewers interpreted as facial features. ([NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia01141-geologic-face-on-mars-formation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This is a fascinating part of the story because it demonstrates how a technical limitation can contribute to an already unusual visual illusion.

The original image was not equivalent to a modern high-resolution photograph.

It was a relatively crude representation of the Martian surface, and viewers naturally filled in some of the missing or ambiguous information with what their brains expected to see.
