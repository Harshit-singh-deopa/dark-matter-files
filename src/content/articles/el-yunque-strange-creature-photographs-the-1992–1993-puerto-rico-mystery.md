# **Introduction**

Deep inside Puerto Rico's tropical **El Yunque rainforest** lies one of the more unusual photographic claims in UFO literature.

In **March 1993**, two young men, **Nelson Berríos and Joaquín Ruiz**, visited the area around **La Mina Falls** with friends.

Ruiz used Berríos's 35mm Concord camera to photograph the scenery and people around the waterfall.

Neither man reportedly noticed anything unusual at the time.

But when the film was developed, the laboratory allegedly noticed something strange in several photographs.

A small figure appeared near a rock.

According to the account later published by UFO investigator **Jorge Martín**, the figure appeared to have a humanoid shape, a pale or pinkish appearance, large/deep-set eyes and unusual hair-like material on its head.

The photographs became known within UFO circles as evidence of a possible humanoid presence in El Yunque.

But there is a crucial limitation:

### **The photographs have never been scientifically established as photographs of an unknown creature.**

# **El Yunque National Forest**

El Yunque is a real tropical rainforest in northeastern Puerto Rico.

The U.S. Forest Service describes it as the **only tropical rainforest within the U.S. National Forest System**.

The forest covers nearly **29,000 acres** and extends across several municipalities in northeastern Puerto Rico.

Its steep terrain, dense vegetation, rivers and waterfalls make it an extraordinary natural environment.

La Mina is one of the forest's recreation areas.

The landscape is therefore important to the story because dense vegetation, shadows, rocks, flowing water and changing light can make visual identification difficult.

# **The 1993 La Mina Falls Photograph Case**

## **The Visit**

According to Jorge Martín's published account, the incident occurred on an afternoon in **March 1993**.

Berríos and four other friends were visiting the area around **Las Minas Falls**, described in the account as a popular waterfall and swimming area.

At approximately **6:30 p.m.**, Berríos was swimming.

He asked his friend **Joaquín Ruiz** to use his camera to take photographs.

Ruiz agreed.

The camera was a **35mm Concord camera** that Berríos had purchased from a department store.

The film inside was reportedly a **24-exposure roll of 400 ASA film**.

# **How Many Photographs Were Taken?**

Ruiz later said that he had taken approximately **seven or eight photographs**.

This is important because the strange figure was not supposedly visible in only one isolated frame.

According to the published account, the figure appeared in **at least four photographs**.

The men reportedly did not notice the figure while taking the photographs.

That is one of the main reasons the case became interesting to UFO investigators.

# **The Film Was Developed**

After the visit, Berríos took the film to a photographic laboratory in **Carolina, Puerto Rico**.

According to the account, the laboratory personnel noticed something unusual in several photographs.

They reportedly asked Berríos about a strange object or figure visible toward the side of the images.

Berríos and Ruiz then examined the photographs themselves.

They claimed that something resembling a small humanoid could be seen near one of the rocks.

The men said they had not noticed a person or creature in that location when the photographs were taken.

# **What Did the Figure Look Like?**

The description comes from the witnesses and Jorge Martín's publication, rather than from a scientific identification.

The figure was described as:

* Small
* Roughly child-sized
* Humanoid in appearance
* Pale or pale-pink in color
* Having prominent/deep-set oval eyes
* Having unusual facial features
* Having strange light-colored hair or fuzz on its head

Jorge Martín's English-language account describes the figure as having **pale-pink skin** and unusual, straw-like hair standing upward from its head.

Ruiz reportedly described it as approximately the size of a child and having deep-set oval eyes and wrinkled-looking features.

# **The Figure Was Behind a Rock**

The alleged creature was reportedly positioned near a rock beside the waterfall.

The important claim was that it appeared to be **partially concealed**.

Only part of the figure could supposedly be seen.

This made the photographs difficult to interpret because the figure was relatively small compared with the entire scene.

The published account says the figure appeared in several frames while remaining in approximately the same area.

# **Why Didn't the Witnesses See It?**

This is one of the central questions.

Ruiz was physically present and looking through the camera while taking the photographs.

Yet he reportedly saw nothing unusual.

The witnesses later suggested that the creature might have been hiding behind the rock.

The original article records the witnesses' speculation that it might have been avoiding the camera's flash.

That explanation, however, is **their interpretation**, not an established fact.

# **The Photographs Were Enlarged**

After discovering the unusual figure, Berríos reportedly ordered enlargements.

The enlargements made the small figure easier to see.

He then provided the material to **Jorge Martín**, a Puerto Rican UFO investigator and editor of *Evidencia OVNI*.

Martín subsequently published the photographs and investigated the story.

# **Jorge Martín's Investigation**

Jorge Martín became one of the best-known UFO investigators in Puerto Rico.

His publications documented numerous alleged UFO and humanoid reports around El Yunque.

The 1993 photograph case was eventually included in a special **1996 edition of *Evidencia OVNI*** dealing with El Yunque.

The same case was later published in English in **Flying Saucer Review**.

The *Flying Saucer Review* article identifies itself as a translation from Martín's Spanish-language material.

# **The Flying Saucer Review Account**

The English article was titled:

### **"A Strange Creature Photographed in the Puerto Rico National Forest of El Yunque"**

It was credited to **Jorge Martín**, editor of *Evidencia OVNI* and a consultant to *Flying Saucer Review*.

The article describes the 1993 photograph and interviews with Berríos and Ruiz.

This is one of the most important surviving published sources for the case.

# **The Earlier 1992 El Yunque Reports**

The year **1992** is sometimes combined with the 1993 photograph case, but the material describes separate incidents.

One notable report came from the **Colinas del Yunque** area in approximately **August 1992**.

According to reports attributed to Jorge Martín, **Soraya Collazo and members of her family and neighborhood** reportedly saw two small humanoid figures walking along a roadway near the forest.

The beings were described as approximately **1.2 meters (about 4 feet) tall**, with unusual heads and greenish skin.

These were **eyewitness reports, not photographs**.

# **The 1992 "Green Humanoids"**

The 1992 account described the alleged beings as:

* Approximately four feet tall
* Thin
* Greenish in appearance
* With relatively large heads
* Long arms
* Light-brown/stringy hair

One version says the children followed them briefly before the figures entered a wooded area.

Another detail in the published account says one of the figures allegedly made a two-finger gesture resembling a **peace sign**.

Again, these are **reported witness claims**, not independently verified observations.

# **Why 1992 and 1993 Became Connected**

The two years became connected because UFO investigators noticed similarities between the alleged beings.

The 1992 eyewitness reports described small humanoids.

The 1993 photograph appeared to show another small humanoid.

This led investigators to suggest that the same type of entity might have been present in the El Yunque region.

But there is no independent evidence demonstrating that the 1992 witnesses and 1993 photographers encountered the same phenomenon.

# **The 1993 Photograph's Most Important Claim**

The strongest claim made by the witnesses was not simply:

**"There is something strange in this photograph."**

It was:

### **"We were there, we photographed the scene, but we did not see the figure with our own eyes."**

That distinction makes the case interesting but also creates an evidentiary problem.

A photograph can sometimes reveal something a person did not notice.

But without the **original negative**, camera information, independent forensic examination and controlled reproduction, it is difficult to determine exactly what the photographed object was.

# **What Happened to the Original Film?**

This is one of the frustrating aspects of the case.

The published material documents the existence of the photographs and their reproduction in UFO publications.

However, there is no widely available independent scientific archive containing the **original negative strip** and complete photographic documentation.

That means modern researchers generally have to work from reproductions.

This significantly limits what can be concluded.

# **Why the Original Negative Matters**

If the original negative were available, investigators could potentially examine:

Without the original negative, many photographic questions remain difficult to resolve.

# **Could It Be a Person?**

One obvious skeptical possibility is that the figure could simply have been a person.

El Yunque is a popular recreational area, and the La Mina area contains trails, waterfalls and swimming locations.

A person partially hidden behind a rock could appear unusually small or strange depending on distance, perspective and lighting.

However, the witnesses specifically stated that they did not see anyone matching the figure at the time.

That makes the human explanation possible but not conclusively demonstrated.

# **Could It Be a Child?**

This is another straightforward possibility.

The figure was described as approximately child-sized.

A child standing or crouching behind vegetation or a rock could potentially produce a similar appearance.

The witnesses' claim that no child was there is important testimony, but it cannot independently establish that the figure was non-human.

# **Could It Be an Animal?**

Another possibility is an animal or partially obscured animal.

El Yunque contains a diverse range of wildlife.

The combination of:

* Distance
* Vegetation
* Rock formations
* Shadows
* Motion
* Film grain

can make an animal look surprisingly humanoid in a low-resolution photograph.

There is no published scientific identification establishing that the figure is an animal either.

# **Could It Be a Photographic Artifact?**

Another possibility is a photographic or development artifact.

Film photography can contain:

* Scratches
* Dust
* Hair
* Chemical marks
* Emulsion defects
* Light leaks
* Reflections
* Motion blur
* Exposure artifacts

The fact that the alleged figure reportedly appears in several frames makes a simple single-frame defect less obvious, but without the original negatives, this possibility cannot be completely evaluated.

# **Could It Be a Hoax?**

A staged photograph is another possibility.

That does not mean the witnesses necessarily fabricated the story.

A hoax could theoretically involve:

* A person deliberately positioned in the scene
* A prop
* A model
* An object placed behind a rock
* A later manipulation

There is no publicly established evidence proving that the 1993 photographs were deliberately fabricated.

Therefore, **hoax should be treated as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion**.

# **The Cryptid Explanation**

Another interpretation is that the figure could be an unknown animal or cryptid living in the rainforest.

El Yunque's remote and dense environment makes this appealing to some paranormal researchers.

However, no biological evidence has been produced showing that an unknown humanoid species lives there.

There is:

* No body

This is another claim that is often exaggerated online.

That does not disprove individual reports of unusual events.

It simply means that the broad claim that the entire forest is a permanently inaccessible secret facility is not consistent with its documented public recreational status.

# **What Do Skeptics Think?**

Skeptical interpretations generally fall into several categories:

### **1. Human**

A person, especially a child, may have been partly hidden by the rocks.

### **2. Animal**

A nearby animal may have appeared humanoid because of perspective.

### **3. Optical illusion**

Vegetation and rocks may have combined to create a face or body-like pattern.

### **4. Photographic artifact**

Film defects or exposure effects may have created an unusual shape.

### **5. Deliberate staging**

The image could have been intentionally created as a hoax.

None of these explanations has been conclusively demonstrated either.
