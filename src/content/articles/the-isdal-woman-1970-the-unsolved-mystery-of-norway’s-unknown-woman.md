# **The Discovery in Isdalen**

The story began on **Sunday, 29 November 1970**, near Bergen in western Norway.

A man hiking with his two young daughters entered the mountainous area known as **Isdalen**, literally “Ice Valley.” They discovered the body of an unidentified woman among the rocks.

The area was remote and difficult to access, making the discovery particularly unusual. Bergen police were contacted and began an investigation that became known as **case 134/70**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdal_Woman?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

The woman's face had been badly affected by the fire, making visual identification difficult. Investigators also found various personal objects around the scene.

Importantly, the case did not begin as a mysterious spy story.

It began as the investigation of an unidentified woman whose death had occurred under unusual circumstances.

# **The Scene and the Evidence**

Police documented numerous objects around the woman.

Reported items included a liquor bottle, plastic water bottles, a passport holder, rubber boots, a woollen jumper, a scarf, nylon stockings, an umbrella, a purse, a matchbox, a watch, earrings and a ring. Investigators also recorded traces of burned material and found evidence associated with petrol. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdal_Woman?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

One particularly striking feature was the removal of identifying information.

Labels had been cut from clothing and identifying marks had been removed or rubbed away from various belongings.

This did not prove that someone else had prepared the scene. It did, however, establish that the woman had either deliberately concealed her identity herself or that somebody else had done so.

That question became central to the entire investigation.

# **The Forensic Examination**

The autopsy was carried out at the Gades Institute in Bergen.

Investigators found evidence of **phenobarbital**, a barbiturate sedative. Reports indicate that approximately **50 to 70 Fenemal sleeping pills** had been consumed, while additional tablets were found near the body. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdal_Woman?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

The forensic examination also detected **carbon monoxide**, and soot was found in the airways.

The soot was important because it indicated that she was breathing while the fire was burning. ([Bergens Tidende](https://www.bt.no/nyheter/brif/i/O781V/fem-ting-du-boer-vite-om-isdalskvinnen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

A bruise was also noted around the neck. Contemporary reporting described it as the only notable sign of possible physical injury and said it could have resulted from a fall or a blow. The significance of that injury was never conclusively established. ([Bergens Tidende](https://www.bt.no/nyheter/brif/i/O781V/fem-ting-du-boer-vite-om-isdalskvinnen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

The official forensic interpretation therefore did not establish a simple answer such as “she died from the fire.”

The recorded explanation involved the combination of **barbiturate intoxication and carbon-monoxide poisoning**, with the fire injuries also contributing.

# **The Wigs and Disguises**

That naturally led investigators and later researchers to wonder whether she had been deliberately changing her appearance.

But the existence of wigs does not automatically establish espionage.

A person trying to avoid recognition for many different reasons could use a wig.

The wigs therefore belong in the category of **documented evidence**, while the interpretation of why she used them remains uncertain.

# **Her Hotel Trail**

The hotel records allowed investigators to reconstruct parts of her movements.

One of the most important stays was at **Hotel Hordaheimen in Bergen**.

She stayed in **Room 407** and used the name Elisabeth Leenhouwer in the registration record. According to the historical reporting, she checked out on **23 November 1970**, six days before her body was discovered. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdal_Woman?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

Hotel staff described her as a well-dressed woman with dark hair and brown eyes. She generally kept to herself and appeared guarded.

# **The 2017 Isotope Investigation**

The next major development came from the woman's teeth.

Researchers analyzed **strontium and oxygen isotopes** preserved in tooth enamel.

Tooth enamel forms during childhood and can retain chemical information connected with the environment in which a person lived.

The research was conducted by Norwegian investigators with assistance from **Professor Jurian Hoogewerff of the University of Canberra** and researchers at the University of Bergen. Three teeth were analyzed, representing different periods of her childhood and adolescence. ([Bergens Tidende](https://www.bt.no/nyheter/lokalt/i/pyj7R/isdalskvinnen-var-trolig-fra-tyskland?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

The results suggested a childhood connection to **southeastern Germany**, with later evidence pointing toward western parts of Europe, including areas of northern France.

Bergens Tidende reported that the strongest interpretation pointed toward southeastern Germany, although France remained a possibility. ([Bergens Tidende](https://www.bt.no/nyheter/lokalt/i/pyj7R/isdalskvinnen-var-trolig-fra-tyskland?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

This was a major narrowing of the geographical search

But it was still **not an identification**.

# **What the Isotope Evidence Tells Us**

The isotope research is sometimes presented online as if scientists discovered the woman's exact hometown.

That is too strong.

Isotope analysis can narrow geographical possibilities because the chemistry of food and water can leave measurable signatures in developing teeth.

It can therefore indicate where someone likely lived during particular periods.

It cannot normally provide a person's name by itself.

The researchers found evidence consistent with movement from a Central European region toward western Europe during her childhood or adolescence. ([Bergens Tidende](https://www.bt.no/nyheter/lokalt/i/pyj7R/isdalskvinnen-var-trolig-fra-tyskland?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) 

This also fits some of the earlier handwriting evidence, which suggested that she may have received part of her education in a French-speaking environment.

# **What Her DNA Revealed**

Later genetic work also provided information about her ancestry.

Reports have associated her maternal line with **mtDNA haplogroup H24**, which is found in parts of southeastern Europe and surrounding regions.

This is useful genealogical information, but it should not be confused with an exact nationality.

A mitochondrial haplogroup traces a maternal genetic line; it does not mean that the woman herself was necessarily a citizen of a particular modern country.

Most importantly, the DNA still did not produce a publicly confirmed name. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdal_Woman?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
