# **The Strange Story of Jetpack Man**

In late summer 2020, something extraordinary appeared to be happening in the skies around Los Angeles.

Airline pilots approaching **Los Angeles International Airport (LAX)** began reporting a strange object that looked remarkably like a person wearing a jetpack.

The reports were unusual enough to attract the attention of the **Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)**, the **Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)** and local law-enforcement agencies.

The first major report came on **August 30, 2020**, when pilots aboard American Airlines Flight 1997 reported seeing what appeared to be a person flying beside their aircraft at approximately **3,000 feet**.

Only minutes later, another airline crew reported seeing what appeared to be the same person.

Then, on October 14, a China Airlines crew reported another strange object at roughly **6,000–6,500 feet**.

In December, a flight instructor from **Sling Pilot Academy** recorded a video of an object that appeared to resemble a person carrying a jetpack.

The mystery did not end there.

Additional reports appeared in 2021 and again in 2022.

Yet despite years of investigation, no person was publicly identified as the pilot of a jetpack, and the FAA eventually said that none of the reported sightings had been verified. One official working theory was that pilots could have been seeing **balloons**.

The story is therefore more complicated than the popular nickname "Jetpack Man" suggests.

# **The First Major Sighting — August 30, 2020**

The case became public after a remarkable radio transmission from an American Airlines flight.

On **August 30, 2020**, American Airlines Flight 1997 was approaching Los Angeles International Airport.

According to the FBI's later records, the aircraft was approximately **10 miles from LAX**, around **3,000 feet above the ground**, and travelling at approximately **180 knots**.

The captain noticed something off the aircraft's left side.

At first, he considered several ordinary possibilities.

It could have been:

**A balloon**

**A drone**

**A parachute**

But as the aircraft approached, the object appeared to remain in roughly the same position relative to the airplane.

The captain eventually concluded that it looked like a person wearing a jetpack.

The famous radio transmission was:

> "Tower, American 1997. We just passed a guy in a jet pack."

The tower asked where the object had been.

The pilot replied that it was approximately **300 yards to the left** of the aircraft and around the same altitude.

# **What Did the American Airlines Pilot Actually See?**

According to the pilot's interview, the object appeared vertically oriented.

He described what appeared to be:

**Black boots**

**An orange jumpsuit or jacket**

Most remarkably, he said the apparent figure seemed to be looking toward the aircraft.

He estimated that he observed it for approximately **10 seconds**.

The pilot later told investigators that he had difficulty believing what he had seen.

Two colleagues, including the first officer, also reportedly saw something resembling the same object.

# **Why the First Sighting Was So Serious**

This was not a sighting from somebody standing on the ground looking at a distant light.

It occurred near commercial aviation traffic.

The pilot reportedly worried that the person could potentially collide with the aircraft.

He later told investigators that he wondered whether the apparent individual was intentionally trying to ram the airplane.

He described the situation as an unnecessary risk to passengers and crew.

That concern explains why the incident quickly became an aviation-safety matter.

The FBI was informed of the August 30 incident on **September 1, 2020**.

An FBI case was opened under the description of a possible person with a jetpack flying near American Airlines Flight 1997 as the aircraft approached LAX.

Investigators also obtained material from the FAA, including flight-path information, recordings and other data.

Investigators also had actual video of a human-shaped balloon, resembling Jack Skellington, that demonstrated how strange such an object could look from the air.

One witness told investigators that the balloon's flight characteristics appeared very similar to what he had observed.

This does not prove the December object was that exact balloon.

It simply demonstrates that a plausible conventional object could produce a very similar visual impression.

# **Could It Have Been a Drone With a Mannequin?**

Also possible.

A drone could carry a lightweight mannequin or human-shaped structure.

At a distance, a person-shaped drone could easily be mistaken for an actual human.

The FBI records show that investigators explicitly presented witnesses with footage of remote-controlled flying mannequins.

Some witnesses said the shape resembled what they had seen.

No publicly released evidence identifies a particular drone operator as responsible for the LAX sightings.

# **Could It Have Been a Real Jetpack?**

It cannot be completely ruled out from the public evidence.

Jetpack technology is real.

But there is no publicly verified evidence that a person actually flew a jetpack through the reported LAX airspace.

The major problems are:

**Range**

**Altitude**

**Fuel endurance**

**Noise**

**Visibility**

**Airspace restrictions**

**Takeoff and landing**

**Lack of an identified operator**

The FBI and FAA never publicly verified the existence of a jetpack pilot responsible for the reports.

# **Could It Have Been a Classified Military Aircraft?**

This is one of the more speculative explanations.

Southern California has a long history of aerospace development.

The possibility of experimental technology naturally attracted attention.

However, there is no public evidence showing that the FBI or FAA concluded that the objects were secret military aircraft.

There is also no publicly identified aircraft that matches all of the reported descriptions.

Therefore, this theory remains speculation.

# **Could Some Reports Have Been Completely Unrelated?**

This may actually be one of the most reasonable explanations.

Consider the variety:

One object was described as a human figure.

Another was later described as not really human-shaped.

Another witness described an object resembling a jet ski or racing motorcycle.

Another described a red object.

Another described a person-like object in black and red.

Another video showed something that appeared human-shaped.

These may not have been the same object.

The phrase **"Jetpack Man"** may have gradually turned a collection of different sightings into one apparent mystery.

# **The Role of Media Influence**

This is one of the most interesting psychological aspects of the case.

After the first sightings became national news, later witnesses already knew about "Jetpack Man."

The FBI's China Airlines interview provides a direct example.

The captain initially reported a strange object, but later explained that he associated it with a jetpack partly because he had heard about previous jetpack sightings.

Likewise, the Sling Pilot Academy instructor already knew about the story when she saw her December object.

This does **not** mean the witnesses imagined the objects.

It means the name "Jetpack Man" could have influenced how ambiguous objects were interpreted.

A pilot approaching LAX reports seeing what appears to be a person with a jetpack around **4,500 feet**, approximately 15 miles east of LAX. The FAA again says previous sightings have not been verified and mentions balloons as a working theory.

### **October 2025**

The FBI releases an interim FOIA response concerning its "Investigation of Person Flying a Jetpack." The agency says it reviewed **250 pages** and released **130 pages**, while consultation with another government agency remains ongoing.

# **Why the Jetpack Man Mystery Remains Famous**

The case combines several things that naturally attract attention.

There were multiple pilots.

There were ATC recordings.

There was FBI involvement.

There was FAA involvement.

There was actual video.

There were repeated reports across several years.

And the object appeared to resemble something that should be impossible to encounter casually in commercial airspace.

But the investigation also produced something equally interesting:

**uncertainty.**

The pilots did not all describe exactly the same thing.

Some later became less certain.

Some thought the object might have been a balloon.

Some saw something that did not look human at all.

And no verified jetpack pilot was ever publicly identified.

# **Final Assessment**

The **LAX Jetpack Man mystery is a genuine collection of aviation reports**, not simply an internet hoax invented after the fact.

The FBI really investigated it.

The FAA really investigated the reports.

Pilots really made the radio calls.

The December 2020 video is real.

And the later FBI records show that investigators followed up on many of the reports in considerable detail.

But the evidence does **not** establish that a person repeatedly flew a jetpack around Los Angeles.

The strongest conventional explanation is that **different sightings may have involved different objects**, with balloons and possibly drones being particularly plausible explanations for at least some reports.

The human-shaped balloon discovered by LAPD is especially interesting because investigators were able to compare its appearance and movement with witness observations.

At the same time, not every sighting can be confidently matched to that balloon.

The December video remains visually ambiguous.

The later FBI records also show that some pilots described objects that did not look like human beings at all.

So the most responsible conclusion is:

> **Multiple pilots and other witnesses reported unusual objects around the Los Angeles area between 2020 and 2022. The FBI and FAA investigated the reports, but no person using a jetpack was ever publicly verified. Balloons, drones, unusual inflatables and misidentification remain the leading conventional explanations, while the exact identity of every reported object has not been established.**

There is **no publicly verified evidence that the Jetpack Man was extraterrestrial**, and there is also no evidence establishing one single object behind every sighting.

The mystery is therefore best described as an **unresolved aviation phenomenon with several plausible conventional explanations**, rather than a confirmed UFO or confirmed jetpack pilot.
