## Where Is the Chase Vault?

The Chase Vault is located in the churchyard of Christ Church Parish Church near Oistins, Barbados.

The site has a long religious history, and the present church is not the same building that existed during the early nineteenth century.

The vault itself is a small historic masonry burial structure associated with the Chase family.

Today, the vault is empty.

The official history of Christ Church Parish Church discusses the Chase Vault and the reports that coffins inside the structure were repeatedly found displaced.

## The History of the Vault

The vault appears to have existed before the Chase family became associated with it.

Historical investigations have connected the structure with the Elliot family before it was later acquired by Colonel Thomas Chase.

This is significant because the famous story is often presented as though the Chase family had constructed the vault specifically for themselves.

The surviving historical evidence suggests that the structure had an earlier history.

The vault therefore existed before the most famous incidents traditionally associated with the Chase family.

## The Traditional Timeline

The exact chronology of the Chase Vault story is difficult to establish.

Different historical sources give different dates, names and sequences of burials.

The timeline below therefore describes the traditional story rather than claiming that every event has been independently confirmed.

## The Early Burials

According to later accounts, the vault became associated with the Chase family in the early nineteenth century.

One of the early burials traditionally connected with the vault is that of Ann Maria Chase in 1808.

Different sources use slightly different spellings and descriptions of the names involved.

The existence of the Chase family and several of the people associated with the story is supported by historical and genealogical material.

What is much harder to establish is whether the later stories about the coffins are historically accurate.

## The Reported Incident of 1812

The mystery became famous after another burial was reportedly scheduled inside the vault in 1812.

According to the traditional account, the vault was opened and the coffins already inside were found to have changed position.

The coffins were reportedly returned to their previous arrangement before the vault was closed again.

This event became the beginning of the famous moving-coffin story.

There is, however, no surviving contemporary report that conclusively documents the event exactly as it is usually described today.

The earliest known published account appeared many years later.

## The Burial of Thomas Chase

Later accounts state that Colonel Thomas Chase was buried in the vault during 1812.

His burial is generally regarded as another important point in the traditional chronology.

Some later versions of the story contain additional claims about the Chase family and the circumstances surrounding individual deaths.

Those claims should be treated cautiously because they are not necessary to establish the coffin mystery and are not supported equally well by surviving historical evidence.

The important point is that Thomas Chase became one of the people traditionally associated with the vault during the period of the reported disturbances.

## The Coffins Were Reportedly Found Displaced Again

The traditional story says that the vault was opened on later occasions for additional burials.

Each time, the coffins were allegedly found in positions different from where they had previously been placed.

Some versions describe coffins lying at unusual angles or being pushed against other parts of the vault.

The exact number of incidents varies between accounts.

This is an important problem because different writers do not always agree about the sequence of events.

Some accounts describe several separate disturbances, while others appear to combine details from different versions of the story.

## Why the Coffins Were Considered Mysterious

The story became especially difficult to explain because the coffins were traditionally described as heavy lead-lined coffins.

If the traditional account is accepted literally, the question becomes obvious:

How could several heavy coffins have been moved around inside a relatively small stone vault?

This question eventually became the central mystery surrounding the Chase Vault.

Supporters of the traditional story have argued that the reported movements were too substantial to be explained by ordinary settling.

Skeptical investigators have taken a different approach.

Before attempting to determine how the coffins moved, they argue that historians first need to establish whether the reported movements actually occurred in the way later sources describe them.

## The Sand and Sealing Story

One of the most famous details of the Chase Vault legend concerns the use of sand.

According to later accounts, people became concerned that someone might be entering the vault and moving the coffins.

The entrance was therefore reportedly secured.

Fine sand was also supposedly spread across the floor.

The purpose was simple.

If someone entered the vault, footprints or disturbed sand should provide evidence of an intrusion.

The vault was then reportedly sealed.

This detail transformed the mystery.

The question was no longer simply:

Who moved the coffins?

It became:

How could the coffins have moved if nobody had entered the vault?

The problem is that this famous version of events comes from later historical accounts rather than a surviving contemporary report from the time.

## The 1820 Investigation

The traditional story reaches its climax in 1820.

According to later accounts, the governor of Barbados, Lord Combermere, became involved after hearing about the strange events.

The vault was reportedly opened for inspection.

When the entrance was opened, the coffins were allegedly found scattered or displaced inside.

One of the most famous claims is that the entrance seal was still intact and the sand inside had not been disturbed.

If that account were completely accurate, it would make ordinary human interference much more difficult to explain.

However, this is also one of the parts of the story for which historians have struggled to locate strong contemporary documentation.

## What Happened After the Final Opening?

According to the traditional account, the authorities eventually decided to remove the coffins from the vault.

The bodies were reportedly buried elsewhere in the churchyard.

The vault was then left empty.

The empty vault is still associated with the Chase Vault story today.

## The First Published Account

One of the most important facts about the Chase Vault is that the famous story was not immediately published when the alleged incidents occurred.

The earliest known published version appeared in 1833 in James Edward Alexander's book *Transatlantic Sketches*.

That was more than a decade after the final events traditionally associated with the vault.

Alexander described reports of coffins being found disturbed inside the vault.

His account became an important foundation for later versions of the story.

This does not automatically prove that the events never happened.

## Schomburgk's 1848 Account

Schomburgk described the famous coffin disturbances and the final inspection of the vault.

## The Flooding Theory

One of the most common natural explanations involves water.

Barbados is a tropical island, and water entering an underground structure is physically possible.

The theory suggests that water may have entered the vault and affected the position of the coffins.

If the coffins had become sufficiently buoyant, water could potentially have contributed to movement.

However, this explanation has limitations.

The coffins were reportedly very heavy, and the dramatic movements described in some versions of the story would require considerable force.

There is also no strong surviving evidence showing that flooding occurred during each of the reported incidents.

Flooding is therefore a possible explanation, but it has not been demonstrated as the solution to the mystery.

## The Decomposition Gas Theory

Another proposed explanation involves gases produced during decomposition.

The basic idea is that gases could accumulate inside enclosed burial structures or coffins and potentially create pressure.

This explanation has sometimes been suggested as a possible cause of disturbance.

However, there are physical problems with using gas pressure to explain the movement of several heavy coffins across a stone vault.

It is therefore better described as a proposed explanation rather than an established solution.

## The Earthquake Theory

Barbados experiences seismic activity, so earthquakes have also been suggested.

An earthquake could potentially disturb objects inside a structure.

However, a sufficiently strong earthquake to move several heavy coffins dramatically would likely have affected other structures as well.

There is no strong evidence connecting a specific earthquake to the reported coffin movements.

For this reason, an earthquake remains a possibility in principle but has not been demonstrated as the cause.

## Human Interference

Another explanation is much simpler.
