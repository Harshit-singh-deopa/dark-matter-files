# **Who Was Nicholas Roerich?**

Nicholas Roerich was a Russian-born artist, writer, archaeologist, philosopher and explorer who became particularly famous for his paintings and extensive travels across Central and South Asia.

He was born in **1874** in Saint Petersburg and developed a deep interest in history, archaeology, Asian cultures, mythology and spirituality.

His later life became closely associated with the Himalayan region.

Roerich did not approach Central Asia simply as a tourist. He was interested in the region's archaeological sites, art, religions, traditions and legends.

His **Central Asian Expedition of 1925–1928** traveled through enormous areas of Asia and produced paintings, photographs, observations and writings about the landscapes and cultures encountered along the route. The Nicholas Roerich Museum's archive documents the expedition and preserves photographs from different stages of the journey.

The expedition eventually became an important part of Roerich's legacy.

It also produced the account that later became associated with one of the world's better-known pre-1947 UFO reports.

# **The Central Asian Expedition**

Roerich's Central Asian Expedition lasted from **1925 to 1928**.

The expedition traveled through regions that today encompass parts of countries including India, Pakistan, China, Mongolia and areas of Central Asia.

The journey was difficult.

The expedition crossed mountains, deserts, high plateaus and remote regions where transportation and communication were extremely limited.

The expedition included Roerich, his wife **Helena Roerich**, their son **George Roerich**, other members of the caravan, local guides and religious figures.

The expedition's archive contains photographs from areas including Kashmir, Ladakh, the Karakoram, Chinese Turkestan, Altai, Mongolia and Tibet.

The scale of the journey is important when considering the UFO account.

This was not a story invented around an isolated photograph found decades later.

Roerich was keeping travel records during a real expedition across a remote part of Asia.
