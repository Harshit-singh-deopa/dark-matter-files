# The Case at a Glance

One of the most interesting UAP videos released by the U.S. Department of Defense shows what appears to be a small, silver, spherical object moving across the view of a camera aboard an MQ-9 Reaper in the Middle East.

The video became publicly known during a Senate hearing on unidentified anomalous phenomena on April 19, 2023, when Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick, then director of the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, or AARO, presented the footage.

The Department of Defense officially titled the clip **"Middle East Object."**

The official description says the video was recorded by an MQ-9 in the Middle East and that AARO assessed the object as **not exhibiting anomalous behavior**, while the object nevertheless remained unidentified. The case was retained in AARO's active archive because the available information was insufficient for a definitive identification.

The case became particularly interesting because the object appears to be a bright, metallic-looking sphere or orb moving across the landscape.

At first glance, it can look like the object is travelling extremely quickly.

However, independent analysis later suggested that much of the apparent movement could be explained by **motion parallax** caused by the movement of the MQ-9 itself.

That does not officially solve the case.

AARO still lists the object as unidentified.

The important distinction is that July 12, 2022 is associated with the video/observation in the public record, while the Pentagon's later public description simply identifies it as a 2022 MQ-9 case.

The footage itself became publicly discussed during the April 19, 2023 Senate hearing on AARO.

# What Aircraft Recorded the Object?

The object was recorded by a **U.S. MQ-9 Reaper unmanned aerial vehicle**.

The MQ-9 Reaper is a remotely piloted military aircraft used for surveillance, reconnaissance and other military missions.

The significance of this case is therefore different from a typical civilian UFO sighting.

The object was not simply reported by someone looking through a window.

It was recorded by a military aircraft's sensor system.

The object then disappears from the camera's view.

The footage is short and does not provide enough information to establish the object's complete three-dimensional trajectory.

The Department of Defense therefore classified it as a representative example of cases where AARO has limited data.

# What Is Motion Parallax?

Motion parallax occurs when an observer moves relative to objects at different distances.

Imagine looking out of a moving car.

Nearby objects appear to move quickly across your field of view.

Distant mountains appear to move much more slowly.

The mountains are not actually racing in the opposite direction.

Their apparent movement is caused by the movement of the observer.

The same basic principle can occur with a moving aircraft and a distant object.

The MQ-9 was moving while its camera was observing the orb.

Therefore, some of the apparent movement seen in the video could have been created by the movement of the MQ-9 itself.

This is one of the most important conventional explanations for the case.

# Bellingcat's Parallax Analysis

Bellingcat geolocated the scene and then reconstructed the geometry using satellite imagery.

They identified two ground reference points approximately **750 metres apart**.

These points appeared in different portions of the video approximately **16 seconds apart**.

At first glance, this might make it appear as though the orb itself travelled approximately 750 metres during those 16 seconds.

That would imply a very high speed.

But Bellingcat emphasized that this conclusion would be incorrect.

The 750-metre difference represents the movement of the **observing perspective and scene**, not necessarily the distance travelled by the orb.

The object's apparent movement could therefore be caused substantially by the MQ-9 moving relative to the object.

This is why the video should not be used to claim that the orb demonstrated extraordinary acceleration or speed.
