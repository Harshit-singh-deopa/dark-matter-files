# **The Dyatlov Pass Incident Begins**

In January 1959, a group of students and experienced hikers from the **Ural Polytechnic Institute** in Sverdlovsk organized a demanding winter ski expedition through the northern Ural Mountains.

The expedition was led by **Igor Dyatlov**, a 23-year-old engineering student and experienced skier.

The original group contained ten people.

They planned to travel through difficult mountain terrain toward **Mount Otorten**, completing a route of roughly 250 kilometres.

The trip was classified as a difficult, Category III ski expedition under the standards of the time.

The expedition began on **January 23, 1959**.

At this point, nobody involved could have known that the trip would eventually become one of the most debated mysteries in Russian history.

# **The Journey North**

The group traveled by train and road from Sverdlovsk toward the northern Urals.

They reached the settlement of **Ivdel**, then continued toward the abandoned **Second Northern Mine** settlement.

From there they moved deeper into the mountains.

# **The Avalanche Theory**

The avalanche theory is now one of the most important explanations.

At first glance, an avalanche appears to have a major problem.

For decades, these points were used against the avalanche explanation.

Modern research has changed the discussion.

# **The Delayed Snow-Slab Avalanche Theory**

In 2021, researchers **Johan Gaume and Alexander Puzrin** published a peer-reviewed study in *Communications Earth & Environment*.

Their model proposed a **delayed slab avalanche**.

The idea is different from a huge avalanche sweeping an entire mountainside.

Instead, a relatively compact slab of snow could have become unstable above the tent.

The researchers proposed that the hikers' work to create a flat platform for the tent may have disturbed the snow layers.

Wind could then have transported additional snow onto the slope.

A weak layer underneath could eventually fail.

Hours after the tent was established, the slab could have released and struck the campsite.

The model attempted to explain why the avalanche could have occurred after a delay rather than immediately after the hikers cut into the snow.

# **Why the Avalanche Theory Became More Interesting**

The researchers did not simply say:

"There was an avalanche."

They attempted to reproduce the physical conditions.

Their model considered:

* snow accumulation
* wind loading
* weak snow layers
* slope geometry
* delayed failure
* the location of the tent
* the injuries documented in the autopsies

Their study concluded that a small slab avalanche could plausibly explain the sequence.

In 2022, follow-up expeditions provided additional evidence that avalanches can occur in the region and that some slopes around Dyatlov Pass are capable of producing slab avalanches.

Importantly, the researchers themselves did not claim that their work mathematically proved every event that happened that night.

It demonstrated a **plausible physical mechanism**.

# **How an Avalanche Could Explain the Abandoned Tent**

# **The 2019–2020 Russian Reinvestigation**

Decades after the tragedy, pressure from relatives, researchers and the public contributed to a renewed official investigation.

The Russian prosecutor's office conducted a new examination of the case.

* avalanche
* snow movement
* hurricane or extreme wind conditions

The investigators reconstructed the campsite and examined the terrain.

In **July 2020**, prosecutor **Andrey Kuryakov** announced that the investigation had concluded that an **avalanche was the most likely explanation**.

This was a major development because it represented an official modern reassessment of the case.

# **The 2021 Scientific Study**

The peer-reviewed 2021 study by Johan Gaume and Alexander Puzrin gave the avalanche explanation additional scientific attention.

The authors created a physical and numerical model for a delayed snow-slab release.

Their work addressed several long-standing objections to the avalanche hypothesis.

One major objection was the relatively shallow slope.

Their model showed that under certain conditions, slab avalanches can occur on terrain that is not as steep as people often assume.

Another objection was the delay between the tent being constructed and the proposed avalanche.

Their model showed how wind-driven snow loading could potentially produce delayed failure.

The study was published in *Communications Earth & Environment* and is openly accessible.

# **The 2022 Follow-Up Research**

The researchers continued investigating the location.

A 2022 follow-up publication reported evidence from additional expeditions.

The researchers observed slab avalanches in the region and concluded that avalanche activity is not exceptional around Dyatlov Pass.

They also reported that snow traces from a small slab avalanche can disappear relatively quickly when covered by wind-transported snow.

This is relevant because the original search occurred weeks after the deaths.

An avalanche that occurred earlier might therefore leave less obvious surface evidence by the time searchers arrived.

For decades, the strange circumstances encouraged theories involving murder, military experiments, radiation, secret weapons, UFOs and unknown creatures.

But the available evidence does not require any of those explanations.

The strongest modern explanation is a **delayed snow-slab avalanche followed by a desperate evacuation, severe exposure, injuries and hypothermia**.

The 2020 Russian prosecutorial investigation reached an avalanche-based conclusion, while the 2021 scientific study provided a physical model showing how such a delayed slab could plausibly have occurred. Follow-up research in 2022 also documented avalanche activity in the region.

Nevertheless, nobody witnessed the crucial moments.

For that reason, the most responsible conclusion is not:

**"The Dyatlov mystery has been completely solved."**

It is:

**"The avalanche explanation is currently the strongest evidence-based reconstruction, but some details of the final hours remain uncertain."**

That is what the surviving evidence can actually support.
