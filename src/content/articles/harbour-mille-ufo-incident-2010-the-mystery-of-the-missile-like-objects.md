# **The Harbour Mille Incident at a Glance**

On the evening of **January 25, 2010**, residents of the small Newfoundland and Labrador community of **Harbour Mille** reported seeing unusual objects moving through the sky over or near the Atlantic Ocean.

The objects were described by witnesses as large, elongated, grey or silver shapes resembling **oversized bullets or missiles**. Some witnesses believed they appeared to rise from the ocean. At least one resident, **Darlene Stewart**, photographed one of the objects while she was outside taking pictures of the sunset.

What made the incident particularly unusual was what happened afterward.

The reports quickly developed into a possible missile story. Police investigated. Canadian defence officials became involved. Questions were raised about whether a foreign military might have launched a missile nearby. **Saint Pierre and Miquelon**, the French territory located south of Newfoundland, became part of the speculation.

But the evidence did not ultimately support a missile launch.

The Canadian Prime Minister's Office said there was no indication that a rocket had been launched. French authorities denied that their military activity explained the event. NORAD reportedly had no corresponding rocket launch. Later-released Department of National Defence material showed that military analysts had examined the photograph and rejected ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and licensed model rockets as explanations for the photographed object.

A different explanation eventually emerged: **an ordinary aircraft and its vapour trail illuminated by the setting winter sun**.

That explanation fits several characteristics of the photograph, and a local astronomy representative supported it. However, some witnesses strongly rejected the aircraft explanation because they believed they had seen something different and reported no engine noise.

So what actually happened?

The most defensible answer is more complicated than either "alien UFO" or "missile."

The event was real in the sense that **multiple people genuinely reported seeing something unusual and at least one photograph was taken**. But there is no reliable evidence that an extraterrestrial craft, secret weapon or missile was responsible.

# **Where Is Harbour Mille?**

Harbour Mille is a small coastal community on the southern coast of Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada.

The community sits on the Newfoundland coast facing the Atlantic. Its relatively isolated setting and low population meant that unusual activity in the sky could attract considerable attention.

The location was also important to the story because of its proximity to **Saint Pierre and Miquelon**, the French overseas collectivity located off Newfoundland's southern coast.

That geographical relationship later contributed to speculation that the objects might have been connected with French military activity.

# **What Happened on January 25, 2010?**

The incident began around **5 p.m. on Monday, January 25, 2010**, according to contemporary accounts.

Darlene Stewart was outside photographing the sunset.

While taking pictures, she noticed something unusual moving through the sky.

At first, she apparently could not determine exactly what she was looking at. She photographed the object, and later examined the image more closely.

According to her account reported by CBC, the object appeared unlike an ordinary aircraft.

The sighting became more interesting when Stewart contacted her neighbour, **Emmy Pardy**.

Pardy obtained binoculars and watched the object.

Her description was dramatic: she compared it to a **large silver-grey bullet**, with what appeared to be flames and a smoke trail.

Pardy also believed that the object appeared to come from the direction of the water.

She later told CBC that it appeared to be in the middle of the bay and that she wondered whether something might be being launched there.

# **The Three Objects**

One of the most important details of the case is that this was not necessarily a single-object sighting.

Stewart, Pardy and Stewart's husband reportedly said they saw **three similar objects**, appearing minutes apart.

One was apparently closer, while two others were farther away.

This detail became important because it complicated attempts to explain the sighting as a single unusual object or isolated photographic artifact.

Some later accounts describe three or even four objects, but the most cautious way to describe the evidence is that **multiple witnesses reported seeing several similar objects**.

The exact number should not be treated as independently verified.

# **The First Missile Theory**

Once photographs and witness reports began circulating, the most obvious interpretation was that the objects were missiles.

That immediately created a serious question:

**Who had launched them?**

Harbour Mille is in an area where a mysterious missile launch would obviously attract attention.

The possibility of a military test therefore became one of the major explanations discussed in the days following the sighting.

Some local residents reportedly believed they had heard that the objects could have been test missiles launched from the nearby French territory of **Saint Pierre and Miquelon**.

But this turned out to be a major complication.

French authorities denied that the activity they had conducted corresponded to what had been seen from Newfoundland. Contemporary reporting stated that France had carried out a submarine missile launch around that period, but that its **timing and direction did not match the Harbour Mille reports**.

# **The RCMP Investigation**

The **Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP)** became involved soon after the reports were made.

An RCMP officer travelled to Harbour Mille to investigate the sightings.

The investigation initially created even more uncertainty.

RCMP Sergeant **Wayne Edgecombe** confirmed that investigators had determined that something had been seen, but he initially declined to publicly disclose what information the Department of National Defence had provided.

He also said the police investigation was concerned with whether anything criminal had occurred.

According to his comments preserved in contemporary reports, the matter was not considered a criminal incident.

The RCMP subsequently referred questions to federal authorities.

This back-and-forth between police, Public Safety and Defence officials helped fuel public curiosity.

# **Why Did People Suspect France?**

The French connection came largely from geography.

Saint Pierre and Miquelon is located relatively close to Newfoundland.

Because witnesses believed they had seen what looked like missile launches over the ocean, some people wondered whether the objects could have been connected to French military testing.

There was also a genuine French submarine missile test around the same period, which made the theory seem more plausible to some observers.

But the timing and direction reportedly did not correspond to the Harbour Mille sighting.

French authorities therefore rejected the idea that the reported Newfoundland objects were the result of that test.

This distinction is important:

**France did conduct missile testing around that period, but that does not mean the French military launched the objects seen from Harbour Mille.**

# **The Canadian Government's Response**

The Canadian federal government eventually gave a much more direct response.

On January 28, 2010, Prime Minister Stephen Harper's spokesman **Dimitri Soudas** said there was no indication that a rocket had been launched near Newfoundland's southern coast.

The government therefore rejected the idea that the Harbour Mille sightings were evidence of a known Canadian or foreign rocket launch.

The government also stated that there was no security threat associated with the incident.

That statement did not solve the mystery, however.

It simply ruled out one of the most dramatic explanations.

# **What About NORAD?**

Another important part of the investigation involved **NORAD — the North American Aerospace Defense Command**.

NORAD is responsible for aerospace warning and defence for North America.

Later reporting based on Department of National Defence documents stated that **NORAD and Canada Command had no information corresponding to the reported rockets**.

This does not mean that NORAD proved there was no object.

It means that there was apparently **no known rocket launch in the relevant records that matched the sighting**.

That distinction matters.

A lack of a corresponding military launch record does not automatically turn an unidentified object into an extraterrestrial craft.

# **The Department of National Defence Investigation**

This is arguably the most important part of the entire case.

Months later, CBC obtained Department of National Defence documents concerning the incident.

The documents showed that military specialists had actually examined the photograph.

The analysis came from the **Directorate of Scientific and Technical Intelligence (DSTI)**.

The conclusion was surprisingly specific.

According to the released material, analysts determined that the photographed object was:

* Not a ballistic missile
* Not a cruise missile in its boost phase
* Not a cruise missile in its flight phase
* Not a licensed model rocket launcher

The analysts also pointed out that the photograph contained no distinctive features that could conclusively identify the object.

This is one of the strongest genuine pieces of evidence in the case.

But there is an important misconception to avoid.

The Defence analysis **did not prove that the object was extraterrestrial**.

It simply found that the photograph did not fit several specific missile and rocket categories.

The apparent trail also appeared longer than the body of the object.

# **The Aircraft-and-Contrail Explanation**

A much more ordinary explanation appeared shortly afterward.

Finnish UFO researcher and author **Björn Borg** examined the Harbour Mille photographs.

He proposed that the witnesses had actually seen a distant aircraft and its vapour trail illuminated by the setting winter sun.

According to this theory, the aircraft itself could become difficult to see while its high-altitude condensation trail remained brightly illuminated.

From the correct angle, the trail could appear to originate from a bright point and resemble a rocket plume.

The effect could be especially striking around sunset.

Borg called this a recurring winter phenomenon and said similar reports had occurred in other parts of the world.

# **Support for the Contrail Theory**

The contrail explanation received support from **Chris Stevenson**, identified in contemporary reporting as the local president of the Royal Astronomical Society.

Stevenson said he had seen similar effects produced by aircraft.

His explanation was that transatlantic aircraft flying high above the ground could remain illuminated by sunlight even after sunset had occurred for observers below.

The resulting vapour trail could become strongly coloured and visually dramatic.

He also argued that the appearance of the photographed trail was inconsistent with a conventional rocket plume because the trail seemed to become more transparent rather than remaining a dense exhaust plume all the way back to the launch point.

This is probably the most important conventional explanation for the case.

# **Why Didn't Witnesses Accept the Aircraft Explanation?**

Her reasoning was understandable: if an aircraft was responsible, why hadn't anyone heard it?

But there is a potential problem with that argument.

A distant high-altitude aircraft can be difficult or impossible to hear from the ground, particularly in windy or noisy environmental conditions.

Therefore, silence alone cannot prove that the object was not an aircraft.

At the same time, the witnesses' direct observations cannot simply be dismissed because a conventional explanation exists.

Their testimony remains part of the historical record.

# **The Sunset Was Extremely Important**

The timing of the event may be one of the biggest clues.

At higher altitude, however, an aircraft could still be illuminated by sunlight.

It can also make the trail look like a fiery exhaust plume.

# **The "It Came Out of the Ocean" Question**

One of the most interesting aspects of the Harbour Mille case is the repeated claim that the objects appeared to emerge from the ocean.

Emmy Pardy described one object as appearing to come from the ocean.

However, a photograph taken from a single viewpoint cannot establish that an object physically originated from the water.

A distant aircraft travelling toward or away from the observer can create an optical illusion in which its apparent path seems to begin at the horizon.

The same problem applies to the apparent vertical trajectory.

Without triangulation from multiple observation points, determining the object's actual three-dimensional movement is extremely difficult.

Therefore:

**"It appeared to come from the ocean" is a genuine witness statement, but it is not proof that the object physically emerged from the water.**
